# Phase 1: Concept, "2:13 AM"

## The idea in one sentence
One night in the support queue of a platform that offers custom domains, told entirely on the support lead's own screens. It ends with the same screen a month later, after the platform shipped CustomDomain™.

## Who it is for, and what they should feel
- **Viewer:** support and operations leads at a platform with custom domains, and the founders who read their weekly ticket review.
- **What they should feel:** recognition first. "My domain isn't working" at 2 a.m. is their week. Then relief.
- **What they should know:**
  - The domain tickets are not about the platform's product. They are about DNS at someone else's website.
  - CustomDomain™ removes the step those tickets come from: the customer signs in to their provider inside the app and approves.
- **What they should do:** forward this to whoever owns the custom-domain feature.

## How it differs from the films before it
- **The earlier films** explain or demonstrate. This one tells a story with a character, a time of night and an ending.
- **Its grammar is screen-life:** everything happens on a screen, in real time, through notifications, a ticket queue, a reply box and a team chat. There is no narrator outside the character. The voice is the support lead's own quiet inner monologue.
- **Its look carries the arc:**
  - the night is the screen in dark mode, in the brand's ink and stone
  - after the turn, the same screen is in light mode, in paper
  - green appears once: a "live" notification
  - the brand marks errors by inverting, never with red, so the night's errors are inverted labels

## Formats
- **16:9:** a laptop desktop, with a menu bar clock, a helpdesk window, a chat window and notification toasts.
- **9:16:** a phone, with a lock screen at 2:13, stacked notifications, the helpdesk app and the chat app. This is re-blocked for the phone, not cropped.

## Accuracy guardrails
- **The failure in Maya's ticket is real and documented:** the full hostname typed in the Name field (content/troubleshooting.mdx:32-44).
- **"The cost is not the DNS write, it is the support queue behind it."** That is the docs' own framing (guides/choosing-a-custom-domain-solution.mdx:33-38). The docs publish no ticket statistics, so the film shows none about CustomDomain™. The queue numbers belong to the fictional platform's own night.
- **The resolution is shown with Maya's provider,** which supports sign-in, so "sign in to her DNS provider, inside our app, and approve" is literally true for her.
- **"Kept that way"** means the hourly checks. The film claims no alerts or automatic repair.
- **The platform and every person are fictional:** "yourplatform", Maya and Sam. No real company's interface is imitated: the helpdesk and the chat are generic.
