Your product gives each customer a page, a store, a help center or an app, and
they want it on a domain they already own. CustomDomain™ adds that step to your
product: the customer types their domain, and the records are written for them
where their DNS provider allows it, or shown with exact steps and checked
automatically where it does not.

## When this fits

- Your customers own the domains, and they should connect them **inside your
  product**, in your branding.
- You do not want to support DNS by hand: which record, which provider panel,
  why it has not gone live yet.
- You need the certificate issued and renewed for every customer domain, or you
  already terminate TLS yourself and only need the DNS part.

If the domain is yours rather than your customers', see
[Your own domain](/docs/use-cases/your-own-domain). If your server decides which
domains get connected and there is no customer in the loop, the
[API path](/docs/getting-started/api) fits better than the widget.

## How CustomDomain™ fits in

<Steps>

<Step>

### Your server mints a widget token

Your backend holds the credential and trades it for a short-lived widget token
(15 minutes, one application, optionally one domain and one end user). For an
application made in the console that is an API key sent to
`POST https://app.customdomain.ai/api/widget-token`; for one made over the API it
is the application's `client_secret` sent to `POST /v1/tokens`. Only the token
reaches the browser. See [Widget tokens](/docs/authentication/widget-tokens).

</Step>

<Step>

### The widget connects the domain

`customdomain-js` (or `@customdomain/react`, or the hosted script) opens the
connect modal. It detects the customer's DNS provider and offers the way in it
can actually complete, from the six [ways to connect](/docs/concepts/glossary#ways-to-connect):
a sign-in with the provider, the provider's one-click setup, a secure sign-in, a
scoped API key, the records to add by hand with the provider's own steps and a live
check of each record, or, where you turn it on, DNS hosting. See
[Widget](/docs/getting-started/widget).

</Step>

<Step>

### The domain is verified and served

The connection goes `live` once every record resolves. Through the CustomDomain™
edge, the certificate is issued on the first request and renewed on its own, and
each request is proxied to your origin with the customer's hostname in
`X-Forwarded-Host`; edge serving is part of the Growth plan and up. Or point
customer domains at your own infrastructure, on every plan, and serve TLS
yourself. See [Where domains point](/docs/connect-flow/routing).

</Step>

<Step>

### Your backend hears about it

`connection.live` and `connection.failed` [webhooks](/docs/webhooks/overview)
carry your own id for the customer as `user_id`, and the reason when there is
one. `GET /v1/connections/{id}` is the fallback.

</Step>

</Steps>

Build it on [test mode](/docs/guides/test-mode) first: a test mode application
runs the widget, the API and your webhooks on made up `.test` names, with no card
and nothing billed.

<Callout title="What one click covers">
  Of the 189 providers in the census on 2026-10-21, 73 have an automatic path
  (6 provider OAuth, 1 Domain Connect, 66 scoped API key). Customers at the other
  116 add the records by hand, with the exact rows shown and verified
  automatically. The live split is at `GET /v1/providers/census`; see
  [DNS providers](/docs/dns/providers).
</Callout>
