---
title: Your own domain
description: "Point a domain you own at something you run, with no code: connect it in the console, add or authorize the DNS records, and get HTTPS issued and renewed."
---

You have a domain and something to put on it: an app, a site hosted elsewhere, a
redirect, or email. The console connects it without code. It finds where the
domain's DNS is hosted, writes the records for you where that provider allows
it, and otherwise shows the exact rows to add and checks them for you.

## When this fits

- The domain is yours, and so is what it should point at.
- You would rather not work out which record goes where at your DNS provider.
- You want HTTPS on it without managing certificates.

If your customers are the ones connecting domains, see
[SaaS platforms](/docs/use-cases/saas-platforms).

## How CustomDomain™ fits in

<Steps>

<Step>

### Sign up and connect the domain

[Sign up](https://app.customdomain.ai/signup); the welcome screen asks who the
domains are for. Then enter the domain, a subdomain such as `www.example.com` or the root `example.com`. Both
work at every DNS provider; the record for a root depends on the provider (see
[Root domains](/docs/dns/root-domains)).

</Step>

<Step>

### Add the records, or let them be written

The console offers what your provider supports, with the recommended way
marked: sign in with the provider, the provider's one-click setup, a scoped API
key, or the records to add by hand. Records written for you usually verify within seconds; records added
by hand are verified as soon as the provider serves them. A domain waiting for
its records has no deadline.

</Step>

<Step>

### Point it at what you run

CustomDomain™ does not host websites. Through its edge it serves your domain from
a site or app hosted elsewhere, with the certificate issued and renewed for you,
which needs the Growth plan. Connecting the domain does not. You can also point
it straight at your own servers on any plan, and serve TLS yourself. See
[Where domains point](/docs/connect-flow/routing).

</Step>

</Steps>

The same domain can carry more than a site:

- **Email.** MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC, with SPF merged into the record you
  already have rather than replacing it. See [Email DNS](/docs/dns/email-dns).
- **A redirect.** A permanent 301 to another URL, issued at the edge, keeping
  the path and query. See [Domain forwarding](/docs/connect-flow/forwarding).
- **No domain yet?** Search for one and buy it in the console. See
  [Buying a domain](/docs/sell/buying-a-domain).
