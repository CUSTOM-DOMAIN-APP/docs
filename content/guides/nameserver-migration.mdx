**Domain maintenance** lets your platform keep your users' domains working for
them. Each user is asked once, in the widget, when they connect a domain. If they
say yes, one setting at the company they bought the domain from changes, and from
then on CustomDomain™ (or your own DNS account) runs the domain's DNS: every
record your platform needs is written and kept correct, the certificate renews,
and problems are fixed without asking the user again. The domain stays registered
where it is, and its website and email keep working.

The technical name is **nameserver migration**, because the one setting that
changes is the domain's nameservers. The API, the webhooks and the MCP tools use
that name (`nameserver_migration`, `nameservers.*`), and so does this page where
it describes them.

This page is for the engineer who turns it on, and for anyone who answers a
user's questions about it ([What changes for your users](#end-user-faq) is at the
end).

## DNS, the registrar and nameservers [#what-nameservers-are]

Three words come up everywhere here. In plain terms:

- **DNS** is the internet's address book. It tells browsers and email where a
  domain's website and mail live. A domain's DNS settings are called its
  *records*.
- **The registrar** is the company a domain was bought from and is renewed with,
  like GoDaddy or Namecheap. It is often not the company that runs the domain's
  DNS: a domain bought at GoDaddy can have its DNS at Cloudflare.
- **Nameservers** are one setting at the registrar. They say who runs the
  domain's DNS. Changing them is the only change a move makes.

So a domain has two parts: where it is registered (paid for), which a move never
touches, and what keeps it working (its DNS), which a move hands to you.

Behind those two parts are three parties:

| Party | What it does | Changes in a move? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registrar | Where the domain is registered and renewed (GoDaddy, Namecheap and so on). It tells the registry which nameservers answer for the domain. | No |
| Nameservers | The servers the registry lists for the domain. Every DNS question about the domain goes to them. | Yes |
| DNS host | Whoever runs those nameservers and keeps the domain's records (`A`, `MX`, `TXT` and the rest). | Yes |

Moving the nameservers changes who answers for the domain: the registry stops
listing the old DNS host's nameservers and lists the new ones. The registrar, the
registration, the renewal date and the owner stay exactly as they were.

What the user gets:

- **One change, made once.** After it, every record your platform needs is
  written for them, and records that drift are put back.
- **Their website and email keep working.** The domain's records are copied to
  the new DNS host before the nameservers move, and the copy counts the mail
  records (`MX`, SPF, DKIM, DMARC) so the screen can say "email included".
- **They can switch back.** We never touch the records at their previous DNS
  host. Some hosts delete a domain's records a few days after its nameservers
  move away (Cloudflare after 7 days), so a move back works as before only while
  the previous host still has them. See [What moving back does](#moving-back).

## Who runs it: CustomDomain™ or your own account [#the-two-routes]

Each application picks who runs the DNS of the domains it maintains. The console
calls this **Who runs it**; the API calls it the `target`.

| Who runs it | `target` | Where the domain's DNS lives | Choose it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CustomDomain™** (recommended, the default) | `{"kind": "managed"}` | CustomDomain™'s [managed DNS](/docs/dns/managed-dns) | You want records kept correct and monitored, changes that take effect at once and automatic certificates, with nothing to run. |
| **Your own account** | `{"kind": "dns_host", "account_id": "pacct_…"}` | Your own account at Cloudflare, Amazon Route 53, Google Cloud DNS or Azure DNS | You keep DNS in your own infrastructure and want us to add each user's domain to it, copy the records and make the change. |
| **Your own nameservers** (under **Advanced**) | `{"kind": "custom", "nameservers": ["ns1.you.net", "ns2.you.net"]}` | Your own nameservers | You run DNS yourself, on your own servers or a host we do not connect to. You set up each domain; we make the change. |

**CustomDomain™** uses [managed DNS](/docs/dns/managed-dns), which is rolling out
gradually, so the domain check says per domain whether it can be offered
([below](#turn-it-on)).

### What your own account needs [#a-dns-host-we-connect-to]

Connect the account first as a
[provider account](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts) of the workspace with
continuous access, using a key that may create zones (a *zone* is one domain's
set of records at a DNS host):

| DNS host | What the key needs | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloudflare | A token that may create zones | A token that reaches several Cloudflare accounts also needs the account named. Each zone gets its own nameserver pair, and records are written DNS only (not proxied). |
| Amazon Route 53 | An IAM access key that may create hosted zones | A hosted zone of that name already in the account is used. The Route 53 access role is scoped to one zone and cannot create zones, so it does not fit here. |
| Google Cloud DNS | A project ID and an access token | An access token expires after about an hour, so a move started later cannot create its zone until the account is connected again. |
| Azure DNS | A subscription, a resource group and an access token | The same hour long token. A zone is never overwritten: one that already exists is used. |

In the console, **Your own account** shows a tile for each of these hosts. A host
you have connected can be picked (with a choice of account when there are
several); one you have not is a link to connect it, and one connected with a key
that cannot create zones says so.

Each zone is found or created, never made twice: a retry uses the zone it already
made, and a zone that already existed in your account keeps what it holds. Only
the connection's own records are written into it, and nothing is deleted.

### Your own nameservers

In the console, open **Advanced** under **Your own account**, tick **Use your own
nameservers instead**, and list them one per line. Give 2 to 13 nameserver host
names. The zone must answer on them before the nameservers move: a move to your
nameservers waits in `pending` until you say the zone is ready
(`zone_ready: true`), then checks that your nameservers answer for the domain
(`skip_zone_check: true` skips that check).

### Per application

The setting, the DNS host account, the console's figures and the webhooks belong
to one application. A move only ever uses its own application's DNS host
account (a call that names another application's account is refused with
`422 dns_host_not_configured`), `GET /v1/applications/{id}/nameserver-migrations`
lists one application's moves, and the `nameservers.*` webhooks go to the move's
own application only.

## Turn it on

In the console: **Applications**, the application, **Settings**, then **Domain
maintenance** (a [test mode](/docs/guides/test-mode) application has none). Turn
on **Maintain your users' domains**, choose **Who runs it**, and **Save**. Nothing
changes for your users until you save. Once saved, each user is asked when they
connect a domain. Turned off again, new users are not asked, and domains already
maintained stay that way.

With the API, `PATCH /v1/applications/{id}` with `nameserver_migration`. The API
is the same as before the console's new words: its switches map to `mode`, and
**Who runs it** to `target`.

| In the console | `mode` | What the widget does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maintain your users' domains** off (the default) | `off` | Never offers it. The application carries no setting. |
| On, **Show it as the first choice** off | `offer` | Shows it as a quiet second choice beside adding records. |
| On, **Show it as the first choice** on | `prefer` | Makes it the first screen of the manual path. Adding records stays one tap away. |

| Who runs it | `target` |
| --- | --- |
| **CustomDomain™** (recommended, the default) | `{"kind": "managed"}` |
| **Your own account**, then the account | `{"kind": "dns_host", "account_id": "pacct_…"}` |
| **Advanced**, **Use your own nameservers instead** | `{"kind": "custom", "nameservers": […]}` |

```bash
curl -X PATCH https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/applications/<APP_ID> \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <API_KEY>" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "nameserver_migration": { "mode": "prefer", "target": { "kind": "managed" } } }'
```

Send `{"nameserver_migration": {"mode": "off"}}` to turn it off. Once it is on,
`POST /v1/domains:check` carries `nameserver_migration` for the domain (`mode`,
`target`, `available`, `path`, `provider`, and `reason` when `available` is
`false`), and only then.

## What your users see [#what-your-end-user-sees]

With **Show it as the first choice** off, the widget's manual setup shows it as a
quiet card beside adding records: "Let Acme Sites keep your domain working", "One
change where you bought it. Your website and email keep working." With it on, the
manual path opens on the consent screen, and **Add records instead** is one tap
away.

The widget asks once, in plain words. The title names your platform and the
domain ("Let Acme Sites keep acme.com working?"), and under it are "Your domain
stays where you bought it. Only one setting there changes.", the nameservers now
and after, "Your website and email keep working. Everything is copied first.",
"You can switch back any time." and one button, **Yes, set it up**. It is white
label: your platform's name, never ours. The id and version of that consent text
(`nsm-consent`, version `2`) are stored with the move and the time; the person's
address is not.

Then the same screen shows its progress (copy settings, security check, make the
change, live) and the next thing to do: the nameservers to type at the registrar,
a button to sign in to the registrar with
[secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in), or that we are waiting on the
registry. **Move back** is there while it can be. A finished move hands over to the
usual success screen. Every string is in the widget's 13 languages.

## What each status means [#the-steps]

A domain's page in the console shows three steps: **Copying settings**
(`pending`, `copying`), **Waiting for the registrar** (`needs_dnssec_off` through
`propagating`) and **Live** (`active`). **Domains you maintain** and the domain's
badge give its status in plain words, the first column here:

| Console says | State | What happens | Waits on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Setting up | `pending` | Your own nameservers only: wait for `zone_ready`, then check your nameservers answer for the zone. | you |
| Setting up | `copying` | Create the zone (managed DNS, or your DNS host account), copy the domain's records into it with the connection's own, count the mail records. Retried every 2 minutes for 6 hours. | us |
| Waiting for the user | `needs_dnssec_off` | The registry publishes a `DS` record and we cannot remove it ourselves. | the person |
| Waiting for the registrar | `waiting_ds` | DNSSEC is off. Wait until the registry stops publishing the `DS` record and its TTL has passed (5 minutes to 48 hours; 1 hour when unknown). | the registry |
| Waiting for the user (paths `manual` and `agent`), or Waiting for the registrar (path `api`) | `setting` | The nameservers are being changed (`waiting_for` says by whom). 14 days at most. | the registrar or the person |
| Almost live | `propagating` | Changed. The delegation is read every 5 minutes for 2 hours, then hourly up to 48 hours. | the registry |
| Maintained | `active` | The registry lists every new nameserver. Read again every 6 hours: a move away is `failed` with `delegation_drift`, and back again is `active`. | |
| Stopped | `failed` | `failure_code` says why. A delegation failure is still read twice a day for 14 days, and turns `active` if the delegation becomes the new one. | |
| Moved back | `rolled_back` | Moved back. No longer maintained. | |

Why a move stopped, as `failure_code` and as the console says it:

| `failure_code` | Console says |
| --- | --- |
| `zone_copy_failed` | Copying the zone failed |
| `zone_not_served` | The new nameservers did not answer |
| `dnssec_timeout` (after 7 days) | DNSSEC stayed on |
| `ds_removal_failed` | DNSSEC could not be turned off |
| `credential_refused` | The key was refused |
| `registrar_refused` | The registrar refused |
| `registrar_locked` | The domain is locked |
| `agent_failed` | The sign in did not finish |
| `delegation_timeout` | The registry never showed it |
| `delegation_drift` | Moved away after |
| `internal` | Something went wrong on our side |

A zone has one open move at a time, so two connections under one registrable
domain (`acme.com` and `shop.acme.com`) share it, and a move of the same zone for
another account is refused with `409`.

## How the nameservers change

No standard lets a third party ask a registrar to change a domain's nameservers.
Domain Connect changes records only, and CDS and CSYNC (RFC 7344, RFC 7477) need
the current DNS host to publish them and the domain to be signed with DNSSEC,
which most domains are not. So each move takes one of three paths:

| `path` | How | Credential |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `api` | The registrar's own API. | A key the customer created: a connected provider account, or a key sent with the call and used once, never stored. A connection linked to a connected account uses it, also for an end user's move. |
| `agent` | [Secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in): the end user signs in to their registrar and approves a plan whose only step is the exact new nameservers (or turning DNSSEC off). Only those controls are opened; DNSSEC stays closed unless the plan turns it off. A code the registrar asks for is handed back to the person. | None: the person's own sign-in. |
| `manual` | The person sets them at the registrar with the values shown. | None |

What each registrar's API can do:

| Registrar | Read | Change | Remove a `DS` record | Most nameservers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GoDaddy | yes | yes | no | 13 |
| Namecheap | yes | yes | no | 12 |
| Name.com | yes | yes | yes | 13 |
| Porkbun | yes | yes | yes | 13 |
| Hostinger | yes | yes | no | 4 |
| Spaceship | yes | yes | no | 12 |
| IONOS | yes | no (secure sign-in or by hand) | no | |

Secure sign-in can change the nameservers at Namecheap, GoDaddy and Name.com, where
it is offered. GoDaddy permits its API to a customer for their own domains, so
CustomDomain™ changes GoDaddy nameservers only with a key the customer created and
gave us, never with one we made or read off a page.

## Why DNSSEC comes first [#dnssec]

DNSSEC is a security lock some domains have on their DNS: the registry publishes
a `DS` record, and resolvers use it to check that answers come from the domain's
DNS host. When the registry publishes one, the nameservers must not move until it
is gone and resolvers have dropped it, or validating resolvers would stop
answering for the domain. With a connected account that keeps access (path `api`)
at a registrar whose API removes `DS` records (Name.com and Porkbun), we remove it
and wait (`waiting_ds`). Otherwise the move stops at `needs_dnssec_off`, the
workspace's owners and admins get a "needs attention"
[notification](/docs/guides/notifications), and the secure sign-in path offers a
session whose only step is turning DNSSEC off. Some registrars turn DNSSEC on by default
(Squarespace and Spaceship among them), so expect it.

The new DNS host does not sign the zone. Turning DNSSEC on again there is the DNS
host owner's choice after the move.

## What moving back does [#moving-back]

`POST /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers:rollback` sets the nameservers the domain
had when the move started (`old_nameservers`). With a key (sent once, or the
move's connected account) the registrar does it at once (`rollback_path: api`);
otherwise the answer names the old nameservers for the person (`manual`, in
`next_step`). A move that changed nothing at the registrar yet is just stopped
(`none`). It is idempotent. In the console, **Move back** on the domain's DNS tab
asks first ("Move acme.com back?").

We never touch the records at the previous DNS host, so the domain answers as
before once the registry lists the old nameservers again, as long as that host
still has them. Some hosts delete a domain's records a few days after its
nameservers move away: Cloudflare deletes such a zone after 7 days. After that, a
move back points the domain at a host with no records for it, and they must be
added there again, so move back soon, or plan for it. Secure sign-in does not move
nameservers back yet.

## API

| Route | Who may call it |
| --- | --- |
| `PATCH /v1/applications/{id}` with `nameserver_migration` | An API key that can write applications. |
| `POST /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers:migrate` | An API key; a widget token (the end user: the application's mode must be `offer` or `prefer`, `consent` is required, and `target`, a key and the zone flags are refused); an agent token with `domains:connect`. |
| `GET /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers` | An API key, a widget token, or an agent token with `domains:read`. |
| `POST /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers:rollback` | An API key; a widget token (without a key); an agent token with `domains:connect`. |
| `GET /v1/applications/{id}/nameserver-migrations?limit=&since=` | An API key: one application's moves and their `stats`. |
| `POST /v1/connections/{id}/browser:start` with `"purpose": "nameservers"` | The widget or the console: the secure sign-in session of the `agent` path, once the move waits for it (`waiting_for: browser_session`). |

Start a move from your server through the customer's connected registrar
account:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/connections/<ID>/nameservers:migrate \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <API_KEY>" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "path": "api", "provider_account_id": "pacct_…" }'
```

To use a key once instead, send `provider` (the registrar's id, such as
`porkbun`) and `credential: {"token": "…"}` in the registrar's key format; it is
used for that call and never stored.

`201` creates the move and `200` resumes the domain's open one with what the call
brings (a key, `zone_ready`). The answer is the move as far as it got in that call:
with a registrar key and no DNSSEC it is often already `propagating`. Poll
`GET /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers` until `state` is `active`, `failed` or
`rolled_back`. `waiting_for` says what it waits on and `next_step` is one sentence
for a person. The shape is illustrative; the
[API reference](/docs/api-reference) has every field under **Nameservers**.

The `422` refusals say why a move cannot start: `nameserver_migration_unavailable`
(`details` says why), `nameserver_api_unsupported` (the registrar has no API call
that changes nameservers), `nameserver_agent_unavailable` (secure sign-in cannot
change this registrar's nameservers), `zone_host_unsupported` or
`dns_host_not_configured`.

## Webhooks

Seven events follow a move. Each carries a `nameservers` object and is written to
the domain's timeline as a row of the same kind. See the
[event catalog](/docs/webhooks/overview#event-catalog).

| Event | When |
| --- | --- |
| `nameservers.started` | A move was created. |
| `nameservers.dnssec_cleared` | The registry stopped publishing the `DS` record. |
| `nameservers.changed` | The registrar took the new nameservers, or the delegation first showed one. |
| `nameservers.active` | The registry lists every new nameserver. |
| `nameservers.failed` | The move stopped (`failure_code`). |
| `nameservers.rolled_back` | Moved back. |
| `nameservers.drifted` | An active move's nameservers were changed away (`observed_nameservers`). It is also an inbox item and the drift email. |

## MCP

The [MCP server](/docs/mcp/overview) has three tools for it:
`check-nameserver-status` (`domains:read`), and `migrate-nameservers` and
`rollback-nameservers` (`domains:connect`, both with `confirm: true`, which an
agent sends only after the person agreed). The tools never send a registrar key,
and a DNS host target always comes from the application's setting.

## In the console

- **Application settings, Domain maintenance**: the switch **Maintain your users'
  domains**, a before and after picture of a user's domain (where it is
  registered stays; what keeps it working moves to CustomDomain™ or your host),
  **Who runs it** (CustomDomain™, or your own Cloudflare, Amazon Route 53, Google
  Cloud DNS or Azure DNS account, with your own nameservers under **Advanced**),
  and **Show it as the first choice**. A **?** beside DNS, registrar and
  nameservers gives one plain line and a link here, and every card links to the
  part of this page that explains it.
- **Domains you maintain**, under it once it is on: how many domains are
  maintained, being set up and need attention, the typical setup time, why moves
  stopped, and one row per domain with who runs it, its status in plain words, the
  records copied and when it was last checked. Each row opens the domain.
- **A domain's DNS tab**: a **Domain maintenance** section with its status, the
  three steps, the next thing to do, the domain's two parts, where its records are
  (listed below for CustomDomain™; a count and a link to your host's console for
  your own account), its nameservers under **Technical details**, and **Move
  back**. A domain your own DNS host runs is not offered CustomDomain™'s managed DNS
  there.
- **Webhooks**: a **Nameserver moves** group in the event picker. The domain's
  timeline has a sentence for each event.

A move is not billed on its own: CustomDomain™ bills connected domains, and a
zone it hosts counts toward the [managed DNS](/docs/dns/managed-dns#limits) zone
limit of the account.

## What changes for your users [#end-user-faq]

In the words you can give them.

**What am I agreeing to?**
That the platform keeps your domain working for you. One setting at the company
you bought your domain from changes, so the platform can keep your domain's
settings correct, renew its certificate and fix problems without asking you
again.

**Will my website or email stop working?**
No. Everything is copied first, email included, before anything changes.

**Does my domain move to another company?**
No. It stays registered where you bought it, with the same renewal date and the
same owner. You keep paying for it there.

**What do I have to do?**
Say yes. Then, unless the change can be made for you, either paste the values
shown at the company you bought your domain from, or sign in there and approve
the change. The screen tells you which.

**How long does it take?**
Usually minutes. It can take up to 48 hours to show everywhere.

**Why do I have to turn off DNSSEC?**
DNSSEC is a security lock on the old settings. It must be off before they move,
or the domain would stop working during the move. You can turn it on again
afterwards.

**Can I switch back?**
Yes, any time. Your previous provider keeps your records for a while, but some
delete them a few days after the domain moves away (Cloudflare after 7), so
switch back soon or expect to add them again.

**Who can change my records afterwards?**
The platform, through the DNS host it uses. You can still ask it to change or
show them.
