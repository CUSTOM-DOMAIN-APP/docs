**Domain maintenance** lets your platform keep your users' domains working for
them. Each user is asked once, in the widget, when they connect a domain. If they
say yes, one setting at the company they bought the domain from changes, and from
then on CustomDomain™ (or your own DNS account) runs the domain's DNS: every
record your platform needs is written and kept correct, the certificate renews,
and problems are fixed without asking the user again. The domain stays registered
where it is, and its website and email keep working.

The technical name is **nameserver migration**, because the one setting that
changes is the domain's nameservers. The API, the webhooks and the MCP tools use
that name (`nameserver_migration`, `nameservers.*`), and so does this page where
it describes them.

This page is for the engineer who turns it on, and for anyone who answers a
user's questions about it ([What changes for your users](#end-user-faq) is at the
end).

## DNS, the registrar and nameservers [#what-nameservers-are]

Three words come up everywhere here. In plain terms:

- **DNS** is the internet's address book. It tells browsers and email where a
  domain's website and mail live. A domain's DNS settings are called its
  *records*.
- **The registrar** is the company a domain was bought from and is renewed with,
  like GoDaddy or Namecheap. It is often not the company that runs the domain's
  DNS: a domain bought at GoDaddy can have its DNS at Cloudflare.
- **Nameservers** are one setting at the registrar. They say who runs the
  domain's DNS. Changing them is the only change a move makes.

So a domain has two parts: where it is registered (paid for), which a move never
touches, and what keeps it working (its DNS), which a move hands to you.

Behind those two parts are three parties:

| Party | What it does | Changes in a move? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registrar | Where the domain is registered and renewed (GoDaddy, Namecheap and so on). It tells the registry which nameservers answer for the domain. | No |
| Nameservers | The servers the registry lists for the domain. Every DNS question about the domain goes to them. | Yes |
| DNS host | Whoever runs those nameservers and keeps the domain's records (`A`, `MX`, `TXT` and the rest). | Yes |

Moving the nameservers changes who answers for the domain: the registry stops
listing the old DNS host's nameservers and lists the new ones. The registrar, the
registration, the renewal date and the owner stay exactly as they were.

What the user gets:

- **One change, made once.** After it, every record your platform needs is
  written for them, and records that drift are put back.
- **Their website and email keep working.** The domain's records are copied to
  the new DNS host before the nameservers move, and the copy counts the mail
  records (`MX`, SPF, DKIM, DMARC) so the screen can say "email included".
  Nothing changes at the registrar until the copy is in place, and a copy we could
  only read from outside waits for a person to check it ([The copy](#the-copy)).
- **They can switch back, two ways.** To the nameservers they had, while their
  previous DNS host still answers for the domain (Cloudflare deletes a moved
  domain's records after 7 days), or to their registrar's own DNS, which is always
  there. See [What moving back does](#moving-back).

## Where the nameservers are changed: at the registrar [#where-the-nameservers-are-changed]

A domain's nameservers are changed at its **registrar**, which is often not the
company that runs its DNS. Registered at GoDaddy with its DNS at Cloudflare is
the most common split, and then the change is made at GoDaddy. So for every move,
CustomDomain™ looks the registrar up:

1. **In public registration data.** A domain's registry publishes who the
   domain is registered with, over RDAP (the protocol behind the public WHOIS
   lookup at lookup.icann.org). The answer names the registrar, its website, its
   IANA Registrar ID and, when the registry shows one, the reseller it was bought
   through.
2. **Matched to a provider we connect to**, by that IANA ID: all of GoDaddy's
   registrar accreditations are GoDaddy, Namecheap's is Namecheap, and so on. A
   registrar we do not connect to, such as a wholesale registrar that sells
   through resellers, is named (by the reseller when the registry shows one), and
   the person makes the change there.
3. **Used for every way of making the change.** A registrar key, a connected
   account, a [secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in) and the manual
   instructions all go to that registrar. An account at the DNS host is never
   used for the change, and a key or account at another company than the
   registrar is refused (`422 registrar_mismatch`). Without an account linked to
   the connection, a connected account of the workspace at the registrar that can
   read the domain's registration is used (for a move your server or the console
   starts).

When the registry answers nothing, the move goes on with the detected DNS host
as a guess and names no company: the next step says to make the change "where
acme.com is registered (the company it was bought from; lookup.icann.org names
it)". A connected account at that DNS host is used then only when it can read the
domain's registration, which shows it is the registrar's. The move carries the registrar as `registrar` (`name`, `provider`,
`iana_id`, `url` and `source`), `null` when it is not known, and the DNS host the
domain left as `old_dns_host`.

**Cloudflare Registrar** requires Cloudflare's own nameservers, so a domain
registered there cannot be moved without transferring it to another registrar
first. The domain check says `available: false` with
`reason: registrar_locks_nameservers`, and a move is refused with
`422 registrar_locks_nameservers` before anything is copied.

## Who runs it: CustomDomain™ or your own account [#the-two-routes]

Each application picks who runs the DNS of the domains it maintains. The console
calls this **Who runs it**; the API calls it the `target`.

| Who runs it | `target` | Where the domain's DNS lives | Choose it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CustomDomain™** (recommended, the default) | `{"kind": "managed"}` | CustomDomain™'s [managed DNS](/docs/dns/managed-dns) | You want records kept correct and monitored, changes that take effect at once and automatic certificates, with nothing to run. |
| **Your own account** | `{"kind": "dns_host", "account_id": "pacct_…"}` | Your own account at Cloudflare, Amazon Route 53, Google Cloud DNS or Azure DNS | You keep DNS in your own infrastructure and want us to add each user's domain to it, copy the records and make the change. |
| **Your own nameservers** (under **Advanced**) | `{"kind": "custom", "nameservers": ["ns1.you.net", "ns2.you.net"]}` | Your own nameservers | You run DNS yourself, on your own servers or a host we do not connect to. You set up each domain; we make the change. |

**CustomDomain™** uses [managed DNS](/docs/dns/managed-dns), which is rolling out
gradually, so the domain check says per domain whether it can be offered
([below](#turn-it-on)).

### What your own account needs [#a-dns-host-we-connect-to]

Connect the account first as a
[provider account](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts) of the workspace with
continuous access and scope `all` (it creates a zone for every customer's domain,
which no list names in advance), using a key that may create zones (a *zone* is
one domain's set of records at a DNS host):

| DNS host | What the key needs | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloudflare | A token that may create zones | A token that reaches several Cloudflare accounts also needs the account named. Each zone gets its own nameserver pair, and records are written DNS only (not proxied). |
| Amazon Route 53 | An IAM access key that may create hosted zones | A hosted zone of that name already in the account is used. The Route 53 access role is scoped to one zone and cannot create zones, so it does not fit here. |
| Google Cloud DNS | A project ID and an access token | An access token expires after about an hour, so a move started later cannot create its zone until the account is connected again. |
| Azure DNS | A subscription, a resource group and an access token | The same hour long token. A zone is never overwritten: one that already exists is used. |

In the console, **Your own account** shows a tile for each of these hosts. A host
you have connected can be picked (with a choice of account when there are
several); one you have not is a link to connect it, and one connected with a key
that cannot create zones says so.

Each zone is found or created, never made twice: a retry uses the zone it already
made, and a zone that already existed in your account keeps what it holds. Only
the connection's own records are written into it, and nothing is deleted.

### Your own nameservers

In the console, open **Advanced** under **Your own account**, tick **Use your own
nameservers instead**, and list them one per line. Give 2 to 13 nameserver host
names. The zone must answer on them before the nameservers move: a move to your
nameservers waits in `pending` until you say the zone is ready
(`zone_ready: true`), then checks that your nameservers answer for the domain
(`skip_zone_check: true` skips that check).

### Per application

The setting, the DNS host account, the console's figures and the webhooks belong
to one application. A move only ever uses its own application's DNS host
account (a call that names another application's account is refused with
`422 dns_host_not_configured`), `GET /v1/applications/{id}/nameserver-migrations`
lists one application's moves, and the `nameservers.*` webhooks go to the move's
own application only.

## Turn it on

In the console: **Applications**, the application, **Settings**, then **Domain
maintenance** (a [test mode](/docs/guides/test-mode) application has none). Turn
on **Maintain your users' domains**, choose **Who runs it**, and **Save**. Nothing
changes for your users until you save. Once saved, each user is asked when they
connect a domain. Turned off again, new users are not asked, and domains already
maintained stay that way.

With the API, `PATCH /v1/applications/{id}` with `nameserver_migration`. The API
is the same as before the console's new words: its switches map to `mode`, and
**Who runs it** to `target`.

| In the console | `mode` | What the widget does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maintain your users' domains** off (the default) | `off` | Never offers it, nor any other DNS hosting. The application carries no setting. |
| On, **Show it as the first choice** off | `offer` | Shows it as a quiet second choice beside adding records. |
| On, **Show it as the first choice** on | `prefer` | Makes it the first screen of the manual path. Adding records stays one tap away. |

This is the one switch for every DNS hosting offer your users see. With it off,
the widget never offers to host their DNS, even where
[managed DNS](/docs/dns/managed-dns) is available, and a widget token can't
start managed DNS (`403`). With **Your own account** or your own nameservers
chosen, the move to them is the offer, and a widget token can't start
CustomDomain™'s managed DNS either (`403`). When the offer comes back `available: false` for a
domain (for example `registrar_locks_nameservers`), the widget shows no hosting
option for it at all. The console's widget designer shows this setting as **DNS
hosting: Off**, **Offered** or **Offered first**, with a link to change it.

| Who runs it | `target` |
| --- | --- |
| **CustomDomain™** (recommended, the default) | `{"kind": "managed"}` |
| **Your own account**, then the account | `{"kind": "dns_host", "account_id": "pacct_…"}` |
| **Advanced**, **Use your own nameservers instead** | `{"kind": "custom", "nameservers": […]}` |

```bash
curl -X PATCH https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/applications/<APP_ID> \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <API_KEY>" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "nameserver_migration": { "mode": "prefer", "target": { "kind": "managed" } } }'
```

Send `{"nameserver_migration": {"mode": "off"}}` to turn it off. Once it is on,
`POST /v1/domains:check` carries `nameserver_migration` for the domain (`mode`,
`target`, `available`, `path`, `provider`, `registrar`, and `reason` when
`available` is `false`, such as `registrar_locks_nameservers`), and only then.
The registrar lookup there waits 3 seconds at most; the move looks again with
more time.

## What your users see [#what-your-end-user-sees]

With **Show it as the first choice** off, the widget's manual setup shows it as a
quiet card beside adding records: "Let Acme Sites keep your domain working", "One
change where you bought it. Your website and email keep working." With it on, the
manual path opens on the consent screen, and **Add records instead** is one tap
away.

The widget asks once, in plain words. The title names your platform and the
domain ("Let Acme Sites keep acme.com working?"), and under it are "Your domain
stays where you bought it. Only one setting there changes.", the nameservers now
and after, "Your website and email keep working. Nothing changes until your
settings are in place.", "You can switch back any time." and one button, **Yes,
set it up**. For a domain whose DNS is at Cloudflare it adds "Switching back to
Cloudflare works for 7 days. After that you switch back to GoDaddy's own DNS."
(the registrar's name, or "your registrar"). It is white label: your platform's
name, never ours. The id and version of that consent text (`nsm-consent`,
version `3`) are stored with the move and the time; the person's address is not.
A widget's move must name a consent text the widget shows (`nsm-consent`
versions `1` to `3`), so a stored consent always names the words that were on the
screen.

Then the same screen shows its progress (copy settings, security check, make the
change, live) and the next thing to do: a copy to check ("Check the copy before
anything changes.", then **Looks right, continue**), the nameservers to type at
the registrar, a button to sign in to the registrar with
[secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in), or that we are waiting on the
registry. The registrar is named only when it is known; otherwise the screen
says "your registrar", never the DNS host's name. **Move back** is there while it
can be, with the other way back beside it when both are possible. A finished move
hands over to the usual success screen. Every string is in the widget's 13
languages.

## What each status means [#the-steps]

A domain's page in the console shows three steps: **Copying settings**
(`pending`, `copying`, `needs_review`), **Waiting for the registrar**
(`needs_dnssec_off` through `propagating`) and **Live** (`active`). **Domains you
maintain** and the domain's badge give its status in plain words, the first
column here:

| Console says | State | What happens | Waits on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Setting up | `pending` | Your own nameservers only: wait for `zone_ready`, then check your nameservers answer for the zone. | you |
| Setting up | `copying` | Create the zone (managed DNS, or your DNS host account), copy the domain's records into it with the connection's own, count the mail records ([The copy](#the-copy)). A copy whose write to the DNS servers failed is retried, never switched to. Retried every 2 minutes for 6 hours. | us |
| Check the copy | `needs_review` | The copy is not proven complete: it was read from public DNS, or some records could not be carried over. `copy_warnings` says what to check, and the move goes on once a person confirms it (`copy_reviewed: true`; in the console, **The records look right, continue**). 14 days at most. | a person |
| Waiting for the user | `needs_dnssec_off` | The registry publishes a `DS` record and we cannot remove it ourselves. | the person |
| Waiting for the registrar | `waiting_ds` | DNSSEC is off. Wait until the registry stops publishing the `DS` record and its TTL has passed (5 minutes to 48 hours; 1 hour when unknown). | the registry |
| Waiting for the user (paths `manual` and `agent`), or Waiting for the registrar (path `api`) | `setting` | The nameservers are being changed (`waiting_for` says by whom). With a key, the registrar is read first and its answer is read back ([below](#how-the-nameservers-change)); when its answer was lost and it cannot be read back yet, the move waits here with `waiting_for: registrar_confirm`, can still be moved back, and the next check reads back first. 14 days at most. | the registrar or the person |
| Almost live | `propagating` | Changed. The delegation is read every 5 minutes for 2 hours, then hourly up to 48 hours. | the registry |
| Maintained | `active` | The registry lists every new nameserver. Read again every 6 hours: a move away is `failed` with `delegation_drift`, and back again is `active`. Once a day, whether the old nameservers still answer for the domain (`old_dns_serves`), which decides the [default way back](#moving-back). | |
| Stopped | `failed` | `failure_code` says why. A delegation failure is still read twice a day for 14 days, and turns `active` if the delegation becomes the new one. | |
| Moved back | `rolled_back` | Moved back. No longer maintained. | |

Why a move stopped, as `failure_code` and as the console says it:

| `failure_code` | Console says |
| --- | --- |
| `zone_copy_failed` (also a copy not confirmed within 14 days) | Copying the zone failed |
| `zone_not_served` | The new nameservers did not answer |
| `dnssec_timeout` (after 7 days) | DNSSEC stayed on |
| `ds_removal_failed` | DNSSEC could not be turned off |
| `credential_refused` | The key was refused |
| `registrar_refused` (it was read back and still lists the old nameservers) | The registrar refused |
| `registrar_locked` | The domain is locked |
| `changed_elsewhere` (the registrar listed other nameservers than the ones the move started from, so they were not overwritten) | Changed elsewhere first |
| `agent_failed` | The sign in did not finish |
| `delegation_timeout` | The registry never showed it |
| `delegation_drift` | Moved away after |
| `internal` | Something went wrong on our side |

A zone has one open move at a time, so two connections under one registrable
domain (`acme.com` and `shop.acme.com`) share it, and a move of the same zone for
another account is refused with `409`.

## The copy [#the-copy]

The new DNS host must hold every record the domain relies on before the
nameservers move, or the switch takes the website and the mail down. There are
two ways to know them, and the move says which in `copy_source`:

- **`api`: the whole zone, read through the old DNS host's API.** When
  CustomDomain™ holds a key at the DNS host the domain uses now (a sign-in kept
  for the connection, or a connected account there), every record is read
  through that host's API. Such a copy is proven complete, unless a record could
  not be carried over (a forwarding rule, or a root `CNAME` a host flattens),
  which `copy_warnings` names.
- **`discovery`: read from public DNS.** Public DNS cannot list a domain's
  records, so the names domains commonly use are asked one by one. Such a copy is
  never proven, and the move waits in `needs_review` until a person checks
  `copy_warnings` and confirms it. Your server may accept it in advance by
  sending `copy_reviewed: true` with the start; a copy that read nothing at all
  always waits for a person, and in the widget only after the end user saw it.
  For a domain whose DNS is at Cloudflare, records that answer with Cloudflare's
  proxy addresses are not copied as if they were the website's own address: they
  are left out and named, so the person adds the real addresses or connects the
  Cloudflare account.
- **`existing_zone`** (your own account only): your zone already held records,
  which are kept; only the connection's own records are written.

Nothing changes at the registrar until the copy is at the new DNS servers: a
copy whose write failed is retried, never switched to. When a person confirms a
copy, the time is kept (`copy_reviewed_at`).

## How the nameservers change

No standard lets a third party ask a registrar to change a domain's nameservers.
Domain Connect changes records only, and CDS and CSYNC (RFC 7344, RFC 7477) need
the current DNS host to publish them and the domain to be signed with DNSSEC,
which most domains are not. So each move takes one of three paths:

| `path` | How | Credential |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `api` | The registrar's own API. | A key the customer created, at the registrar: a connected provider account, or a key sent with the call and used once, never stored. The connection's linked account is used when it is at the registrar, also for an end user's move. |
| `agent` | [Secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in): the end user signs in to their registrar and approves a plan whose only step is the exact new nameservers (or turning DNSSEC off, or for a move back the old nameservers or the registrar's own DNS). Only those controls are opened, and only the approved values are typed; DNSSEC stays closed unless the plan turns it off. A code the registrar asks for is handed back to the person. | None: the person's own sign-in. |
| `manual` | The person sets them at the registrar with the values shown. | None |

A registrar's answer is not taken at its word. Before a change, the registrar is
read: it must still list the nameservers the move started from, so a change
someone made there since is never overwritten (the move stops with
`changed_elsewhere`). After an error, the registrar (then the registry) is read
back, because a timeout can come after the registrar acted: the new nameservers
mean the change happened, the old ones mean it did not (`registrar_refused`), and
no answer leaves the move in `setting` with `waiting_for: registrar_confirm`.

What each registrar's API can do:

| Registrar | Read | Change | Remove a `DS` record | Back to its own DNS | Most nameservers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GoDaddy | yes | yes | no | no (secure sign-in or by hand) | 13 |
| Namecheap | yes | yes | no | yes (BasicDNS) | 12 |
| Name.com | yes | yes | yes | yes | 13 |
| Porkbun | yes | yes | yes | yes | 13 |
| Hostinger | yes | yes | no | yes | 4 |
| Spaceship | yes | yes | no | yes | 12 |
| IONOS | yes | no (secure sign-in or by hand) | no | no (secure sign-in or by hand) | |

Secure sign-in can change the nameservers at Namecheap, GoDaddy and Name.com, where
it is offered, and choose the registrar's own DNS at Namecheap ("Namecheap
BasicDNS") and GoDaddy ("GoDaddy Nameservers"). A [lasting API key](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in#lasting-api-keys)
made at sign-in counts as a connected account (at GoDaddy with GoDaddy's
permission), and it changes the nameservers only for domains of the application
it was made for. A key is never read during a move's own sign-in.

## Why DNSSEC comes first [#dnssec]

DNSSEC is a security lock some domains have on their DNS: the registry publishes
a `DS` record, and resolvers use it to check that answers come from the domain's
DNS host. When the registry publishes one, the nameservers must not move until it
is gone and resolvers have dropped it, or validating resolvers would stop
answering for the domain. With a connected account that keeps access (path `api`)
at a registrar whose API removes `DS` records (Name.com and Porkbun), we remove it
and wait (`waiting_ds`). Otherwise the move stops at `needs_dnssec_off`, the
workspace's owners and admins get a "needs attention"
[notification](/docs/guides/notifications), and the secure sign-in path offers a
session whose only step is turning DNSSEC off. Some registrars turn DNSSEC on by default
(Squarespace and Spaceship among them), so expect it.

The new DNS host does not sign the zone. Turning DNSSEC on again there is the DNS
host owner's choice after the move.

## What moving back does [#moving-back]

A domain can be moved back two ways, and a move back names one in
`rollback_target`:

| Way back | `rollback_target` | Where the domain's DNS goes | When it works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **To the previous nameservers** | `previous` | Back to the DNS host it had before (`old_nameservers`). We never touched the records there, so the domain answers as it did before the move. | Only while that host still answers for the domain. Some hosts delete a domain's records a few days after its nameservers move away: Cloudflare deletes such a zone after 7 days, and its support cannot restore it. So the old nameservers are asked first, and a move back to ones that no longer answer is refused with `409 old_dns_gone`. |
| **To the registrar's own DNS** | `registrar_default` | The registrar's "use our nameservers" choice (often called its default DNS). It holds the records it had before the move, not the ones changed since; at Spaceship and Squarespace DNSSEC may sign the domain again. | Always. |

Without a choice, a move back goes to the previous nameservers while they still
answer, and to the registrar's own DNS once they do not. The move says which:
`rollback_default` is where a plain move back goes, `rollback_targets` lists the
ways possible now (`previous` is left out once the old nameservers stopped
answering), and `old_dns_serves` says whether they still answered when last
asked (once a day while the move is active, and at a move back). A domain that
used the registrar's own DNS when the move started goes back through the
registrar's own reset, not by setting its old names as custom ones.

How it is made, in `rollback_path`:

- `api`: through the registrar's API, at once (`rollback_set_at`), with a key
  sent once or the move's connected account. For the registrar's own DNS, this is
  its reset call where it has one: Namecheap, Spaceship, Name.com, Porkbun and
  Hostinger ([table above](#how-the-nameservers-change)).
- `agent`: the end user signs in to the registrar with
  [secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in) (`purpose: nameservers`) and
  approves the move back: the old nameservers, or the registrar's own DNS. This
  is the way to the registrar's own DNS at GoDaddy, and elsewhere without a key.
- `manual`: `next_step` says what to set, or what to choose at the registrar.
- `none`: nothing had changed at the registrar yet, so the move is just stopped.

A move back reads the registrar first. It must still list the move's
nameservers: if someone changed them since, the answer is
`409 changed_elsewhere` and nothing is written. A list already where the move
back goes (an earlier answer lost) counts as done. A registrar error is read back
too: `502 registrar_refused` when nothing changed, `504 registrar_unconfirmed`
when it cannot tell, and nothing is recorded then, so try again. Any secure
sign-in still working on the move is ended first. It is idempotent, and the
`nameservers.rolled_back` webhook says where it went (`rollback_target`).

In the console, the domain's DNS tab offers the default way first (**Move back**,
or **Move back to GoDaddy's DNS** once the old nameservers no longer answer) and,
beside it, the other one when it is possible. Each asks first ("Move acme.com
back?") and says what it does: "Its nameservers go back to the ones it had before. Its previous provider
still answers for it, so it works as before.", or "It goes back to GoDaddy's own
DNS, with the records it held there before the move. Records changed since then
are not in it." In the widget, your user sees the same two buttons.

A move back does not yet copy the connection's records into the registrar's DNS,
restore how the connection was written before the move, or delete the zone the
move created.

## API

| Route | Who may call it |
| --- | --- |
| `PATCH /v1/applications/{id}` with `nameserver_migration` | An API key that can write applications. |
| `POST /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers:migrate` | An API key; a widget token (the end user: the application's mode must be `offer` or `prefer`, `consent` must name a text the widget shows, `target`, a key and the zone flags are refused, and `copy_reviewed` is taken only for a move in `needs_review`); an agent token with `domains:connect` (only while the application's mode is `offer` or `prefer`, else `403 ns_not_offered`; no target but the application's own, and no `skip_zone_check`: `403 target_not_allowed`). An agent token or a widget token never resumes the open move of another application of the workspace (`409`). |
| `GET /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers` | An API key, a widget token, or an agent token with `domains:read`. Every answer that carries a move also carries `next_action` ([below](#next-action)). |
| `POST /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers:rollback` with an optional `rollback_target` | An API key; a widget token (it may choose where, but not send a key); an agent token with `domains:connect` (its move back is recorded as `agent.acted`). |
| `GET /v1/applications/{id}/nameserver-migrations?limit=&since=` | An API key: one application's moves and their `stats`. |
| `GET /v1/connections?dns_hosting=hosted`, `moving` or `any` | An API key, an app-scoped token or an agent token with `domains:read`: the domains whose DNS CustomDomain™ runs ([below](#find-hosted-domains)). Any other value is `400`. |
| `POST /v1/connections/{id}/browser:start` with `"purpose": "nameservers"` | The widget or the console: the secure sign-in session of the `agent` path, once the move waits for it (`waiting_for: browser_session`). |

### Find the domains that are hosted [#find-hosted-domains]

Every connection carries `dns_hosting` (`state` `none`, `moving`, `hosted` or
`failed`, and `by`, `provider`, `zone` and `since` once there is something to
say), on the connection list and on `GET /v1/connections/{id}`. Ask the list for
the domains whose DNS CustomDomain™ runs with `?dns_hosting=hosted`; `moving`
keeps those being set up or moved, and `any` keeps hosted, moving and failed. A
page is always full and `next_cursor` keeps walking the matching rows.

This finds a domain whose DNS is hosted by a move, and also one whose zone you
started with [managed DNS](/docs/dns/managed-dns) and never moved: the list of
moves above holds moves only. A subdomain counts when its parent domain is hosted
(`shop.acme.com` under `acme.com`). A move that was rolled back, or that failed
for any reason but the domain's nameservers moving away again, is not hosting.

Start a move from your server through the customer's connected registrar
account:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/connections/<ID>/nameservers:migrate \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <API_KEY>" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "path": "api", "provider_account_id": "pacct_…" }'
```

A provider account given for some domains only (scope `selected`) moves only
those domains' nameservers: for any other domain the call answers `403`, and a
move already under way stops using it once the domain is taken off its list. To
use a key once instead, send `provider` (the registrar's id, such as `porkbun`)
and `credential: {"token": "…"}` in the registrar's key format; it is used for
that call and never stored.

`201` creates the move and `200` resumes the domain's open one with what the call
brings (a key, `zone_ready`, `copy_reviewed` once a person checked the copy). The
answer is the move as far as it got in that call:
with a registrar key and no DNSSEC it is often already `propagating`. Poll
`GET /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers` until `state` is `active`, `failed` or
`rolled_back`. `waiting_for` says what it waits on and `next_step` is one sentence
for a person. The shape is illustrative; the
[API reference](/docs/api-reference) has every field under **Nameservers**.

The `422` refusals say why a move cannot start: `nameserver_migration_unavailable`
(`details` says why), `nameserver_api_unsupported` (the registrar has no API call
that changes nameservers), `nameserver_agent_unavailable` (secure sign-in cannot
change this registrar's nameservers), `registrar_locks_nameservers` (Cloudflare
Registrar, [above](#where-the-nameservers-are-changed)), `registrar_mismatch`
(the key or account is at another company than the domain's registrar),
`zone_host_unsupported` or `dns_host_not_configured`.

**Agent tokens move nameservers only while DNS hosting is on.** Moving a domain's
nameservers hands the whole domain, mail included, to whoever runs the new ones, so
an AI agent may do it only for an application that offers it (mode `offer` or
`prefer`). With the mode `off`, or no setting saved, every call to
`:migrate` by an agent token is refused with `403 ns_not_offered` and changes
nothing: it covers a start and the resume of a move a person began, writes no row
and calls no registrar. A person can turn DNS hosting on in the application's
settings (the DNS hosting card offers it), or your platform can start the move for
one domain with its API key, which this rule leaves as it was; your end users'
widget keeps its own `403` for an application that does not offer it. Moving back
(`:rollback`) stays open to an agent, so it can undo a move.

**Agent tokens never choose where the nameservers go.** A delegated agent token
(an AI agent acting for a person) may start a move only to the application's own
target, and may not send `skip_zone_check`; anything else is refused with
`403 target_not_allowed`. With a registrar account linked, nameservers an agent
chose would hand it the whole domain in one call. Your own API key may still name
`managed` or `custom`.

**Agent tokens and widget tokens act only on their own application's moves.** Two
applications of one workspace can share a customer's zone (`www` in one, the apex
in another), and a credential bound to one application never resumes the other's
open move: the call answers `409`, with no word of the move, and only an API key,
which spans the workspace, resumes it. Every start, resume or move back by an
agent token that changed the move is in the [audit log](/docs/agents/overview#what-makes-it-safe)
as `agent.acted` (`moved a domain's nameservers`, `moved a domain's nameservers
back`) under `agent:<grant id>`, whichever key it used; a call that only read the
move leaves no row.

### What to press next [#next-action]

Every answer that carries a move has `next_action`: the one request to press next,
or `null` when nothing is waiting for a press. `waiting_for` and `next_step` say what
a move waits on in words; `next_action` says which button to show. It is
`{ "kind", "method", "path", "body" }`, with the connection's id in `path`, and it
is the same for every caller.

| `kind` | When | The request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sign_in` | The move waits for the secure sign-in (`needs_dnssec_off` or `setting` on the `agent` path), or a move back by secure sign-in is not made yet. | `POST /v1/connections/{id}/browser:start` with `{"purpose": "nameservers", "provider": "..."}`. Add the `return_origin` and `consent_version` you send for any sign-in. |
| `rollback` | The move failed after the registrar was changed or asked (`can_rollback` is `true`), for example `delegation_timeout` or `delegation_drift`. | `POST /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers:rollback` with `{"rollback_target": "..."}`, the default way back. |
| `retry` | The move failed before anything changed at the registrar (`can_rollback` is `false`), for example `zone_copy_failed` or `credential_refused`. | `POST /v1/connections/{id}/nameservers:migrate` with no body (an end user's start also sends `consent`). |

It is `null` while the move copies, waits for a copy review (`copy_reviewed`), for the
registry, for DNSSEC or the change to be made by a person at the registrar, or for a
key to be reconnected, once it is `active`, and when it is over.

Because `next_action` is the same for every caller, it can name a request the caller
who reads it is refused. There are three such cases. A caller in one of them does not
send the request; it tells the person what `next_step` says.

- `sign_in` points at `browser:start`, which runs only from a customer's browser or the
  console. It refuses every agent token, and every API key without the console's
  label, with `403 browser_customer_present_only` ([secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in)).
- `retry` is refused to an agent token while the application's DNS hosting is off
  (`403 ns_not_offered`), even when the failed move is one a person started.
- `retry` is refused to a widget token (the end user) when the application's setting is
  off (`403 forbidden`, "this application does not offer nameserver migration").

`rollback` is open to all of them whatever the setting says.

A move back takes `{"rollback_target": "previous"}` or
`{"rollback_target": "registrar_default"}`, or nothing for the default
([What moving back does](#moving-back)). Its refusals: `409 old_dns_gone` (the
old nameservers no longer answer: move back to the registrar's own DNS instead),
`409 changed_elsewhere` (the registrar lists other nameservers than the move's),
`502 registrar_refused` (nothing changed) and `504 registrar_unconfirmed` (the
answer was lost and could not be read back: try again).

## Webhooks

Seven events follow a move. Each carries a `nameservers` object and is written to
the domain's timeline as a row of the same kind. When they are known, the object
also names the `registrar` (`name`, `provider`, `iana_id`) and the `old_dns_host`,
and on `nameservers.rolled_back` it says where the move back went
(`rollback_target`). See the
[event catalog](/docs/webhooks/overview#event-catalog) and the
[payload](/docs/webhooks/overview#payload-envelope).

| Event | When |
| --- | --- |
| `nameservers.started` | A move was created. |
| `nameservers.dnssec_cleared` | The registry stopped publishing the `DS` record. |
| `nameservers.changed` | The registrar took the new nameservers, or the delegation first showed one. |
| `nameservers.active` | The registry lists every new nameserver. |
| `nameservers.failed` | The move stopped (`failure_code`). |
| `nameservers.rolled_back` | Moved back, to the previous nameservers or the registrar's own DNS (`rollback_target`). |
| `nameservers.drifted` | An active move's nameservers were changed away (`observed_nameservers`). It is also an inbox item and the drift email. |

## MCP

The [MCP server](/docs/mcp/overview) has three tools for it:
`check-nameserver-status` (`domains:read`), and `migrate-nameservers` and
`rollback-nameservers` (`domains:connect`, both with `confirm: true`, which an
agent sends only after the person agreed). The tools never send a registrar key.
With an agent token, `migrate-nameservers` refuses `target` and `nameservers`:
the application's setting decides where the nameservers go, and the control plane
refuses the same (`403 target_not_allowed`). A move that another application of
the workspace has open is never resumed with an agent token (`409`).
While the application's DNS hosting is off, `migrate-nameservers` is refused with
the error `ns_not_offered` and changes nothing: the agent does not retry, and tells
the person they can turn DNS hosting on in the application's settings, or that the
platform can start the move with its API key. `rollback-nameservers` is not refused.
`migrate-nameservers` takes `copyReviewed` once the agent showed the person the
copy's warnings and they agreed, and `rollback-nameservers` takes `target`
(`previous` or `registrar_default`). Results carry `registrar`, `oldDnsHost`, `copySource`,
`copyWarnings`, `rollbackDefault` and `rollbackTargets`.

## What changes for your users [#end-user-faq]

In the words you can give them.

**What am I agreeing to?**
That the platform keeps your domain working for you. One setting at the company
you bought your domain from changes, so the platform can keep your domain's
settings correct, renew its certificate and fix problems without asking you
again.

**Will my website or email stop working?**
No. Your settings are copied first, email included, and nothing changes until
they are in place. If some could only be read from outside, you are asked to
check them first.

**Does my domain move to another company?**
No. It stays registered where you bought it, with the same renewal date and the
same owner. You keep paying for it there.

**What do I have to do?**
Say yes. Then, unless the change can be made for you, either paste the values
shown at the company you bought your domain from, or sign in there and approve
the change. The screen tells you which.

**How long does it take?**
Usually minutes. It can take up to 48 hours to show everywhere.

**Why do I have to turn off DNSSEC?**
DNSSEC is a security lock on the old settings. It must be off before they move,
or the domain would stop working during the move. You can turn it on again
afterwards.

**Can I switch back?**
Yes, any time, two ways. To your previous provider, while it still has your
records: some delete them a few days after the domain moves away (Cloudflare
after 7). Or to your registrar's own DNS, which is always there, with the records
it held before the move.

**Who can change my records afterwards?**
The platform, through the DNS host it uses. You can still ask it to change or
show them.
