title: Provider accounts
description: "Connect a DNS provider or registrar account once, for some or all of its domains, with one time or continuous access, see who on your team can manage it, and find every change in the audit log."
---

A provider account is a key for a DNS provider or registrar that the workspace
connects once. CustomDomain™ checks the key before keeping anything, keeps it
encrypted (`GRANT_ENCRYPTION_KEY`), and never returns it.

The key is checked by listing the provider's domains. A provider that cannot
list them is asked for one of the selected domains instead; with scope `all`,
name a domain the key manages in `check_domain`, or the call answers `400`
`check_domain_required`. A key is never kept unchecked.

## Access

- **Continuous**: kept until you revoke it. It keeps the records it wrote:
  putting back records that disappear, where the deployment has automatic
  repair on ([monitoring](/docs/connect-flow/monitoring)), removing records
  when a domain is [removed](/docs/connect-flow/offboarding), and re-applying
  them when you ask (`POST /v1/connections/{id}:reapply`, which answers
  `via: "provider_account"`). Whoever may manage the account also uses it to
  list the domains it reaches and connect more of them, and a
  [nameserver move](/docs/guides/nameserver-migration) may use it. Each of
  these is in the [audit log](#audit-log).
- **One time**: used to connect the domains you choose, then deleted. It works
  for an hour at most: past the hour every use is refused with `409` and the
  key is deleted, whether or not the hourly cleanup got to it first. The
  account stays as the record of what it connected.

Certificates renew without any DNS access, so both behave the same there.

### Credentials that are used once

Some providers' credentials are never kept, because they are an account login
or a token that expires within hours or cannot be limited: ALL-INKL (the KAS
login), Arsys (the Panel de Control password), Azure DNS and Google Cloud DNS
(access tokens that expire within an hour), Binary Lane (a token that reaches
the whole account), INWX, Loopia, Plesk and Subreg (logins), and Openprovider
(a token that expires after 48 hours). They get one time access only:
`"access": "continuous"` answers `422` `use_once_credential` when you connect
the account, switch its access or give it a new key, and a connect that names
no access gets one time access. The console offers them one time access only,
and says why.
Sign-ins get the same choice: `oauth:start` takes `"access": "continuous"` or
`"one_time"`; continuous is offered where the deployment can keep that
provider's sign-in.

## Defaults and limits

When you leave them out, `access` is `continuous` (`one_time` for a
[credential that is used once](#credentials-that-are-used-once)), and `scope`
is `selected` if you send `domains` and `all` if you do not. `POST /v1/provider-accounts/{id}/connect`
takes at most 200 domains per call (`400` beyond that).

## Scope

All of the account's domains (`"scope": "all"`, including ones added later), or
a selected list (`"scope": "selected"`, `domains`) picked from the provider's
own list where it has one.

## Who can manage an account

Anyone whose role operates applications may connect a key, read the accounts
and revoke one. Changing an account's access, scope or domains, listing the
domains its key reaches, connecting domains with it and giving it a new key take
an admin, an owner or the member who connected it, because the key reaches zones
the others were never given; anyone else gets `403`. Each account says whether
you may (`can_manage`).

A viewer reads the accounts without what identifies your customer at their
provider: a [key made at a secure sign-in](#keys-made-at-a-secure-sign-in) shows
them no `agent_key.account` (the customer number) and no `agent_key.session_id`.

Sharing a key with another application is narrower still: only an admin or an
owner may, not the member who connected the key. See
[which applications may use it](#which-applications-may-use-it).

## Endpoints (API key)

| Call | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /v1/provider-accounts` | Connect a key: `{ provider, credential: { token }, access, scope, domains?, check_domain? }`. Answers the provider's domains when it lists them. |
| `GET /v1/provider-accounts` | Every account with access, domains, last use and expiry, plus kept sign-ins and Domain Connect authorizations grouped by provider. |
| `PATCH /v1/provider-accounts/{id}` | Change label, access or scope, or the applications the key is shared with (`application_ids`, admin or owner only). A domain taken out stays connected, without continuous access. |
| `POST /v1/provider-accounts/{id}/zones` | The domains the key reaches. Takes whoever may manage the account. |
| `POST /v1/provider-accounts/{id}/connect` | Connect domains to an application with the key: `{ application_id, domains }`. A domain is linked to the account once its records are written; a domain in a zone the key does not reach fails alone (`zone_not_reached`) and is not linked. |
| `POST /v1/provider-accounts/{id}/reconnect` | A new key, for an account the provider stopped accepting: `{ credential: { token, params? }, access?, check_domain? }`. Checked before it is kept; `params` replace the kept ones. |
| `DELETE /v1/provider-accounts/{id}` | Revoke: the key is deleted; its domains stay connected. |

A key the provider refuses marks the account `needs_reconnect` and each of its
domains records it on its timeline. A refusal that only concerns one zone (the
key works, but not for that domain) fails that domain and leaves the account
as it is. The console's **Connected accounts** page
shows all of this; *Remember this key* on the connect page keeps the key as a
continuous account.

## Keys made at a secure sign-in

When a [secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in#lasting-api-keys) makes a
lasting API key at a domain's DNS provider, CustomDomain™ keeps it as a provider
account of the workspace, checked and encrypted like any other, with continuous
access and the domain's zone as its scope. Such an account is different in five
ways:

- **It belongs to one application.** `application_id` names the application
  whose domain it was made for. Connecting another application's domain with it
  is refused with `403`, unless an admin
  [shared the key](#which-applications-may-use-it) with that application.
- **It can serve the application's other domains.** A key made at a sign in you
  or a teammate started in the console connects another domain of the same
  application with no second sign in and no second key: CustomDomain™ first
  reads that domain's records with the key, and only when the provider answers
  is the domain connected; its zone joins the key's scope once the records are
  written there. A domain in another account at the provider is refused with
  `400`, and needs its own sign-in. "Started in the console" means the
  console's own signed call: a sign-in your backend starts through the API, even
  one that names the console in its headers, makes a key that serves its one
  domain only. A key your customer's sign-in made in the widget serves only the
  domain they agreed to.
- **It says where it came from.** `origin` is `browser_agent`, and `agent_key`
  holds its record, never the key: the `name` it was made with (`CustomDomain`
  and your application's name), its `scopes` and `rights` (what it can do beyond
  DNS: `transfer`, `billing` or `account`), the provider's `account` identifier
  when the page showed one (never an email address, and not shown to a
  viewer), the `session_id` that made it (not shown to a viewer either), when it was checked (`verified_at`) and ends (`expires_at`), the last
  `notice`, the `reconnect_reason`, `reconnect_sent_at` and `renewed_at`,
  `old_key: "revoke_by_hand"` after a renewal, and the provider's `revoke_url`.
- **It is renewed by a sign-in, not a pasted key.** Before its end, or once the
  provider refuses it, the account is `needs_reconnect` and your customer signs
  in again ([reconnecting](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in#reconnect)). In the
  console, **Connected accounts** shows it as **Made at sign in**, and its only
  action is **Remove**.
- **Removing it deletes only our copy.** `DELETE /v1/provider-accounts/{id}`
  answers the provider's `revoke_url` and sends `credential.removed`: the key
  itself lives at the provider until it is revoked there.

## Which applications may use it

A key made at a secure sign-in belongs to one application (`application_id`) and
serves its domains alone. It serves another application of the workspace only
when an admin shares it, so a workspace with two applications can sign in once
for a provider account and use that key for both.

Send `application_ids` to `PATCH /v1/provider-accounts/{id}`: the whole list of
**other** applications that may use the key, besides its own.

```json
{ "application_ids": ["app_marketing"] }
```

- **Who may.** An admin or an owner. The member who connected the key manages it,
  and still gets `403` here. Anyone else gets `403` too.
- **Which keys.** A key bound to one application. A key your customer's own
  sign-in made is never shared (`403`): it acts only for that customer in their
  application. A key you pasted that is not bound to an application already
  serves every one, so naming an application answers `422`
  `serves_every_application`.
- **Which applications.** Each must belong to your workspace, or the call
  answers `404`. The key's own application always serves, so naming it, naming an
  application twice or sending a blank changes nothing. At most 100 per call.
- **It replaces the set.** Leave an application out to take the key back from it,
  and send `[]` to share with none. The answer's `application_ids` is the list
  now in force.
- **What a share allows.** The shared application's domains can use the key like
  its own: the saved-access list offers it for the second application's domains
  (and never for a third), `POST /v1/provider-accounts/{id}/connect` takes
  that application's id, a second secure sign-in for the same provider account
  from the shared application swaps its key into the shared account instead of
  adding a second one, and reconnecting a domain of the shared application renews
  the key in the same account.
- **What taking it back does.** The domains of that application that were using
  the key stay connected, without continuous access, and each records
  `access_revoked` with the reason `unshared` on its timeline.
- **Audited.** Each application shared writes `provider_account.shared` and each
  taken out `provider_account.unshared`, with the applications the key served
  before and after and the ones that changed.

## Whose key it is, and saved access

Every account says whose it is. `owner_kind` is `workspace` for a key your
workspace holds (one you pasted, or one made at a sign-in you or a teammate
started in the console) and `end_user` for a key your customer's own sign-in
made. A key made by a customer acts only for that customer, in the application
where they made it: you can narrow it or remove it, and you cannot use it, list
the domains it reaches or widen it. `account_ref` is the provider account the
key belongs to, and `end_user_ref` your id for the customer, when the sign-in
named one. A viewer sees neither.

**Saved access** answers "which kept key can act for this domain, for me" in one
place, so a second domain at the same provider does not start a second sign-in:

- `GET /v1/connections/{id}/saved-access` lists them, best first. A key is
  listed when it is at the domain's provider, kept (continuous) and still
  working, serves the connection's application, and you may use it. A key whose
  scope already holds the domain's zone is `ready`. A key made at a sign-in
  started in the console may also be listed with `ready: false`, after one read
  with it showed it reaches the zone; using it adds the zone to its scope. At
  most three such reads happen per call, and each answer is kept ten minutes.
  There is one entry per provider account.
- `POST /v1/connections/{id}:use-saved-access` with `{"account_id": "..."}`
  writes the domain's records with that key and links it once the write worked.
  A write that fails links nothing, leaves the key's scope as it was and
  records the failure on the domain (`error_code` and `error_message`):
  `502 saved_access_write_failed`, with the provider's words. An account that
  cannot act for the domain is `409 saved_access_not_usable`.
- `rails.saved_access` on `POST /v1/domains:check` is the same list for an API
  key caller, and `recommended_rail` is then `saved_access`. An end user's
  widget token never gets it, and no MCP tool uses a kept key: `discover-provider`
  reports `rails.savedAccess` and tells the agent to point the person at the
  domain's page in the console.
- `GET /v1/provider-accounts?provider=&domain=` keeps the accounts that can act
  for a domain.

A secure sign-in or a provider sign-in for a domain that a saved key can already
act for answers `409 saved_access_available` before anything starts. Send
`new_sign_in: true` to sign in with another account. Your customers' widget
sessions are not affected: saved access for returning customers is not offered
yet.

A second secure sign-in for a provider account you already hold a key for, from
the application the key belongs to or one it is shared with, does not add a
second row. Its key is swapped into the existing account, and the
domain's zone joins that account's scope. The key it replaces still lives at the
provider unless it was the same key, so the account shows `old_key:
"revoke_by_hand"` with the provider's `revoke_url`; CustomDomain™ never revokes
a key itself. If the write that follows the sign-in fails, the account keeps the
new key (and `old_key`), the zone leaves its scope again unless the account
covered it before, and the domain records the failure; an account other domains
use is never removed for one failed write. GoDaddy is the exception for now: each
sign-in there keeps its own key.

## Audit log

Every change to a provider account, and every use of its key beyond the records
it wrote, is in the workspace's audit log (`GET /v1/audit`, and the console's
**Activity** page), with who did it as the server knows them, never the key:

| Event | When |
| --- | --- |
| `provider_account.created` | A key is kept: pasted, or made at a secure sign-in. |
| `provider_account.updated` | Its label, access, scope or domains change, with the values before and after. |
| `provider_account.domain_added` | A key made at a sign-in in the console starts serving another domain's zone. |
| `provider_account.relinked` | A domain the application already had now uses this account. |
| `provider_account.key_replaced` | It gets a new key: pasted, or made when someone signed in again. |
| `provider_account.shared` | An admin or an owner lets another application use the key. Names the applications it serves before and after, and the ones just shared (`application_ids`). |
| `provider_account.unshared` | An admin or an owner takes that back. Same fields. |
| `provider_account.removed` | Our copy is deleted: revoked, its last domain removed, or the write after a sign-in failed. |
| `credential.used_for_nameservers` | A [nameserver move](/docs/guides/nameserver-migration), or a move back, uses its key. |
