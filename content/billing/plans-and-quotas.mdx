## Plans

`GET /v1/plans` returns the public catalog, with a `billing: { configured, mode, trial_days }` block.
The figures it returns are the defaults.
A deployment's operator can change some of them without a release.
They can change a plan's price (`price_cents`) and its domain allowance (`domains_per_year`, and so `domains_per_month`).
They can also change its trial (`trial_days`) and whether it blocks at quota (`hard_cap`).
So read them from `GET /v1/plans` instead of hard coding them.
`billing.trial_days` is the default plan's trial.

<OpenInConsole to="/app/billing/plans" label="Open Plans in console" />

Entitlements gate which products a tenant may use.
**Enforced today** are `Power` and `Secure`.
`Power` covers serving domains through the CustomDomain™ edge.
That means the `/v1/connections/{id}/power*` surface, the application's default origin, and an `application_url` sent with `POST /v1/connections`.
`Secure` covers the `/v1/ssl*` surface.
An unentitled tenant gets `402 plan_upgrade_required`.
`WhiteLabel` (widget branding) is enforced by dropping the branding, as described below.
`Connect` is included on every plan and needs no gate.
`Monitor` is listed as an entitlement on Premium and Enterprise, but nothing enforces it.
Every connection that has `monitor` on (the default) is monitored, on every plan.
The deployment-level `MONITOR_ALERTS_ENABLED` flag governs alert delivery for everyone.

| Plan | Price | Domains / year (sold) | Enforced allowance | Refused at quota? | Entitlements | Motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `starter` | $10/mo | 10 | 10 in any 12 months | **yes** | Connect | self-serve, 14 day free trial |
| `startup` | $149/mo | 600 | 50 a month | no | Connect | self-serve, 14 day free trial |
| `growth` | $649/mo | 600 | 50 a month | no | Connect · Power · Secure/SSL | self-serve, 14 day free trial |
| `premium` | demo-gated | 12,000 | 1,000 a month | no | + Monitor (listed, not enforced) | contact sales |
| `enterprise` | custom | 12,000+ | 1,000 a month | no | + white-label | contact sales |

Prices and the domain columns above are transcribed from `GET /v1/plans`
(`price_cents`, `domains_per_year`, and `quota` over `quota_window_months`). If
this table and that endpoint ever disagree, the endpoint is right and this page
is stale.

**Starter replaced the Free plan on 2026-09-29.** `free` is retired.
No new workspace is put on it, `GET /v1/plans` no longer lists it, and Checkout refuses it.
Workspaces that were on Free keep working while they move to a paid plan.
See [The Free plan's retirement](#the-free-plans-retirement).

### Two numbers, and which one gates you

A tier is **sold** annually. The meter counts domain connections per UTC
calendar month, and the quota gate compares a count against the plan's
**`quota`** over its **`quota_window_months`**:

- **Starter (and the retired Free): 10 over a rolling 12 months.**
  The gate adds this month's count to the 11 months before it.
  So a Starter workspace can connect several domains in one month, as long as it stays within 10 in any 12 months.
  Before November 2, 2026, Starter was held to 1 a month.
- **Every other tier: its monthly slice, `domains_per_month`**, which is
  `ceil(domains_per_year / 12)`, over 1 month. Startup's 600/year is therefore
  **50 in any one month**. Ceiling division means the annualized entitlement
  never shrinks below what was sold.

> **Whether that monthly number *blocks* takes both a plan property and a deployment switch.**
> The plan property is `hard_cap`, and it is `true` only on `starter` (and the retired `free`).
> See [Overage](#overage) for why no larger tier is ever refused.
> The switch is `BILLING_ENFORCE_QUOTA`.
> `infra/docker-compose.prod.yml` ships it defaulted to `0`, so the hosted service only meters and refuses nothing at quota.
> `1` turns enforcement on.
> Both must hold.
> The gate also fails **open** by design, even when enabled.
> A billing lookup error allows the connect, instead of blocking a customer during a billing outage.
> The gate is `enforceQuota` in `services/control-plane/internal/api/billing.go`.
> The billing service applies the same `hard_cap` rule in `ConnectDomain`, so the two cannot disagree.

All of these are published on `GET /v1/plans` (`domains_per_year`,
`domains_per_month`, `quota` and `quota_window_months`). `GET /v1/billing/usage`
compares its `quota_used` (the count over the window) with `quota`; its `used`
stays this month's count.

Self-serve plans (`starter`, `startup`, `growth`) are bought through Stripe
Checkout. `premium` and `enterprise` route to a "book a demo" CTA and have no
public price.

**White-label is enforced on the server, not just hidden in the console.**
A direct widget `open()` always applies whatever branding you configure.
That's your own client-side config, and the control plane never sees it.
A connect may be forwarded through a share link and resumed by a teammate (`loadSharedFlow`).
Then the control plane checks the sharing tenant's plan.
It strips any `whiteLabel` branding from the resumed session unless the tenant is on `enterprise`.
The flow itself still works, and falls back to default, unbranded styling.
See [Widget SDK reference](/docs/widget-sdk/reference#white-label-configuration).

> **Not included, despite older copy:** **SSO** and **SCIM** are *not* built, and no tier grants them.
> No `/scim/v2/*` endpoint exists.
> The console's authentication supports email and password, optional Google and GitHub sign-in, and WebAuthn passkeys.
> There is no SAML or OIDC provider.
> **Monitor** is DNS *record drift* detection only.
> Nothing probes a domain or origin for reachability, status code or latency, so there is no uptime monitoring.
> Drift alerts are also off unless the deployment sets `MONITOR_ALERTS_ENABLED` (see [Webhooks](/docs/webhooks/overview)).

## Subscriptions and trials

14 day free trial. Your first ten domains connect free, before you pick a plan. No card needed to sign up.

Every workspace needs an active subscription, or a free trial of one, to connect **new** domains.
It also needs one to hold **new credentials**: creating an API key, creating an application, and rotating an application's secret.
The one exception is a new workspace's **first ten domains**, which are free (see [The first ten domains are free](#the-first-ten-domains-are-free)).
Billing never touches domains that are already connected.
Live traffic, certificate renewals, rechecks and monitoring carry on whatever the billing state.
Credentials that already exist keep working.

- **A new workspace** can look around first.
  Signing up does not require a card.
  You can check domains, design and preview your widget, and read the docs without one.
  Your first ten domains also connect without one.
  You can start a plan from the console whenever you are ready, and you can skip the offer.
  We ask for the card where it is needed: when you create an API key, create an application, or connect an eleventh domain.
  That starts a **14 day free trial** in a checkout on the page itself, not a pop-up.
  The order summary, the total and the buttons are the console's own, in light or dark.
  Only the card fields are Stripe's.
  The first charge happens when the trial ends, and canceling before then costs nothing.
  Until the trial starts, new credentials are refused, and so is every connection after the first.
- **Promotion codes** go in the order summary of the checkout.
  You can also send one as `promotion_code` when you start a checkout yourself (`POST /v1/billing/checkout`).
  Or apply one to a running subscription with `POST /v1/billing/promotion-code`.
  Both accept only codes that belong to this deployment: made through the operator console, or limited to its products in Stripe.
  They answer `404` for any other code on a shared Stripe account, the same as for a code that does not exist.
  So a code cannot be told apart from a stranger's.
  Stripe's own code field is never shown on the checkout.
  A checkout started with a code carries it as its one discount.
  A Checkout Session cannot be given a discount after it is created.
  So adding or taking off a code in the order summary starts a new checkout in its place.
  The card fields of the old one are empty again.
  Neither charges anything until the payment is confirmed.
- **Changing plans** on a running subscription is
  `POST /v1/billing/change-plan`; Stripe prorates the difference. During a
  trial, the new price applies from the trial's end.
- **Card updates and cancellation** happen in the Stripe Billing Portal
  (`POST /v1/billing/portal`, with `flow: "payment_method_update"` or
  `"subscription_cancel"` to open straight at that task).

### Billing states

`GET /v1/billing/status` (owners and admins) evaluates the workspace into one
of eight states. Only the last two refuse new connections.

| State | Meaning | New connections |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `trialing` | In the free trial of a paid plan; the first charge is at `trial_ends_at`. | allowed |
| `active` | A paid subscription in good standing. | allowed |
| `past_due` | A payment failed and Stripe is retrying it. Owners and admins are asked to update the card; nothing else changes. | allowed |
| `comped` | Complimentary access granted by CustomDomain™ (until `comped_until`, or with no end). | allowed |
| `grace` | A workspace that was on Free, inside its 14 day grace period. | allowed |
| `legacy_free` | A Free workspace whose grace has not started yet. | allowed |
| `blocked` | No subscription after the trial, grace or comp (`reason`: `setup_required`, `grace_ended`, `comp_ended`), or a subscription Stripe gave up on after its retries (`unpaid`). | **refused** (except a new workspace's first ten domains) |
| `canceled` | The subscription ended. | **refused** |

`GET /v1/billing/status` also returns `payment_method`: what the workspace is charged on, or `null` when there is none.
`type` is `card`, `link` or `cashapp`.
A card has `brand`, `last4`, `exp_month` and `exp_year`.
It also has `wallet` (`apple_pay` or `google_pay`) when it was paid through a wallet.
A Link account has the account's `email`, and the card behind it is never shown.
Cash App Pay has only its type.
Any of them counts as a payment method on file.
So a workspace that paid with Link is not shown "No card on file", and is not refused by the card check on agent purchases.
Checkout opens with the owner's email already filled in.

### What a refusal looks like

The control plane refuses at one place, before a connection is created.
That covers every surface that can start one: the console, the API, bulk connect, the MCP server, the embedded widget and the start of a domain purchase.
Three more calls are refused the same way while a workspace has no trial or subscription.
They are creating an API key (`POST /v1/applications/{id}/keys`) and creating an application (`POST /v1/applications` with an API key).
The third is rotating an application's secret (`POST /v1/applications/{id}/client-secret:rotate`).
So a widget you embed never meets a billing refusal in front of your own customers.
Reading, listing, checking domains and the billing routes themselves stay open.

- The workspace itself (an API key, an agent token, an app-scoped token) gets
  `402 billing_required`, with the reason and where to fix it in `details`.
- The tenant's **end users** in the embedded widget get
  `402 connections_unavailable` and a neutral sentence that says nothing about
  the tenant's plan or billing. The widget maps both to `PlanLimitError` on its
  `customdomain:error` event.

The switch is `BILLING_REQUIRE_SUBSCRIPTION` (default `1`). With it set to `0`,
or on a deployment where no Starter price is configured (so nobody could
subscribe), nothing is refused. The check fails open on a lookup error.

### The first ten domains are free

A brand new workspace has never had a subscription or a trial.
It connects its **first ten** domains without a card, from the console, the API, the MCP server or the embedded widget.
So you can see your domains work before we ask for one.
While the workspace has fewer than ten pending, propagating or live connections, a new connection goes through.
So does the start of a domain purchase.
A connection that failed does not count.
Posting the same domain again returns it with `200`, as it always did.
Removing a domain frees its place at once.
The eleventh domain is refused with `402 billing_required`, whose `title` says a card is needed to connect more domains.
Creating an API key or an application still needs the trial.

`GET /v1/billing/notice` and `GET /v1/billing/status` carry `first_domain_free: true` for such a workspace, for owners and admins only.
They add `free_domains`, how many domains it may connect before a card (ten).
While a free domain is left, `restricted` is `false`, and the console says "Your first 10 domains are free".
Once all of them are connected, `restricted` is `true`, and the notice says a card is for more domains.

The console asks owners and admins for a card only once every free place is used.
With one to nine of the ten connected, nobody is sent to the setup flow and no card prompt appears.
You keep connecting.
Once the tenth is connected, the live card and the domain page say a card is for more domains.
The setup flow is offered once, the next time the console loads after the tenth is live, not while it is still connecting.
Skipping it sticks, and invited teammates are never sent there.
A billing read that fails never keeps anyone out of the console.
With an allowance of one, all of this happens after that one domain, as before.
`GET /v1/billing/standing` says the same to an agent or a server holding an API key.

A domain that was a typo holds a free place until it is removed.
Remove it from Domains, which frees its place at once.
When a workspace's allowance is one, the Connect page also offers a shortcut.
It removes the one domain still waiting for its records and connects the new one in the same step.

Support can set a different number of free domains for a workspace, from 1 to 50.
The same reads then say how many ("Your first 3 domains are free").
Setting it back to `0` returns the workspace to the default of ten.
It changes nothing for a workspace the rule does not cover.

The free domains stay as long as the workspace has no subscription, because billing never touches a live domain.
The rule never applies to a workspace that had a trial, a subscription, a comp or the Free plan.
Its switch is `BILLING_FIRST_DOMAIN_FREE`, which defaults to `1`.
With it set to `0`, or switched off at runtime, a new workspace is refused its first connection, exactly as before.

### The Free plan's retirement

A workspace that was on Free keeps its 10 domains a year while it moves:

1. **The grace starts** the first time an owner or admin signs in to the console after the change.
   Or it starts at the workspace's first connection attempt, whichever comes first.
   Starting it at deploy time would have run the clock down for workspaces nobody had looked at.
   Owners and admins are emailed when it starts.
2. **For 14 days** everything keeps working.
   Owners and admins see the setup flow when they sign in to their own workspace, with a "Not now" for the rest of that session.
   Someone who joined the workspace by invite gets the banner instead.
   They also see a banner on every console page, and get email reminders with 7, 3 and 1 days left.
3. **Subscribing during the grace costs nothing before the grace ends:** the
   subscription starts with a trial that runs exactly to the grace's end.
4. **After the grace**, without a subscription, new connections are refused.
   Live domains keep serving.

### Payment failures

A failed payment moves the subscription to `past_due`.
Owners and admins get an email with a link to update the card in the Billing Portal, and a banner.
Stripe retries on its own schedule, and nothing is refused during the retries.
If every retry fails, the Stripe account's setting decides what happens next ("if all retries for a payment fail").
**Cancel the subscription** and **mark it unpaid** both end in new connections being refused.
The states are `canceled`, or `blocked` with `reason: "unpaid"`, and an email comes first.
Leaving it past due would never restrict anything.
You fix an unpaid subscription with a working card, which pays the open invoice.
A second checkout is refused (`409`).

### When a paid plan ends

When a paid subscription ends (`canceled`, or `unpaid` after its retries), the
workspace's connected domains keep serving for **30 days**. Owners and admins
are emailed when the 30 days start, with the date, and again 7 days and 1 day
before. After that the domains show a neutral hold page until a paid plan is
active again, and then they serve again at once.

- A `past_due` plan (Stripe still retrying), a running trial, a workspace that
  never subscribed and a comped workspace never lapse.
- The hold page is a plain `503` that says "This site is temporarily unavailable."
  It carries no brand and no link, asks search engines not to index it, and sets `Retry-After` to an hour.
  Certificates keep renewing, so the domain works the moment the plan is back.
- A workspace is never held before its owners got the first email.

### Who sees billing

Billing belongs to a workspace's **owners and admins** (`billing:manage`).
The billing routes answer members, viewers and widget tokens with `403`.
Those routes are status, invoices, checkout, portal, plan changes, promotion codes and the grace start.
The console shows members and viewers a neutral note ("ask an owner or admin") instead of the billing page.
Two reads stay open to every member.
`GET /v1/billing/usage` gives them the counts without the billing fields.
`GET /v1/billing/notice` tells them only whether billing needs attention and whether new connections are paused.
It never shows the plan, dates or amounts.

### Billing emails

Owners and admins of the workspace get these, each exactly once:

| Email | When |
| --- | --- |
| Trial started | The trial begins (Checkout completed). |
| Trial ending | 3 days before the first charge. |
| Payment failed | A charge fails; links to the card update in the Billing Portal. |
| Free plan ending | The grace starts, and again with 7, 3 and 1 days left. |
| New connections paused | The workspace becomes restricted (grace or comp ended, or unpaid after retries). |
| Subscription active again | A restricted or past due workspace is back in good standing. |
| Subscription canceled | The subscription ends. Once [lapsed plans](#when-a-paid-plan-ends) are on, the first lapse email below goes instead. |
| Domains pause on a date | A paid plan ended: the 30 days start. It gives the date the domains pause. |
| Domains pause soon | 7 days and 1 day before the domains pause. |
| Domains paused | The 30 days are over and the domains show the hold page. |

## Metered usage

`GET /v1/billing/usage` reports the caller tenant's metered **domain
connections** for the period against the plan's included quota. The tenant is
derived from the authenticated credential, never a query parameter, and
`?period=` defaults to the current UTC calendar month.

<OpenInConsole to="/app/usage" label="Open Usage in console" />

```json
{
  "org": "ten_1a2b3c",
  "period": "2026-07",
  "used": 412,
  "mode": "live",
  "plan": "growth",
  "plan_name": "Growth",
  "quota": 50,
  "limit": 50,
  "quota_used": 412,
  "quota_window_months": 1,
  "metered": true,
  "hard_cap": false,
  "status": "active",
  "billing_state": "active",
  "quota_state": "at_limit",
  "quota_enforced": false,
  "remaining": 0,
  "has_billing_account": true
}
```

`used` is this month's count.
Compare **`quota_used`** against **`quota`**.
`quota_used` is the count over the last `quota_window_months` months.
That's 12 on Starter, so this month plus the 11 before it, and 1 on every other tier, where it equals `used`.
`quota` is the plan's allowance over that window, and `0` means unlimited.
`limit` is an alias carrying the same value.
The published schema always declared `limit`, while the handler only ever emitted `quota`.
So both are sent now, and you can read either.
`status`, `has_billing_account` and `billing_state` are billing data.
They are only sent to a credential that holds `billing:manage`.

`quota_state` is the server's own verdict: `ok`, `approaching` (at or past 80%
of quota) or `at_limit`. `remaining` is `quota - quota_used` floored at zero,
omitted entirely on an unlimited plan.

`hard_cap` is the plan's refusal policy, and it is `true` only on `starter`.
`quota_enforced` reports whether a `402` is actually possible **right now**.
It needs `hard_cap` *and* the deployment's `BILLING_ENFORCE_QUOTA`, and a client cannot see the second.
On every larger tier, `quota_enforced` is `false` at any usage.
The example above is a `growth` tenant at 412 used against a quota of 50.
It reads `at_limit` and `quota_enforced: false`, and nothing is refused.
Treat `at_limit` as "over the line", never as "blocked", and check `quota_enforced` for that.

### Overage

**No plan above Starter is ever refused a connection at quota.**
On `startup`, `growth`, `premium` and `enterprise`, a connection past the monthly quota is allowed.
It is recorded as overage and surfaced, never blocked.
`starter` is the only tier that returns `402 quota_exceeded` at its quota, where the deployment enforces quotas at all.

The reason is the blast radius of the refusal, not generosity.
The quota gate sits on the connection path that the **embedded widget** calls with an end user credential.
So a `402` does not land on the tenant's admin.
It lands on *their* end user, mid-connect, inside the customer's own signup funnel.
Breaking a paying customer's funnel to collect overage is a bad trade at any quota.
It is worse here, because **no code path has ever reported usage to Stripe**.
The Stripe client has no usage record or meter event call at all.
So the debt being enforced was never billed in the first place.

Overage is recorded and surfaced instead.
`/v1/billing/usage` reports `quota_state` and `remaining`.
The console warns at 80%, and again past quota.
The usage band feeds the CRM, so the account team can right-size the plan.
An overage becomes a conversation, not an outage.

`metered` on `growth`/`enterprise` describes how a tier is *priced* and carries
no enforcement meaning at all. Read **`hard_cap`** for a plan's refusal policy.

> **Retraction.** An earlier revision of this page said every tier was hard capped.
> It promised metered tiers would only stop blocking *in the same change that started charging for overage*.
> **That promise is withdrawn.**
> Paid tiers stopped blocking, and no charging was introduced.
> The change is strictly in the customer's favour.
> That's why it shipped without the notice period that promise described.
> If usage based billing is ever built, it will be announced before it takes effect.
> That would use Stripe Billing Meters, since the legacy Usage Records API is deprecated.

## Checkout and portal [#checkout--portal]

<OpenInConsole to="/app/billing" label="Open Billing in console" />

```bash
# Start a Stripe Checkout for a self-serve plan (a new workspace gets its 14 day trial)
curl -X POST https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/billing/checkout \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <KEY>" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"plan":"starter","success_url":"https://app.acme.example/ok","cancel_url":"https://app.acme.example/cancel"}'
# → { "id": "cs_...", "url": "https://checkout.stripe.com/...", "trial_days": 14, "first_charge_at": "..." }

# Open the Stripe Billing Portal at the card update (requires a prior checkout)
curl -X POST https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/billing/portal \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <KEY>" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"return_url":"https://app.acme.example/billing","flow":"payment_method_update"}'
```

Pass `return_url` instead of `success_url` and `cancel_url` for Stripe
**Embedded** Checkout; the response then carries a `client_secret` to mount.
When billing is not available, these routes return `503 service_unavailable`
while the plan catalog stays public. A workspace that already has a running subscription is
refused a second checkout with `409`. Enforcement is on the control plane's
write path only, never on the edge `ask` hot path, so serving traffic never
blocks on billing.

`promotion_code` is optional: the code as a customer types it.
It holds at most 64 characters, and never a Stripe `promo_` id.
The control plane checks it before asking Stripe for a session.
It takes the code only when it is one of this deployment's own (see Promotion codes above), and makes it the session's one discount.
A code that does not exist, ran out, or belongs to another product on the same Stripe account is a `404`, and no session is created.
A code of this deployment that Stripe will not give this order is a `400`.
Examples are a first order only code, or a minimum amount.
Stripe's own code field is never switched on, and a session cannot be given a discount after it is created.
So to change the code, start the checkout again with the new one (or none), and mount the new `client_secret`.

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/billing/checkout \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <KEY>" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"plan":"starter","return_url":"https://app.acme.example/done","promotion_code":"LAUNCH50"}'
```

`ui_mode` is optional and picks how the session is shown.
Leave it out, or send `embedded`, and nothing changes.
`null` and an empty string count as leaving it out.
You then get Stripe Embedded Checkout, an iframe Stripe styles itself, when `return_url` is set.
You get the hosted redirect when only `success_url` and `cancel_url` are set.
`elements` is Elements with the Checkout Sessions API.
You render the whole page: the plan, the price, the trial, the promotion code box and the totals.
You mount only Stripe's Payment Element with the returned `client_secret`, so the page can wear your own theme.
It needs `return_url`.
An `elements` session is created on Stripe API version `2026-08-26.dahlia`, pinned on that one request.
This is the one place the exact version is written down; the API reference says only that it is the dahlia release train.
So mount the session with a Stripe.js of the dahlia train.
Every response carries `ui_mode`, the mode the session really has: `embedded`, `elements`, or `hosted` for the redirect flow.
An `elements` request answers `embedded` when Stripe cannot create an Elements session.
So read the response before you mount anything.
`hosted` is only ever an answer.
A client that keeps the response's `ui_mode` must not send it back, because any value other than `embedded` and `elements` is a `400`.
The same field works on `POST /v1/billing/setup` (save a card) and `POST /v1/registrar/checkout` (buy a domain).

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/billing/checkout \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <KEY>" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"plan":"starter","return_url":"https://app.acme.example/billing/return?session_id={CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}","ui_mode":"elements"}'
# → { "id": "cs_...", "client_secret": "cs_..._secret_...", "ui_mode": "elements", "trial_days": 14, "first_charge_at": "..." }
```

### When the subscription is recorded

**Starting a checkout grants nothing.**
`POST /v1/billing/checkout` validates the tier.
It rejects unknown ids, the retired `free` and the two contact-sales tiers with `400`.
Then it opens a Stripe session stamped with the tenant and tier.
The subscription is recorded only from a session that reads `status: "complete"` **and** `payment_status: "paid"`.
A trial reads `no_payment_required`, which also counts.
Two things can confirm it.
One is Stripe's signature-verified `checkout.session.completed` event.
The other is `POST /v1/billing/checkout:confirm`, which the console calls when Stripe returns the browser, and which reads the session from Stripe itself.
An abandoned, expired or unpaid session leaves the tenant exactly where it was.

`customer.subscription.*` events apply Stripe's subscription (its status, trial
end, period end, price and cancellation) to the workspace, and the state above
follows from it.
