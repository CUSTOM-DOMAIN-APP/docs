## Plans

The public catalog is returned by `GET /v1/plans` (along with a
`billing: { configured, mode, trial_days }` block).

Entitlements gate which products a tenant may use. **Enforced today:** `Power`
(the `/v1/connections/{id}/power*` surface) and `Secure` (the `/v1/ssl*`
surface); an unentitled tenant gets `402 plan_upgrade_required`. `WhiteLabel`
(widget branding) is enforced by dropping the branding, as described below.
`Connect` is included on every plan and needs no gate. `Monitor` is not gated per tenant yet; the deployment-level
`MONITOR_ALERTS_ENABLED` flag governs it for everyone.

| Plan | Price | Domains / year (sold) | Domains / month | Refused at quota? | Entitlements | Motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `starter` | $10/mo | 10 | 1 | **yes** | Connect | self-serve, 14 day free trial |
| `startup` | $149/mo | 600 | 50 | no | Connect | self-serve, 14 day free trial |
| `growth` | $649/mo | 600 | 50 | no | Connect · Power · Secure/SSL | self-serve, 14 day free trial |
| `premium` | demo-gated | 12,000 | 1,000 | no | + Monitor (preview) | contact sales |
| `enterprise` | custom | 12,000+ | 1,000 | no | + white-label | contact sales |

Prices and both domain columns above are transcribed from `GET /v1/plans`
(`price_cents`, `domains_per_year`, `domains_per_month`). If this table and that
endpoint ever disagree, the endpoint is right and this page is stale.

**Starter replaced the Free plan on 2026-09-29.** `free` is retired: no new
workspace is put on it, `GET /v1/plans` no longer lists it and Checkout refuses
it. Workspaces that were on Free keep working while they move to a paid plan;
see [The Free plan's retirement](#the-free-plans-retirement).

### Two numbers, and which one gates you

A tier is **sold** annually but **metered** monthly: the meter counts domain
connections per UTC calendar month, and the quota gate compares against
`domains_per_month`, which is `ceil(domains_per_year / 12)`. Startup's 600/year
is therefore **50 in any one month**, and Starter's 10/year is **1/month**.
Ceiling division means the annualized entitlement never shrinks below what was
sold (Starter's 1/month is 12/year effective).

> **Whether that monthly number *blocks* takes both a plan property and a
> deployment switch.** The plan property is `hard_cap`, and it is `true` only on
> `starter` (and the retired `free`); see [Overage](#overage) for why no larger
> tier is ever refused. The switch is `BILLING_ENFORCE_QUOTA`:
> `infra/docker-compose.prod.yml` ships it defaulted to `0`, so the hosted
> service runs meter only and refuses nothing at quota; `1` turns enforcement
> on. Both must hold. The gate also fails **open** by design even when enabled:
> a billing lookup error allows the connect rather than blocking a customer
> during a billing outage. The gate is `enforceQuota` in
> `services/control-plane/internal/api/billing.go`; the billing service applies
> the same `hard_cap` rule in `ConnectDomain`, so the two cannot disagree.

Both numbers are published on `GET /v1/plans` (`domains_per_year` and
`domains_per_month`); `GET /v1/billing/usage`'s `quota` is the monthly one,
because `used` is a monthly count.

Self-serve plans (`starter`, `startup`, `growth`) are bought through Stripe
Checkout. `premium` and `enterprise` route to a "book a demo" CTA and have no
public price.

**White-label is enforced server-side, not just hidden in the console.** A
direct widget `open()` always applies whatever branding you configure: that's
your own client-side config, and the control plane never sees it. But when a
connect is forwarded through a share link and a teammate resumes it
(`loadSharedFlow`), the control plane checks the sharing tenant's plan and
strips any `whiteLabel` branding from the resumed session unless it's on
`enterprise`; the flow itself still works, it just falls back to default,
unbranded styling. See
[Widget SDK reference](/docs/widget-sdk/reference#white-label-configuration).

> **Not included, despite older copy:** **SSO** and **SCIM** are *not* built and
> no tier grants them. SCIM's `/scim/v2/*` surface is deliberately unmounted, and
> the console's authentication supports email/password, optional Google and GitHub
> sign-in, and WebAuthn passkeys; there is no SAML/OIDC provider. **Monitor** is DNS
> *record drift* detection only; nothing probes a domain or origin for
> reachability, status code or latency, so there is no uptime monitoring. Drift
> alerts are additionally off unless the deployment sets
> `MONITOR_ALERTS_ENABLED`; see [Webhooks](/docs/webhooks/overview).

## Subscriptions and trials

Every workspace needs an active subscription (or a free trial of one) to
connect **new** domains and to hold **new credentials**: creating an API key,
creating an application and rotating an application's secret. The one exception
is a new workspace's **first ten domains**, which are free (see
[The first ten domains are free](#the-first-ten-domains-are-free)). Domains that are
already connected are never touched by billing: live traffic, certificate
renewals, rechecks and monitoring carry on whatever the billing state, and
credentials that already exist keep working.

- **A new workspace** can look around first. Signing up does not require a card:
  you can check domains, design and preview your widget and read the docs without
  one, and your first ten domains connect without one. The setup flow is offered
  once, after all ten are connected and the tenth is live, and skipping it sticks.
  The card is asked for where it is needed, when you create an API key, create an
  application or connect an eleventh domain, and that starts a **14 day free trial** in a checkout
  on the page itself, not a pop-up. The order summary, the total and the buttons
  are the console's own, in light or dark; only the card fields are Stripe's.
  The first charge happens when the trial ends; canceling before then costs
  nothing. Until the trial starts, new credentials are refused, and so is every
  connection after the first.
- **Promotion codes** are typed into the order summary of the checkout, or sent
  as `promotion_code` when you start one yourself
  (`POST /v1/billing/checkout`), or applied to a running subscription with
  `POST /v1/billing/promotion-code`. Both accept only codes that belong to this
  deployment (made through the operator console, or limited to its products in
  Stripe) and answer `404` for any other code on a shared Stripe account, the
  same as for a code that does not exist, so a code cannot be told apart from a
  stranger's. Stripe's own code field is never shown on the checkout. A
  checkout started with a code carries it as its one discount, and a Checkout
  Session cannot be given a discount after it is created, so adding or taking
  off a code in the order summary starts a new checkout in its place: the card
  fields of the old one are empty again, and nothing is charged by either until
  the payment is confirmed.
- **Changing plans** on a running subscription is
  `POST /v1/billing/change-plan`; Stripe prorates the difference. During a
  trial, the new price applies from the trial's end.
- **Card updates and cancellation** happen in the Stripe Billing Portal
  (`POST /v1/billing/portal`, with `flow: "payment_method_update"` or
  `"subscription_cancel"` to open straight at that task).

### Billing states

`GET /v1/billing/status` (owners and admins) evaluates the workspace into one
of eight states. Only the last two refuse new connections.

| State | Meaning | New connections |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `trialing` | In the free trial of a paid plan; the first charge is at `trial_ends_at`. | allowed |
| `active` | A paid subscription in good standing. | allowed |
| `past_due` | A payment failed and Stripe is retrying it. Owners and admins are asked to update the card; nothing else changes. | allowed |
| `comped` | Complimentary access granted by CustomDomain™ (until `comped_until`, or with no end). | allowed |
| `grace` | A workspace that was on Free, inside its 14 day grace period. | allowed |
| `legacy_free` | A Free workspace whose grace has not started yet. | allowed |
| `blocked` | No subscription after the trial, grace or comp (`reason`: `setup_required`, `grace_ended`, `comp_ended`), or a subscription Stripe gave up on after its retries (`unpaid`). | **refused** (except a new workspace's first ten domains) |
| `canceled` | The subscription ended. | **refused** |

`GET /v1/billing/status` also returns `payment_method`, what the workspace is
charged on, or `null` when there is none. `type` is `card`, `link` or `cashapp`.
A card has `brand`, `last4`, `exp_month` and `exp_year`, and `wallet`
(`apple_pay` or `google_pay`) when it was paid through a wallet. A Link account
has the `email` of the account (the card behind it is never shown), and Cash App
Pay has only its type. Any of them counts as a payment method on file, so a
workspace that paid with Link is not shown "No card on file" and is not refused
by the card check on agent purchases. Checkout opens with the owner's email
already filled in.

### What a refusal looks like

The control plane refuses at one place, before a connection is created, for
every surface that can start one: the console, the API, bulk connect, the MCP
server, the embedded widget and the start of a domain purchase. Creating an API
key (`POST /v1/applications/{id}/keys`), creating an application
(`POST /v1/applications` with an API key) and rotating an application's secret
(`POST /v1/applications/{id}/client-secret:rotate`) are refused the same way
while a workspace has no trial or subscription, so a widget you embed never
meets a billing refusal in front of your own customers. Reading, listing, checking
domains and the billing routes themselves stay open.

- The workspace itself (an API key, an agent token, an app-scoped token) gets
  `402 billing_required`, with the reason and where to fix it in `details`.
- The tenant's **end users** in the embedded widget get
  `402 connections_unavailable` and a neutral sentence that says nothing about
  the tenant's plan or billing. The widget maps both to `PlanLimitError` on its
  `customdomain:error` event.

The switch is `BILLING_REQUIRE_SUBSCRIPTION` (default `1`). With it set to `0`,
or on a deployment where no Starter price is configured (so nobody could
subscribe), nothing is refused. The check fails open on a lookup error.

### The first ten domains are free

A brand new workspace, one that has never had a subscription or a trial,
connects its **first ten** domains without a card, whether it connects from the
console, the API, the MCP server or the embedded widget, so you can see your
domains work before you are asked for one. While the workspace has fewer than ten
pending, propagating or live connections, a new connection (and the start of a
domain purchase) goes through. A connection that failed does not count, posting
the same domain again returns it with `200`, as it always did, and removing a
domain frees its place at once. The eleventh domain is refused with
`402 billing_required`, whose `title` says a card is needed to connect more
domains. Creating an API key or an application still needs the trial.

`GET /v1/billing/notice` and `GET /v1/billing/status` carry
`first_domain_free: true` for such a workspace (owners and admins only), with
`free_domains`, how many domains it may connect before a card (ten). While a
free domain is left `restricted` is `false` and the console says "Your first 10
domains are free"; once all of them are connected `restricted` is `true` and the
notice says a card is for more domains.

The console asks owners and admins for a card only once every free place is used.
With one to nine of the ten connected nobody is sent to the setup flow and no
card prompt appears; you keep connecting. Once the tenth is connected, the live
card and the domain page say a card is for more domains, and the setup flow is
offered once, the next time the console loads after the tenth is live (not while it is still
connecting). Skipping it sticks, invited teammates are never sent there, and a
billing read that fails never keeps anyone out of the console. With an allowance
of one, all of this happens after that one domain, as before.
`GET /v1/billing/standing` says the same to an agent or a server holding an API key.

A domain that was a typo holds a free place until it is removed: remove it from
Domains, which frees its place at once. (When a workspace's allowance is one, the
Connect page also offers to remove the one domain still waiting for its records
and connect the new one in the same step.)

Support can set a different number of free domains for a workspace, from 1 to 50,
and the same reads then say how many ("Your first 3 domains are free"); setting
it back to `0` returns the workspace to the default of ten. It changes nothing for
a workspace the rule does not cover.

The free domains stay for as long as the workspace has no subscription, because
billing never touches a live domain. The rule never applies to a workspace that
had a trial, a subscription, a comp or the Free plan. Its switch is
`BILLING_FIRST_DOMAIN_FREE` (default `1`); with it set to `0`, or switched off
at runtime, a new workspace is refused its first connection exactly as it was
before.

### The Free plan's retirement

A workspace that was on Free keeps its 10 domains a year while it moves:

1. **The grace starts** the first time an owner or admin signs in to the
   console after the change, or at the workspace's first connection attempt,
   whichever comes first. (Starting it at deploy time would have run the clock
   down for workspaces nobody had looked at.) Owners and admins are emailed
   when it starts.
2. **For 14 days** everything keeps working. Owners and admins see the setup
   flow when they sign in to their own workspace (with a "Not now" for the rest
   of that session; someone who joined the workspace by invite gets the banner
   instead), a banner on every console page, and email reminders with 7, 3 and
   1 days left.
3. **Subscribing during the grace costs nothing before the grace ends:** the
   subscription starts with a trial that runs exactly to the grace's end.
4. **After the grace**, without a subscription, new connections are refused.
   Live domains keep serving.

### Payment failures

A failed payment moves the subscription to `past_due`: owners and admins get an
email with a link to update the card in the Billing Portal, and a banner, while
Stripe retries on its own schedule. Nothing is refused during the retries. If
every retry fails, what happens next is the Stripe account's setting
("if all retries for a payment fail"): **cancel the subscription** or **mark it
unpaid** both end in new connections being refused (`canceled`, or `blocked`
with `reason: "unpaid"`), with an email first. Leaving it past due would never
restrict anything. An unpaid subscription is fixed with a working card, which
pays the open invoice; a second checkout is refused (`409`).

### Who sees billing

Billing belongs to a workspace's **owners and admins** (`billing:manage`). The
billing routes (status, invoices, checkout, portal, plan changes, promotion
codes, the grace start) answer members, viewers and widget tokens with `403`,
and the console shows members and viewers a neutral note ("ask an owner or
admin") instead of the billing page. Two reads stay open to every member:
`GET /v1/billing/usage` gives them the counts without the billing fields, and
`GET /v1/billing/notice` tells them only whether billing needs attention and
whether new connections are paused, never the plan, dates or amounts.

### Billing emails

Owners and admins of the workspace get these, each exactly once:

| Email | When |
| --- | --- |
| Trial started | The trial begins (Checkout completed). |
| Trial ending | 3 days before the first charge. |
| Payment failed | A charge fails; links to the card update in the Billing Portal. |
| Free plan ending | The grace starts, and again with 7, 3 and 1 days left. |
| New connections paused | The workspace becomes restricted (grace or comp ended, or unpaid after retries). |
| Subscription active again | A restricted or past due workspace is back in good standing. |
| Subscription canceled | The subscription ends. |

## Metered usage

`GET /v1/billing/usage` reports the caller tenant's metered **domain
connections** for the period against the plan's included quota. The tenant is
derived from the authenticated credential, never a query parameter, and
`?period=` defaults to the current UTC calendar month.

```json
{
  "org": "ten_1a2b3c",
  "period": "2026-07",
  "used": 412,
  "mode": "live",
  "plan": "growth",
  "plan_name": "Growth",
  "quota": 50,
  "limit": 50,
  "metered": true,
  "hard_cap": false,
  "status": "active",
  "billing_state": "active",
  "quota_state": "at_limit",
  "quota_enforced": false,
  "remaining": 0,
  "has_billing_account": true
}
```

Compare `used` against **`quota`**: the plan's included connections for one
calendar month, i.e. `domains_per_month` from the catalog (`0` means unlimited).
`limit` is an alias carrying the identical value: the published schema had
always declared `limit` while the handler only ever emitted `quota`, so both are
sent now and either may be read. `status`, `has_billing_account` and
`billing_state` are billing data and are only sent to a credential that holds
`billing:manage`.

`quota_state` is the server's own verdict: `ok`, `approaching` (at or past 80%
of quota) or `at_limit`. `remaining` is `quota - used` floored at zero, omitted
entirely on an unlimited plan.

`hard_cap` is the plan's refusal policy (`true` only on `starter`), and
`quota_enforced` reports whether a `402` is actually possible **right now**: it
needs `hard_cap` *and* the deployment's `BILLING_ENFORCE_QUOTA`, the second of
which a client cannot see. On every larger tier `quota_enforced` is `false` at
any usage. The example above is a `growth` tenant at 412 used against a quota of
50: `at_limit`, `quota_enforced: false`, and nothing refused. Treat `at_limit`
as "over the line", never as "blocked"; check `quota_enforced` for that.

### Overage

**No plan above Starter is ever refused a connection at quota.** On `startup`,
`growth`, `premium` and `enterprise`, a connection past the monthly quota is
allowed, recorded as overage, and surfaced, never blocked. `starter` is the only
tier that returns `402 quota_exceeded` at its quota (where the deployment
enforces quotas at all).

The reason is the blast radius of the refusal, not generosity. The quota gate
sits on the connection path that the **embedded widget** calls with an end user
credential, so a `402` does not land on the tenant's admin; it lands on *their*
end user, mid-connect, inside the customer's own signup funnel. Breaking a
paying customer's funnel to collect overage is a bad trade at any quota, and
doubly so here because **no code path has ever reported usage to Stripe**: the
Stripe client has no usage record or meter event call at all, so the debt being
enforced was never billed in the first place.

Overage is instead recorded and surfaced: `/v1/billing/usage` reports
`quota_state` and `remaining`, the console warns at 80% and again past quota,
and the usage band feeds the CRM so the account team can right-size the plan.
An overage becomes a conversation, not an outage.

`metered` on `growth`/`enterprise` describes how a tier is *priced* and carries
no enforcement meaning at all. Read **`hard_cap`** for a plan's refusal policy.

> **Retraction.** An earlier revision of this page said every tier was hard
> capped, and promised that metered tiers would only stop blocking *in the same
> change that started charging for overage*. **That promise is withdrawn.** Paid
> tiers stopped blocking, and no charging was introduced: the change is strictly
> in the customer's favour, which is why it shipped without the notice period
> that promise described. If usage based billing is ever built (Stripe Billing
> Meters; the legacy Usage Records API is deprecated), it will be announced
> before it takes effect.

## Checkout & portal

```bash
# Start a Stripe Checkout for a self-serve plan (a new workspace gets its 14 day trial)
curl -X POST https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/billing/checkout \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <KEY>" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"plan":"starter","success_url":"https://app.acme.example/ok","cancel_url":"https://app.acme.example/cancel"}'
# → { "id": "cs_...", "url": "https://checkout.stripe.com/...", "trial_days": 14, "first_charge_at": "..." }

# Open the Stripe Billing Portal at the card update (requires a prior checkout)
curl -X POST https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/billing/portal \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <KEY>" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"return_url":"https://app.acme.example/billing","flow":"payment_method_update"}'
```

Pass `return_url` instead of `success_url` and `cancel_url` for Stripe
**Embedded** Checkout; the response then carries a `client_secret` to mount.
Checkout and the portal require `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and the `STRIPE_PRICE_*`
ids (`STRIPE_PRICE_STARTER`, `STRIPE_PRICE_STARTUP`, `STRIPE_PRICE_GROWTH`);
without them these routes return `503 service_unavailable` while the plan
catalog stays public. A workspace that already has a running subscription is
refused a second checkout with `409`. Enforcement is on the control plane's
write path only, never on the edge `ask` hot path, so serving traffic never
blocks on billing.

`promotion_code` is optional: the code as a customer types it (at most 64
characters, never a Stripe `promo_` id). The control plane checks it before
Stripe is asked for a session, takes it only when it is one of this
deployment's own (see Promotion codes above), and makes it the session's one
discount. A code that does not exist, ran out, or belongs to another product on
the same Stripe account is a `404`, and no session is created; a code of this
deployment that Stripe will not give this order (a first order only code, a
minimum amount) is a `400`. Stripe's own code field is never switched on, and a
session cannot be given a discount after it is created, so to change the code,
start the checkout again with the new one (or none) and mount the new
`client_secret`.

```bash
curl -X POST http://localhost:8080/v1/billing/checkout \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <KEY>" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"plan":"starter","return_url":"https://app.acme.example/done","promotion_code":"LAUNCH50"}'
```

`ui_mode` is optional and picks how the session is shown. Leave it out (or send
`embedded`; `null` and an empty string count as leaving it out) and nothing
changes: Stripe Embedded Checkout, an iframe Stripe styles itself, when
`return_url` is set, and the hosted redirect when only `success_url` and
`cancel_url` are. `elements` is Elements with the Checkout Sessions API: you
render the whole page (the plan, the price, the trial, the promotion code box,
the totals) and mount only Stripe's Payment Element with the returned
`client_secret`, so the page can wear your own theme. It needs `return_url`. An
`elements` session is created on Stripe API version `2026-08-26.dahlia`, pinned
on that one request. This is the one place the exact version is written down
(the API reference says only that it is the dahlia release train), so mount the
session with a Stripe.js of the dahlia train. Every response carries `ui_mode`,
the mode the session really has: `embedded`, `elements`, or `hosted` for the
redirect flow. An `elements` request answers `embedded` when Stripe cannot
create an Elements session, so read the response before you mount anything.
`hosted` is only ever an answer: a client that keeps the response's `ui_mode`
must not send it back, because any value other than `embedded` and `elements`
is a `400`. The same field works on `POST /v1/billing/setup` (save a card) and
`POST /v1/registrar/checkout` (buy a domain).

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/billing/checkout \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <KEY>" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"plan":"starter","return_url":"https://app.acme.example/billing/return?session_id={CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}","ui_mode":"elements"}'
# → { "id": "cs_...", "client_secret": "cs_..._secret_...", "ui_mode": "elements", "trial_days": 14, "first_charge_at": "..." }
```

### When the subscription is recorded

**Starting a checkout grants nothing.** `POST /v1/billing/checkout` validates the
tier (unknown ids, the retired `free` and the two contact-sales tiers are
rejected with `400`) and opens a Stripe session stamped with the tenant and
tier. The subscription is recorded only from a session that reads
`status: "complete"` **and** `payment_status: "paid"` (or
`no_payment_required`, which is what a trial reads), confirmed either by
Stripe's signature-verified `checkout.session.completed` event or by
`POST /v1/billing/checkout:confirm`, which the console calls when Stripe returns
the browser and which reads the session from Stripe itself. An abandoned,
expired or unpaid session leaves the tenant exactly where it was.

> **Operators: `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` is required**, and the webhook endpoint
> must subscribe to `checkout.session.completed`,
> `customer.subscription.created`, `customer.subscription.updated`,
> `customer.subscription.deleted`, `customer.subscription.trial_will_end`,
> `invoice.paid` and `invoice.payment_failed`. Without them renewals, payment
> failures and cancellations never reach the workspace's state.

> **Operators: set `STRIPE_PRODUCT_IDS`** (comma separated `prod_...` ids) on
> every deployment that makes promotion codes, including a self-hosted one with
> its own Stripe account. The Stripe account may be shared with other products,
> and a coupon without a product list would discount all of them, so with it
> empty, making a promotion code in the operator console is refused (`503`) and
> creates nothing. The same ids decide which hand-made Stripe codes customers can redeem. To turn
> the in-page checkout off, set `CHECKOUT_UI_MODE=embedded` on the console and
> recreate its container (no rebuild); the console then asks for Stripe's own
> embedded checkout. Deploy the control plane before a console that sends
> `ui_mode`.

`customer.subscription.*` events apply Stripe's subscription (its status, trial
end, period end, price and cancellation) to the workspace, and the state above
follows from it.
