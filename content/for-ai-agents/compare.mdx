There are three choices: build it yourself, use CustomDomain™, or use another
vendor. The full checklist is
[Choosing a custom-domain solution](/docs/guides/choosing-a-custom-domain-solution);
this is the short form.

## Build it yourself when

- your customers are technical and expect to edit DNS themselves,
- you can require a subdomain, so the root domain problem never comes up, and
- your volume stays low enough that certificate rate limits never bind.

## Questions that separate vendors

- **For what share of your customers does the user type nothing?** Every vendor
  reaches every provider by showing the records to type. Ask how many providers have
  a one-click rail (Domain Connect or a sign-in at the provider), and how good the
  manual path is, since that is the common case for everyone. For calibration, the
  Domain Connect project lists nine live DNS provider implementations on its own
  site, and the CustomDomain™ census lists 189 providers (2026-10-21), 116 of them
  connected by hand.
- **Root domains, conflicts and CAA:** does it connect a root domain, warn about a
  record it would replace, and catch a CAA record that would block the certificate,
  before the user starts?
- **Email:** does it merge SPF into the one existing record rather than add a
  second?
- **After go-live:** what notices that a customer's records changed, and how do you
  hear about it?
- **Integration:** an API you drive from your backend, a widget you can brand, an
  OpenAPI document the running service serves, and agent access a person approves
  and can revoke.
- **Credentials:** does the vendor hold DNS credentials, and what happens to a
  customer's grant when they leave you?
- **Pricing:** model it at ten times today's number of domains.

## Where CustomDomain™ fits

- **A fit** when you want the connect flow as infrastructure you drive from an API,
  embed as a widget or hand to an AI agent, and you care about what happens after
  the domain is live.
- **Not a fit** if you need one-click coverage at providers that have no automated
  rail (nobody can offer that), or if your customers are all technical and you can
  require a subdomain.
- **What it has:** a REST API whose OpenAPI document the service serves
  (`GET https://api.customdomain.ai/v1/openapi.json`), the `customdomain-js` widget,
  a hosted MCP server with access a person approves, SPF merging, a pre-flight that
  reports conflicting records, and DNS record drift monitoring. It has no uptime
  monitoring, and drift alerts are not delivered on the hosted service yet.
