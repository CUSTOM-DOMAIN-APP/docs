## Read the status first

Every connection carries its own diagnosis: `error_code` and `error_message` on
`GET /v1/connections/{id}`, on every list and on the webhook events. The status is
one of four:

| Status | Means | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pending` | The records are not in public DNS yet | Checked every five seconds at first, then every 15 minutes, then every six hours after three days (with `error_code` `setup_incomplete`), with no deadline. It goes live the moment the records appear |
| `propagating` | A rail wrote the records and DNS is being watched | It fails after 24 hours (`propagation_timeout`) if they never resolve, usually a wrong value or a proxy in the way |
| `live` | Every record resolves to its expected value | A domain served through the CustomDomain™ edge gets its certificate on the first request |
| `failed` | Records a rail wrote never resolved within 24 hours (`propagation_timeout`) | Not final: a recheck, creating the same domain again, or real traffic at the edge revives it |

A provider that refuses a write does not make a connection `failed`: it keeps its
status, and `error_code` says `dns_write_failed` with the provider's reason.

## The mistakes that block a connection

- **The full hostname typed in the Name field.** Most panels append the domain, so
  `www.acme.com` becomes `www.acme.com.acme.com`. Type `www`, or `@` for the root.
- **Cloudflare's proxy is on** (orange cloud). Public DNS then shows Cloudflare's
  addresses instead of the record. Switch the row to DNS only.
- **An old record is still there.** A leftover `A` or `AAAA` record at the same name
  keeps sending some visitors to the previous host.
- **A conflicting record**: a `CNAME` cannot sit beside other records at a name, and
  a `CAA` record can refuse Let's Encrypt.
- **The records were added at the wrong place.** They belong at the provider that
  serves the domain's nameservers (`dig NS your-domain.com`), which is not always the
  registrar.
- **A hosting platform's subdomain** (`something.vercel.app`, `*.netlify.app`,
  `*.github.io`) cannot be connected: the platform owns its DNS.

## The calls that help

- `GET /v1/connections/{id}/diagnose` (MCP: `diagnose-connection`): each record,
  whether public DNS has it, and what answers instead.
- `POST /v1/connections/{id}:recheck` (MCP: `recheck-connection`): check now
  instead of waiting.
- MCP `check-certificate-status`: the HTTPS certificate's issuer, expiry and any
  renewal error.
