Use the console when the domain is **yours** and you want it to point at
something you run. Want your *customers* to connect *their* domains inside your
product? Use the [widget](/docs/getting-started/widget) instead.

Two different people connect domains, and the console shows which did:

| Who | Where | What they see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your customer | Inside your product, in the widget | The widget, in your branding. The domain appears under **Domains** as *From your product (widget)*. |
| You or a teammate | Here, in the console | The console connect flow this page describes. The domain appears as *Added in the console*. |

Your server, AI agents and MCP clients can create domains through the
[API](/docs/getting-started/api) too; those are listed as *Via the API or
agents*.

## 1. Sign up and verify

Sign up at [app.customdomain.ai](https://app.customdomain.ai/signup).
We send a verification email, and you click its link before you can reach the console.
The link is valid for **one hour**.
Unverified accounts can't sign in or create API keys.
You'll see a *Check your email* screen with **Resend the link**.
If your link expired, signing in sends a fresh one automatically.
Google and GitHub follow the same rule.
If the provider has not confirmed your email address, you land on *Check your email*, and the link we send finishes the sign in.
Such a provider also cannot sign in to an existing account with that address.
Sign in with your password instead, then add Google or GitHub under **Settings, Security**.
The provider must have confirmed the address for that too.

Under the form, passkey, Google and GitHub sit in one row of square icon buttons.
Hover or focus one to see its name.
Where Google sign in is set up, Google also shows its own **Sign in with Google** card at the top right of the sign in and sign up pages (Google One Tap).
On the sign in page it can sign a returning person in without a click.
It won't if you signed out on that browser since you last used it.
On the sign up page it only offers: creating an account always takes a click.
It does not show on the invite page or inside a frame.
It also stays hidden when your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, or when you turned analytics off in cookie settings.
It follows every rule the Google button does, including the email rule below.

Tick the box under the heading to agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy.
Email, Google, GitHub and the Google card all wait for it.
If you pick a Google account on the card before ticking, nothing is sent.
The box then says "Tick this box to continue with the Google account you chose".
Ticking it signs you in with that account and records your agreement.
Some people never saw the box: accounts made before it existed, invites, and Google or GitHub sign ins from the sign in page.
For them the console asks once, on a page of its own, then takes them where they were going.

**Signing up with Google or GitHub fills in the welcome.**
GitHub may give a company and a website on the profile.
A Google Workspace account uses a company's own domain, not Gmail.
In either case the welcome starts with that company name and website.
Anything you typed yourself is never overwritten.
An address at a free mail service gets no company guess.

The signup form also carries bot protection: a bot-check widget, signup rate
limits, and disposable-email-address blocking. If the bot check fails, wait a
moment and try again.

Signed up with an email and a password?
Once the welcome is done, **Overview** offers once to add Google or GitHub as another way to sign in.
The choices are **Add Google**, **Add GitHub** or **Not now**.
It only connects an account that uses the same email address as yours.
Either answer is remembered on every device.
You can do the same any time under **Settings, Security**.

## 2. Tell us who the domains are for

A new workspace goes straight to the path for **My product's users**: "I run a product or platform. My users bring their own
domains, through our widget or API." There is no question to answer first.

A workspace that already chose another path keeps it. The older welcome asked **Who are you connecting domains for?**
and also offered **My clients** and the link **Just connecting a domain of my own?**.
Each answer sets up only the steps it needs.
Every later step has **Skip to dashboard** at the bottom right, which takes you to the console.
What is left stays on the checklist on **Overview**.
**Change my answers** there opens the welcome again.
A step whose own button does the work also has **Not now**, which moves to the next step.

| You pick | The steps after the question |
| --- | --- |
| **My product's users** | Your product's website (see below), your brand with a live preview on the real widget, then who writes the code: **I am writing the code** or **My AI writes the code**. The same screen shows your application ID and your default key to copy. Then the widget code, or for your AI, the setup for the agent you use and one prompt to copy (it has your application ID, never your key). That is the last step |
| **Just connecting a domain of my own?** | Your domain, with an option to set up email on it in the same step |

**I am writing the code** shows the widget code in four steps, each with a copy button.
The steps are your key, a token route on your server, the widget for your framework with your application ID filled in, and the result.
Then pick where to keep building, or **Try it now**.

**What's your product's website?** is the step after **My product's users**.
The address is filled in from a work email, not from free mail such as Gmail.
We read the page in the background while you type, so **Continue** works at any time.
What we find comes back as one sentence you can correct, with **Yes** and **Not quite**.
For example: "Looks like Acme is a website builder. Right?"
A sentence we are sure of shows as already confirmed.
**Not quite**, **No website yet**, or a site that says too little opens one optional line instead: "In a few words, what does Acme do?"
The kind of product sets several things for you.
It picks the widget options in your install code and the example customer domain in the brand preview.
It also picks the prompt for your AI and the guide the code step links to.
Your product name sits under it, filled in from the website or your email address.
Your customers see that name on the widget.

The first screen asks nothing about AI agents.
With **My product's users**, **My AI writes the code** is the agent path.
Pick the agent you use: Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex, Cursor, VS Code, ChatGPT or another.
Add CustomDomain™ to it with a command or a few fields, and copy the prompt.
With **My clients** or a domain of your own, the last screen offers **Let an AI agent connect domains** ("It only does what you approve.").
It opens the console's **MCP server** page with the setup for your agent.
Most agents sign in with your account, so there is no key to paste.
An agent counts as connected only once it has signed in and you approved what it may do.
See [MCP: drive it from an agent](/docs/getting-started/mcp#set-up-an-agent-from-the-welcome) and [Agents](/docs/agents/overview).

Pointing a domain at a website needs the Edge plan.
CustomDomain™ does not host websites.
It serves your domain through its edge to a site hosted elsewhere (see [Where domains point](/docs/connect-flow/routing)).
Once your own domain is connected, the last screen says so beside **Point it at my website**.
Connecting the domain does not need the Edge plan.

Everything else we can work out is filled in for you to confirm.
Your logo, name and brand color are read from your website.
For your own domain, the welcome offers the one your work email is at.
Change anything that is off.

The domain step finds where the domain is registered and hosted as soon as you type it.
It says whether we can set it up automatically or will show you the records.
Then it opens the same connect widget your customers use.
If the domain is not registered yet, it says so.
You can also carry on and connect one when you have it.
When something is left, it says what and who can do it.
Anyone with access to your DNS host can finish it.

Your place and answers are saved on every step.
Close the tab and come back, and you resume where you left off.
Each step has its own address (`/welcome?step=brand`), so a refresh stays on it.
The browser's Back goes where the **Back** button does.
A domain you already connected shows as connected.
The last screen shows what you have and the one next action, with a few more to start with.
While a domain waits for its DNS records, it also offers to ping you when it is live, with **Notify me** and **Not now**.
Your browser asks only when you click.
It turns push on for a domain going live and nothing else (see [Notifications](/docs/guides/notifications)).
Open the welcome again any time to change who the domains are for.
Use the first row of the checklist on **Overview**.
If you picked **My product's users**, you can also use **Change my answers** under the go-live list.

A short tour of the console follows.
Replay it any time from the setup checklist on **Overview**.
The checklist follows what you picked.
For your own domain, it covers connecting it, plus email or pointing it at your site if you chose those.
For a few clients, it covers connecting the first domain.
It always ends with inviting your team.
For clients or your own domain, a **Your site is live** row also appears while a live domain has nothing to serve.
Such a domain points at our edge, but no app address is set, so its visitors see an error page.
The row stays open until every live domain serves something.
If you chose **Let an AI agent connect domains**, an optional **Connect your agent** row appears.
It is done when an agent has signed in and been approved, and it names the agent.
An agent that uses a key leaves nothing to see, so the row says that too.
If you picked **My product's users**, the checklist is the go-live list below.
It ends with the same optional row when the welcome found your product is an AI app or agent builder.

### Your go-live list, My product's users [#your-go-live-list-my-customers]

If you run a product and want your users to bring their own domains, **Overview**
shows a go-live list. A row is done when something has happened that proves it,
not when you visited a page, and each row says what was checked:

| Row | Done when |
| --- | --- |
| **Start your free trial** (owners and admins) | Billing is set up and a trial or a plan is running. A card starts the 14 day trial and nothing is charged until it ends. Creating API keys needs it |
| **Run your first connection** (then **Try the widget with your own domain**) | A domain connected through the widget is live, including one you connect from [**Try it live**](#try-it-live). A real domain counts as one of your domains, which is why this row says so. **Start** opens four ways to begin: see the widget as your customers do, connect a domain you own, try the whole flow in the Sandbox on made up `.test` names (they use nothing), or copy a prompt for your AI agent with your application ID. The same choice opens once by itself after the welcome; **Skip** closes it for good, and the row stays open until a real domain is live |
| **Mint widget tokens on your server** | Your backend asked for a widget token with an API key (the route from the **Install** tab, written for your backend). A token minted from the console's **Try it live** does not count: it is the console's own, minted for you and not for a customer |
| **Choose where customers' domains point** | Your own infrastructure is set as the target, or a default origin is set for the edge (which needs the Edge plan) |
| **Get told when a domain goes live** | A webhook delivery was answered with a success status |
| **Make the widget yours** (optional) | A look was saved, for the workspace or for an application |
| **Connect your first customer** | A real customer connected a domain through your widget, which is a connection that carries the customer id your server passed when it minted the token. A domain you connect from **Try it live** carries the console's own id for you (`console:<member>`), so it never counts as a customer, however many you try |

Sandbox is a separate application you switch to from the application switcher, marked
**Sandbox** wherever it appears. It needs no card, it is not counted or billed, and
its domains show on **Domains** and **Activity** only while you are switched to it. See
[Sandbox](/docs/guides/test-mode) for what each ending does and what it leaves out.

A row can be done and still warn.
Examples are a default origin that failed its check, or a webhook whose newest delivery failed.
Each warning links to the fix.
The list stays on **Overview** while any row warns, even when every required row is done.
It reloads when you come back to the tab, because you finish most of these steps somewhere else.
A customer who connects also marks the earlier rows done.
They cannot have connected without the widget opening and your server minting a token.
The console does not see a token minted straight at the API with your client secret.
Your first customer proves it instead.

**Dismiss** on the list hides it for you, on every device, and a reload shows no list.
With the new menu (**Settings → General → Navigation**), **Help → Show the setup list** at the bottom of the menu brings it back.
On a phone or tablet, **More** holds Help.
It opens **Overview** with the list on it, and the list stays through a reload.
The list is yours to hide, not the workspace's.
A teammate's list is theirs.

The list follows what the workspace's owner or an admin said.
A teammate you invite sees the same go-live list, without the trial row, which is for owners and admins.
In a workspace for clients or for your own domain, a teammate sees the usual checklist until they answer the welcome too.
Your customers' domains are on **Domains**, on the **From your product (widget)** tab.
The list itself opens on **All**.

#### API key or client secret [#key-or-secret]

Two credentials look alike and do different jobs. The **Install** tab's first step
shows them as two cards.

| | API key | Client secret |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What it is** | The workspace's key, `cd_live_…` or `cd_test_…`. It proves a call comes from your server. | One application's secret, `sk_…`. |
| **Where to find it** | **API & SDK**: the default key at the top, or **Create key** for more. The key bar on **Install** has **Get your API key**. | Shown once, in the response that creates an application over the API. An application made in the console never shows it. |
| **What it can do** | Full access to the workspace: domains, connections, keys, webhooks. Keep it on your server. | Mint widget tokens for that application and nothing else. It is not an API key, and an API key in its place is refused with `401`. |

Put the API key in your server's environment as `CUSTOMDOMAIN_API_KEY`. You do
not need the client secret for an application made in the console: see below.

The application the console made for you has a client secret.
The console keeps it sealed and never shows it.
To mint widget tokens from your server, send one of these keys as the bearer to `POST /v1/tokens`.
The **Install** tab on **Widget** writes the code for you.
Do not rotate that application's secret.
The console's copy would go stale, and the preview and **Try it live** would stop working.
See [Widget tokens](/docs/authentication/widget-tokens).

## 3. Connect a domain

**Connect a domain** (the sidebar button, **Overview** or **Domains**) first
asks who is connecting it:

- **Connect a domain yourself**: your own site, or one you're setting up for
  a customer. You add the DNS records. This is the console path, and the
  rest of this page.
- **Let your customers connect theirs**: takes you to the **Widget** page to
  embed it in your product.

Links that already say which (a domain's **Resume** link, or **Connect a
domain yourself** on Overview) skip the question. The connect page then says
plainly that you are on the console path. Enter the hostname:

- `www.acme.com` / `app.acme.com`: a subdomain. Any DNS provider can hold the
  `CNAME` we need.
- `acme.com`: the root. It works everywhere, but the record depends on the
  provider; see [Root domains](/docs/dns/root-domains).

The pre-flight refuses two things up front.
The first is a domain that doesn't resolve at all: unregistered, or never delegated at the registrar.
The second is a hosting platform's own subdomain (`www.<name>.vercel.app`).
Both used to sit on "waiting for DNS" for days.

## 4. Choose how to add the records

The options shown are the truth for *your* provider, straight from the server.
They are the [ways to connect](/docs/concepts/glossary#ways-to-connect) that fit
the domain. The recommended one is open; the others are closed one-line rows under
**Other ways to connect**, in this order:

1. **Sign in with the provider**: Cloudflare, DigitalOcean, DNSimple, Linode, Netlify, Vercel (by installing the CustomDomain™ integration) and WordPress.com, plus **Approve access at Porkbun**.
   Approve once in a popup and we add the records.
   This is the fastest way.
   **Keep this sign-in** starts ticked.
   We keep it, encrypted, so we can put the records back and remove them with the domain.
   Untick it to use the sign-in once.
   Some sign-ins are always used once.
   These are Azure DNS (a Microsoft account), and Dynadot or Unstoppable Domains (through the provider's MCP server).
   OVHcloud's consent page and the other MCP server sign-ins are built but not switched on yet.
   See [DNS providers](/docs/dns/providers#built-but-not-switched-on-yet).
2. **One-click setup**: only where the provider serves our template
   (NameSilo, Glauca Digital, Domain Chief today). After you approve the change
   there, the provider sends you back here. GoDaddy, IONOS, Aruba,
   Squarespace, Cloudflare and Vercel are *not* offered this, on purpose: they
   have not onboarded our templates.
3. **Secure sign-in**: at Namecheap, GoDaddy, IONOS,
   Name.com, Squarespace, Hover, Spaceship and Hostinger, where neither of the
   two above runs. **Open secure sign-in** opens the provider's own sign-in in a
   secure window, and we add the records. Changes that replace or remove a record wait for the customer's approval. See
   [Secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in).
4. **API key**: paste a scoped provider credential, one labeled field per part.
   Each field links to where the credential is created, with the steps and the one requirement that matters.
   Anything else, and the provider's notes, sit under a closed **Details**.
   We keep it encrypted when **Remember this key** stays ticked.
   Untick it to use the key once.
   Any GoDaddy account that holds a domain can create a Personal Access Token.
   Namecheap's API needs an eligible account and our server's IP on its allowlist.
   Route 53 offers an access role first, with no key to paste.
5. **Add the records** by hand: the exact rows to copy, with a link to your
   provider's DNS panel. Then **Check now**.
6. **DNS hosting**: you change the domain's nameservers once, where it was bought.
   CustomDomain™ writes every record after that.
   It is not a row on this page.
   It is the **DNS hosting** card at the foot of the domain's **DNS** tab.
   The application's **DNS hosting** setting decides whether the widget offers it to your customers ([set in Ways to connect](#domain-maintenance)).
   The domain check reports the same verdict as `rails.nameservers`.
   See [Managed DNS](/docs/dns/managed-dns).

Under the detected DNS host, the page also says whether your application offers DNS hosting to its users.
It reads **DNS hosting for your users: Offered**, with a line on what it is and a link to its guide.
A **Change** link opens [its row in Ways to connect](#domain-maintenance).

Not the one with DNS access? Under the records, **Send these records to a
teammate** emails them to whoever is. Replies go to you.

- An **owner or admin** may send to someone who isn't in the workspace.
  That also invites them as a member, and the email carries the records and the invite.
- Someone **already in the workspace** gets the records and a link back to
  this domain's setup in the console.
- A **member** can send the records but not invite: the email links a
  single-use guided setup that needs no account.
- **Viewers** can't send email from the console.

Someone outside your team, such as a client who has the DNS? Use **Copy a link
for someone outside your team** instead. It makes a setup link for this one
domain that works with no account and invites nobody; see
[A link for the client](#a-link-for-the-client).

## 5. Verify

The connection sits in `pending` until the records resolve, then goes `live`.
Records written for you usually verify within seconds.
That covers a sign-in, one-click setup or an API key.
A new connection is checked every five seconds at first.
When public DNS still has an old answer, we ask the domain's own nameservers instead.
You can leave and come back.
**Domains** shows every connection with its reason and a **Resume** link.
After three minutes of waiting, the page offers a fallback.
A stalled connection is *paused*, not failed, and it keeps being checked every six hours.

While a domain waits for its DNS, its row on **Domains** and the header of its page say how long it has waited.
They use three words:

| Status | When | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Checking DNS** | The first 30 minutes after the domain was added | We look for the records on our own. Usually a few minutes. |
| **Records found** | Our check has seen the records and DNS is still updating | Waiting for DNS to update. Usually minutes, sometimes a few hours. |
| **Not finished** | After 30 minutes without the records, or a check that stalled | The row says "Started 3 days ago" and gives the next step, with **Finish setup** to open the domain's connect page. |

These words describe the wait only.
The connection's status in the API is unchanged (`pending`, `propagating`, `live`, `failed`).
A domain that is adding its records or waiting for its HTTPS certificate keeps its own label.
"Started" means when the domain was added, since nothing records when you added the records.

Every domain in your workspace opens on its detail page, whichever application it belongs to.
The page names the application.
A domain that isn't live leads with **What happened**.
It says what the last attempt did.
It may have timed out, the provider may have refused a key, it may still wait for the records, or it may be connected somewhere else.
Then it lists the next steps in order and the action that fits.
The actions are **Finish setup**, **Retry** (which restarts a failed attempt), or removing an attempt nobody will finish.
Below it, a short history says when the domain was added and through what.
It also says when its DNS was last checked.

While a domain waits, owners and admins see **Get a ping when it is live** on its detail page.
You can close the tab and be told the moment it connects (see [Notifications](/docs/guides/notifications)).
The page also opens **What public DNS shows right now**.
For each record it shows the Name to type, whether public DNS has it, and what answers instead ("still points to Squarespace").
It also flags mistakes that block a connection even when a record looks right.
Two common ones are the full hostname typed into the Name field and Cloudflare's proxy turned on.
It is the same check the widget runs for your customers (`GET /v1/connections/{id}/diagnose`).
Send a customer whose domain is stuck to this page.

### How CustomDomain™ reaches the domain, and who runs its DNS [#how-we-reach-a-domain]

Under the actions, a domain's page says two things in plain words.

**Who runs its DNS** is the first fact.
It names the company whose servers answer for the domain (its [DNS host](/docs/concepts/glossary#dns-host)).
Once we host the zone, it names CustomDomain™ ([DNS hosting](/docs/dns/managed-dns)).
A move to DNS hosting that is under way still shows the old company, with "moving to CustomDomain™" beside it.
A move that failed says it needs a look.

**How CustomDomain™ reaches this domain** is a card. It says which access we keep to
change the domain's records, in one line each:

| The card says | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Kept by your IONOS account "Acme"** | A [connected account](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts) with its label. We use its key. |
| **Kept by your Cloudflare sign-in** | You approved us once at the provider, in a popup, and we kept that approval. |
| **Kept by your one-click setup at GoDaddy** | You approved the change once at the provider, and we kept that approval. |
| **Through DNS hosting** | We run the domain's DNS, so we write its records ourselves. |
| **DNS hosting, setting up** | The nameservers have not moved yet. We hold its records and they go live once its nameservers point to us. |
| **DNS hosting needs a look** | Hosting stopped working for this domain. Open its DNS tab to see why. |
| **Kept by your AWS access role** | A role in your AWS account. It can add and change records, not delete them. |
| **Not kept** | We keep no access, so a change to the records needs you at the provider. |

The card's buttons follow from that.
**Re-apply now** writes the records again, and shows for any kept access that can write right now.
**Stop managing** shows whenever the domain is managed.
It drops the sign-in or one-click setup we hold and leaves the records where they are.
It shows even when the card says DNS hosting or **Not kept**.
A managed domain that moved to DNS hosting keeps its old sign-in, and this is how you let go of it.
**Make this domain managed** shows only where it can run.
That means nothing is kept yet, and the provider signs in or the deployment can offer one-click setup for it.
**Change** opens a menu:

- **Use another account** lists the saved accounts that may act for this domain, and writes its records with the one you pick.
  When none is kept, it reads **Use a saved account**.
  An account has to be at the same DNS provider and still work.
  Admins and owners see the workspace's accounts for the domain's application.
  A member sees the ones they connected, so the list can be empty.
- **Add an account** opens [Add an account](#add-an-account), where you can connect the provider's account once and use it for this domain and others.
- **Host its DNS instead** opens a sheet that starts [DNS hosting](/docs/guides/nameserver-migration#start-one-domain) for this one domain.
  It works whatever the application's DNS hosting setting says.
  It lists the ways that would work, and nothing is picked until you pick.
  The ways are a saved account at the registrar (the company the domain is registered with), **Secure sign-in**, and **Change it yourself**.
  Sometimes the move cannot start, and the sheet says why with nothing to start.
  Reasons include the plan's limit of hosted domains, a registrar that keeps its own nameservers, or a move already under way.
  Once the move starts, the page goes to the domain's DNS tab, which shows the move.
  A domain whose DNS is already ours shows **See its DNS hosting** instead.
  One whose move is under way or failed shows **See the DNS hosting move**.
  The connect page has the same button under the detected DNS host.

A viewer sees the card with its buttons off, and the reason under them: members, admins
and owners can change domains. The **Management** line under **Details** reads
**Managed** whenever any access is kept, and **Not managed** only when none is. See
[Managed connections](/docs/connect-flow/managed-connections#what-managed-means).

### A domain's settings [#domain-settings]

A domain's page has seven tabs: **Overview**, **DNS**, **Certificate**, **Serving**, **Traffic**,
**History** and **Settings**. On a phone the last ones sit under **More**. **Serving** is the tab that
was called **Edge**: it keeps its address, so old links still open it. **Settings** holds what belongs to
the domain itself, one card each. The first three are fields with their own **Save**, so you can change
them any time after the domain is set up:

- **Who it is for** is the client or customer name the **Domains** list shows and filters by.
  Type a name and **Save**.
  To take it off, empty the field and **Save**.
  The name is yours, and your customers never see it.
  It is not the id your product gave the customer, which shows under the field and stays as it is ([whose a domain is](#many-domains-and-whose-they-are)).
  It can be up to 200 characters.
  Characters that draw nothing are dropped, such as a right to left override, a zero width space or blank fillers.
  A name with nothing readable in it is refused.
- **www redirect** sends visitors of a root domain on to `www.` that domain.
  It shows on a root domain only (`acme.com`, not `shop.acme.com`).
  It is on every plan, and the **www redirect** fact on **Overview** opens this card.
  Turning it on adds a `www` record to the records the domain needs.
  After **Save**, the card says who adds that record.
  We add it when CustomDomain™ runs the domain's DNS or a connected account we keep can write it.
  Otherwise you add it where you manage DNS, and the card shows the record and links to the **DNS** tab.
  The domain stays live meanwhile.
  Turning it off also stops us answering for `www.` that domain, unless you connected it as its own domain.
- **Forwarding** sends every visit to another address, with the same path.
  Type an `https://` address and **Save**, or **Clear**.
  Forwarding is part of edge serving.
  A workspace without edge serving reads **Included on the Edge plan and up.**
  A domain that points at your own servers reads **Not on the edge**, and its www redirect does nothing there.
  A deployment with edge serving off has no card.
  A refused address says why.
  It must start with `https://`, and it can't hold a user name or password.
  It must be a public domain name, not an IP address, `localhost` or an internal name.
  It can't be a domain another CustomDomain™ workspace has connected or claimed, though one your own workspace serves is fine.
  It can't send visitors round in a circle, back to where they started.
  That includes the domain itself, or a loop through the `www` redirect or your other forwards.
  It also can't pass through more than 8 forwards in a row.
  See [forwarding](/docs/connect-flow/forwarding#from-the-console).
- **Remove this domain** opens the same removal dialog as the **Overview**'s menu, and links
  [what removing does](/docs/connect-flow/offboarding).

Members, admins and owners can change a domain's settings. A viewer reads the same cards as values, with
**Remove domain** turned off and the reason under it. A Sandbox domain has **Who it is for** and
**Remove this domain** only. Every change is recorded in the [audit log](/docs/security/overview) as
**Domain settings changed**.

## 6. Serve something

Live means the edge terminates TLS and proxies the domain to your application's **origin** (**Edge serving**).
If you haven't set an origin, the domain shows a "not configured" page.
Set it under **Routing**, on the page titled **Where domains point**, as the **Application address**.
Or point the domains straight at your own servers there instead.
See [Where domains point](/docs/connect-flow/routing).
The domain's **Serving** tab shows whether the edge serves it and where your application runs.
Root-domain redirects (`www_redirect`) and forwarding show on its **Settings** tab.
You set them with the API, along with per-path rules.

## 7. See its traffic

Once the CustomDomain™ edge serves a domain, its detail page has a **Traffic** tab.
It shows requests by the kind of answer visitors got: success, redirects, client errors and server errors.
It also shows data sent to visitors and how fast your origin answered (p95 and median time to first byte).
It adds approximate visitors and when the last request arrived.
You can view 24 hours, 7, 30 or 90 days.
**Analytics** has a **Traffic** tab for the whole workspace or one application, with the busiest domains ([below](#analytics)).

Data sent to visitors counts two things.
It counts the response bodies the edge relayed from your application.
It also counts the pages the edge wrote itself: redirects, the page for a domain with no application address yet, and its error page.
Before 2026-11-02 the edge's own pages were not counted.
So a domain whose requests were all "nothing to serve" pages showed 0 B beside thousands of requests.

Only traffic that passes through the edge is counted.
A domain connected for DNS only points at your own servers, so its visitors never reach us.
Its page says so instead of showing zeros, and your hosting has those numbers.

Counts arrive about a minute after each visit.
No visitor's IP address is stored.
Visitors are estimated from a sketch of keyed hashes.
Someone who visits on two days counts once on each.
Hourly detail is kept 14 days and daily totals 400 days.
The API has the same figures: `GET /v1/connections/{id}/traffic` and `GET /v1/traffic`.

## Analytics [#analytics]

**Analytics** has four tabs. All follow the date range and the application picker.

### Widget

The widget funnel counts sessions at each step.
The steps are entered a domain, offered a [way to connect](/docs/concepts/glossary), shown records, and connected.
**Most common stopping point** names the step people most often stopped at.
It also says how many of the unfinished sessions stopped there.
The step is the last one a person saw before closing the widget.
Until 2026-11-02 the card nearly always read "Closed the widget", because closing overwrote the step.
The control plane now keeps the step before the close.
Sessions recorded earlier read correctly once it is updated.
Steps with the same label add up.

**Is the widget installed?**
Analytics, the widget card on **Overview** and the check on the **Install** tab all answer it the same way.
They read the sessions the widget records when it opens.
A session is a **console test** when it came from your own console: **Try it live** and the welcome's domain step.
Every other session came from your product.
You see one of:

- "Installed on yourapp.com, last seen 2 hours ago": the widget opened in your
  product. The site is the page's address without a path.
- "Only seen on localhost": it opened only on your own machine so far.
- "Not seen in your product yet": no session from your product has been recorded.

Sessions are split into **in your product** and **console tests**.
So your own clicks in **Try it live** never make the widget look installed.
The **Design** preview opens no session.
The API has it as `install` on [`GET /v1/analytics/connect`](/docs/api-reference/analytics/getConnectAnalytics), one entry per application.
Each entry has `product_sessions`, `test_sessions`, the same two counts inside the date range, `last_product_at`, `last_product_host` and `last_test_at`.
An application with no session is not listed.

### Traffic

What the edge answered for your domains. The tab opens with what a request is: one
page, image or file a visitor's browser asked for. Crawlers and scanners count.
Visits to a domain that only uses CustomDomain™ for DNS are not counted, and
neither are our own domain checks (they identify as `CustomDomainBot/`).

Then three figures:

- **Requests**, the number of requests.
- **Served or redirected**, the share that got a page, a file or a redirect.
  Under it, how many were not served or failed.
- **Data sent to visitors**, the size of the response bodies (headers are not
  counted).

**How requests were answered** splits the requests by what the visitor got, with a line on each.
**Served (2xx)** means the page loaded.
**Redirected (3xx)** means the visitor was sent elsewhere.
**Not served (4xx)** means the page does not exist, was refused, or the domain has no application address set yet.
**Application errors (5xx)** means your application did not answer or failed.
The table of busiest domains has four columns: **Domain**, **Requests**, **Not served** and **Last request**.
**Not served** is the share answered with a 4xx or 5xx.
The API has the same figures ([`GET /v1/traffic`](/docs/api-reference/analytics/getTrafficSummary)).

### Providers

Where domains stall, by the DNS provider they are with:

- A table with a row per provider and five columns.
  **DNS provider** names it, and **Started** counts domains added in the period.
  **Connected** counts how many are live now, next to **Rate**.
  **Top reason stuck** names the most common reason that provider's unfinished domains are stuck, or "Nothing stuck".
- **Time to go live** counts the live domains by how long they took from being added.
  The bands are under 5 minutes, 5 minutes to 1 hour, 1 hour to 1 day, and over a day.
- **How domains connect** lists each way of connecting and each domain type (root domain, subdomain).
  For each it shows domains added, how many are live and the median time to go live.

The API returns the same on [`GET /v1/analytics/connect`](/docs/api-reference/analytics/getConnectAnalytics), all under `connections`.
Each provider has `top_stuck`, `top_stuck_count` and `median_seconds_to_live`.
Each method and domain type has `median_seconds_to_live`.
`time_to_live` sits beside `stuck`.

#### The numbers in the API [#analytics-api]

| Field | Where | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `top_stuck` | each entry of `connections.by_provider` | The most common reason that provider's not-live domains are stuck (a key of `stuck`). Absent when none are. |
| `top_stuck_count` | the same | How many domains have that reason. |
| `median_seconds_to_live` | the same, and each entry of `by_method` and `by_kind` | Median seconds from adding a domain to it going live, over that group's live domains. `0` when none are live. |
| `time_to_live` | `connections` | Live domains by how long they took: `under_5m`, `5m_to_1h`, `1h_to_1d`, `over_1d`. Always all four, in that order. |

### Secure sign-in [#secure-sign-in-tab]

The fourth tab answers "how long does a [secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in)
take, and where do people give up?" without a query. It follows the date range and
the application picker, and has a provider filter. It shows nothing until your
customers have used secure sign-in.

- **Started and finished**, with the typical and the slow time to **Done**. The
  time is measured to the **Done** your customer saw, so a lasting API key made
  after it is not in the number.
- **The funnel.** Started, sign-in shown, signed in, changes ready, making
  changes, done. Each stage shows how many sessions reached it and the share lost
  since the stage before. A session you finished by hand and one still running are
  counted apart and are not a loss.
- **Time per step.** For each step, a bar shows the median and the slowest tenth.
  The steps are opening the browser, your customer signing in, reading the DNS page and planning, review and start, and writing the records.
- **Why sessions stopped,** in plain words.
  The reasons are: the browser did not start, left at sign in, code never entered, could not finish at the provider, provider stopped the sign-in, timed out, cancelled, changes failed, or something else.
  Each session is filed under one reason.
- **Recent runs.** The newest sessions with their provider, outcome and total time.
  Open one to see its steps as a timeline, with the seconds since the run began.
  Steps are fixed words: a timeline never holds a code, a password, a domain or
  anything typed.

A domain's **History** tab lists that domain's own secure sign-in runs the same
way, and shows nothing for a domain that never used it.

The API has the same: [`GET /v1/analytics/secure-sign-in`](/docs/api-reference/analytics/getSecureSignInFlow),
[`GET /v1/analytics/secure-sign-in/sessions`](/docs/api-reference/analytics/listSecureSignInSessions)
and [`GET /v1/analytics/secure-sign-in/sessions/{sid}`](/docs/api-reference/analytics/getSecureSignInTimeline).

## Who opens the widget, and secure sign-in

Two more panels on **Analytics** appear once there is something to count. Both
follow the date range and the application picker, like the rest of the page.

- **Devices and places**, on the **Widget** tab, ranks the widget sessions by device (desktop, phone, tablet).
  It also ranks top countries and browsers.

### The API key a secure sign-in made [#provider-api-key]

When a secure sign-in made a
[lasting API key](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in#lasting-api-keys) at a domain's DNS
provider, the domain's page has a **Provider API key** card:

- One line on where the key stands, and its status: **Working**, **Ends soon**,
  **Needs the end user to sign in again** or **Ended**.
- When it ends, its name at the provider, when it was checked, and when it was
  renewed and a reconnect link last went out.
- **What it can do**, when the key reaches more than the domain's DNS.
  It reads as a sentence, such as: "This key can also transfer the domain away from Spaceship, and make charges on the account (renewals and purchases)."
- **Send reconnect link** emails your customer a link to sign in again when you
  gave us their address, and copies the link otherwise. **Copy reconnect link**
  copies one to pass on. A link works once, for 14 days

- After a renewal, a note says the old key still exists at the provider.
  It links to the provider's keys page so you can revoke it there.

**Connected accounts** lists such a key with the badge **Made at sign in**.
Its sheet shows the domain, when the key ends, its name, scopes and when it was checked.
It also shows the account's name or number at the provider, where the page showed one.
It never shows the key.
A key your customer's sign-in made serves only that customer's domains.
Either kind is renewed by a new sign-in, never by pasting a key, so its only action is **Remove**.
Removing it deletes CustomDomain™'s copy.
The key itself stays at the provider until it is revoked there.
A domain's timeline says when the key was made, renewed, nearing its end or refused.

## Many domains, and whose they are

If you manage domains for clients, or have a lot of your own, you don't connect them one at a time.
After **My clients**, the welcome's next screen is a box.
Paste a column from a spreadsheet, a line of addresses or a list of links, in any mix.
The same box is on **Domains** under **Add several**, up to 200 at a time.

- **What it reads.** An address gives its host.
  A repeat is added once and counted.
  Anything that is not a domain is listed and left out.
  Nothing you typed is thrown away: what could not be added stays in the box.
- **Whose it is.** A name typed above the box is shown next to every domain
  below it. A name after a comma or a tab on a line (`beta.org, Beta Bakery`) is
  used for that domain instead. A domain already in your workspace is left as it
  was. The name can be changed later: open the domain's **Settings** tab, type
  another name and **Save**, or empty the field to take it off.
- **The first ten domains are free.**
  A new workspace connects its first ten domains without a card, and the rest once the free trial has started.
  The box says so before you paste, with **Start free trial** beside it.
  Nothing is charged for 14 days.
  It adds what it can now and keeps the rest in the box.
  After the trial starts, you land back on the same page with the rest still there.
  A teammate who is not an owner or an admin is told to ask one of them.
- **Adding is not connecting.** A list puts the domains in your workspace, each
  waiting for its DNS records. Finish each the way you would finish one, from its
  row on **Domains**, or send the step to the person who has the DNS (below).

On **Domains**, a workspace for clients says **client** where the others say
**customer**:

- **Client** narrows the list to one client's domains, or to those with no
  client.
- **Order** shows newest first (the default), oldest first, domain, client, or
  what needs attention first.
- **Export CSV** saves exactly the rows shown, in the order shown.
  Each row has the domain, status, client, DNS host, when and where it was added, the application, and what is left to do.
  It opens in a spreadsheet.
  A client name that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` is saved as text, so a spreadsheet never runs it.

A domain's own page says "For Acme Dental" in a workspace for clients, where it
would say who connected it elsewhere.

### A link for the client

When an owner or an admin uses **Send these records to a teammate**, it also invites the recipient to the workspace.
That is wrong for a client who has the DNS.
**Copy a link for the client** makes a link for that one domain instead.
Find it in the ⋯ menu of a row on **Domains**, and beside the email option on the connect step.
Other workspaces see it as **Copy a setup link**.
The link shows:

- Whoever opens it finishes that domain with no account, and nobody is invited.
  The page carries your workspace's name and nothing about its billing.
- It works once and lasts 30 days. It cannot be listed or cancelled afterwards,
  so make it when you are ready to send it. The console shows it on the page as
  well as copying it.
- It is for a domain that is not live yet, and it needs the member role or
  above. A workspace can make 30 an hour.

### Add an account

**Connected accounts** has **Connect an account**. It opens a page, `/app/accounts/new`, because the steps
are too long for a pop-up on a phone. Nothing is chosen for you.

1. **Which company.** Pick it from the list, or search it by name. A registrar is where you bought
   the domain, and a DNS host runs the records that say where a name goes. If you are not sure who
   runs your domain's DNS, type a domain you have there and **Find it** looks it up. A company that connects
   one domain at a time is named and sent to the connect page, because there is no account to add.
2. **How to connect.** Only that company's ways, easiest first.
   **Sign in to the company** opens a secure window over the page.
   You sign in, we make the API key for you, and we show what it can do and when it ends.
   **Paste an API key** shows the company's own steps and the page where the key is made.
   Then **Check the key** reads the domains it reaches.
   A company whose key is used once says why, and links to [what that means](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts#credentials-that-are-used-once).
   With more than one application, you also say which one the key and its domains are for.
3. **What it may do.** Pick every domain in the account, or only the ones you tick.
   A company that cannot list its domains asks for one domain the key manages, so we can check the key.
   Then pick which [applications may use the key](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts#which-applications-may-use-it).
   An admin or an owner can share a sign-in's key with the others.
   Last, choose whether it is [kept or used once](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts#access).

Then **Connect N domains now** writes the records of the domains the account may use.
Each goes into the application you choose, and the page says what happened to each one.
**Later** leaves them for the account's own page.
Sometimes nothing is kept: you close the sign-in window, go back, or take more than ten minutes.
The page then names the key it already made, with the provider's page that deletes it.
Going back to the first step keeps naming it there.
Choosing the same company again names it on its step 2.
If a domain's result does not come back, the page says it cannot tell whether that domain connected.
**Try the ones that failed again** offers it once more, into the same application.
So a repeat never connects a domain twice.
A viewer cannot add an account.
The same steps, with the calls behind them, are in [Provider accounts](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts#add-an-account-in-the-console).

### What every line says

- **How it was made and by whom.** A pasted key says who pasted it (a teammate's e-mail, or the API key's name). A key a secure sign-in made says who started the sign-in, or that it was made when **a customer** signed in. It never names your customer.
- **What it can do.** It says in plain words what the account can do.
  It can put back records it wrote that disappeared, where automatic repair is on.
  It can remove them when a domain is removed, and re-apply them when asked.
  A one time key says it connects the domains you chose now and is then deleted.
  A key a sign-in made also says what it can do beyond DNS records, when the provider gave it that right.
  Examples are transferring the domain away or making charges on the account.
- **Its domains, last use and end.** It shows how many domains it keeps in sync and when it was last used.
  It also shows when it ends.
  A pasted key reads **No end date**.
  A sign-in key shows the key's own date.
  A one time key shows the hour it is deleted by.

### The account's sheet [#account-sheet]

- **Rename.** **Rename** beside the badges changes the account's name, one to 80 characters with no line breaks or invisible characters in it. A name can be an e-mail address, so it is hidden when a session is recorded.
- **Domains it may configure.** The list of domains is in the sheet.
  Open the account, tick the domains, and **Save**.
  A pasted key and a key a secure sign-in made list the domains the key reaches.
  For a key a sign-in made, we read each domain you add with the key first.
  If the key does not reach one, we name it and save nothing.
  A key your customer made can only lose domains.
  It lists the ones it has, never asks the provider, and is never given more.
  See [What each kind of key allows](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts#what-each-kind-of-account-allows).
- **Applications that may use it.** A key a sign-in made belongs to one application. An admin or an owner ticks the others that may use it. A pasted key serves every application, and a customer's key only its own. [Which applications may use it](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts#which-applications-may-use-it).
- **Connect more domains.** Lists what the key reaches that you have not connected, and any other domain you type. Choose the application and connect; each domain says what happened.
- **Check now.** Reads the key at the provider and says it works, was refused, or could not be reached. A key is checked once a day by itself.
- **Sign in again.** This is for a key a sign-in made.
  You sign in to the provider again over the page, and **Use the new key** swaps it in.
  It keeps the same account, with its name, domains and settings.
  We name the old key and link the provider's page where you delete it.
  You replace a pasted key with **Replace key**, by pasting a new one.
  A customer's key is renewed when your customer signs in again.
- **Remove.** It says how many domains use the account.
  They stay connected, but we can no longer repair them or remove their records.
  Once removed, it links the page at the provider that deletes the key.
- **Who holds the limit.** One line says whether the provider or CustomDomain™ keeps the key to the domains you picked.
  For example: "CustomDomain™ uses this key only for the domains you pick. GoDaddy itself lets it reach the whole account."
  See [Who enforces the limit](/docs/connect-flow/provider-accounts#who-enforces-the-limit).

An admin, an owner or the member who connected the account may do all of this.
For a key your customer made, an admin or an owner may.

Leave the field empty and the widget opens on its first screen, as before.

Every token minted here is the console's own.
It carries `console:<your member id>` as its `end_user_ref`.
The console reserves that prefix for its tests, and no customer id starts with it.
That keeps a test apart from a customer:

- The go-live list's **Run your first connection** row is done when the domain is live.
  **Connect your first customer** never is, however many domains you try.
  The owner's nudges count customers the same way.
- The console names who a domain is for on **Domains** and its client filter, the recent domains on Overview, search, and the top of the domain's page.
  In all these places the domain reads **Try it live (your member id)**, where a customer's domain shows the customer's id.
- An API key the secure sign-in makes at the DNS host belongs to that test's end user, and **Connected accounts** says so.
  It never belongs to the workspace.
  So a test never widens a key your customers' domains are served with.
  See [The API key a secure sign-in made](#provider-api-key).
- A webhook for the domain carries `user_id: "console:<member id>"`, which your
  receiver can skip.

A test minted here does not prove **Mint widget tokens on your server**: that
row needs your backend's own route. In the Sandbox the same card connects made up
`.test` names and counts for nothing; see [Sandbox](/docs/guides/test-mode).

## What the console is *not*

It is one application in your workspace.
Everything it does is an API call you could make yourself, and the [API](/docs/getting-started/api) page maps each step.
Its **Widget** and **API & SDK** pages are where the other paths begin.
