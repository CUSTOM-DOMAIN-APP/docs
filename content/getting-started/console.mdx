Use the console when the domain is **yours** and you want it to point at
something you run. If your *customers* should connect *their* domains inside
your product, that is the [widget](/docs/getting-started/widget), not this page.

Two different people connect domains, and the console shows which did:

| Who | Where | What they see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your customer | Inside your product, in the widget | The widget, in your branding. The domain appears under **Domains** as *From your product (widget)*. |
| You or a teammate | Here, in the console | The console connect flow this page describes. The domain appears as *Added in the console*. |

Your server, AI agents and MCP clients can create domains through the
[API](/docs/getting-started/api) too; those are listed as *Via the API or
agents*.

## 1. Sign up and verify

Sign up at [app.customdomain.ai](https://app.customdomain.ai/signup). A
verification email is sent and its link (valid for **one hour**) must be
clicked before you can reach the console: unverified accounts can't sign in
or create API keys. You'll see a *Check your email* screen with **Resend the
link**, and if your link expired, signing in re-sends a fresh one
automatically. Google
and GitHub follow the same rule: if the provider has not confirmed your email
address, you land on *Check your email* and the link we send finishes the sign
in. A provider that has not confirmed the address also cannot sign in as an
existing account that has it: sign in with your password, then add Google or
GitHub under **Settings, Security** (the provider must have confirmed the
address for that too).

Tick the box under the heading to agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy
Policy. Email, Google and GitHub all wait for it. If you already had an account
before the box existed, or you came in through an invite or a Google or GitHub
sign in from the sign in page, the console asks once, on a page of its own, and
then takes you where you were going.

The signup form also carries bot protection: a bot-check widget, signup rate
limits, and disposable-email-address blocking. If the bot check fails, wait a
moment and try again.

Signed up with an email and a password? Once the welcome is done, **Overview**
offers once to add Google or GitHub as another way to sign in: **Add Google**,
**Add GitHub** or **Not now**. It only connects an account that uses the same
email address as yours, and either answer is remembered on every device. You can
do the same any time under **Settings, Security**.

## 2. Tell us who the domains are for

The first time you sign in, the welcome asks one question: **Who are you
connecting domains for?** Two cards answer it, and a click on either moves on:

- **My product's users**: "I run a product or platform. My users bring their own
  domains, through our widget or API."
- **My clients**: "I'm an agency or freelancer. I connect and look after domains
  for the businesses I work for."

Under the cards, the link **Just connecting a domain of my own?** is the third
answer. Each answer sets up only the steps it needs, and the question must be
answered. Every step after it has **Skip to dashboard** at the bottom right,
which takes you to the console; what is left stays on the checklist on
**Overview**, and **Change my answers** there opens the welcome again. A step
whose own button does the work also has **Not now**, which moves on to the next step.

| You pick | The steps after the question |
| --- | --- |
| **My product's users** | Your product's website (see below), your brand with a live preview on the real widget, then who writes the code: **I am writing the code** or **My AI writes the code**. The same screen shows your application ID and your default key to copy. Then the widget code, or for your AI, the setup for the agent you use and one prompt to copy (it has your application ID, never your key). That is the last step |
| **My clients** | A box to paste your domains into, with a client's name beside each (or one domain, if that is all you have). See [Many domains, and whose they are](#many-domains-and-whose-they-are) |
| **Just connecting a domain of my own?** | Your domain, with an option to set up email on it in the same step |

**I am writing the code** shows the widget code in four steps, each with a copy button: your key, a token route on your server, the widget for your framework with your application ID filled in, and the result. Then pick where to keep building, or **Try it now**.

**What's your product's website?** is the step after **My product's users**. The
address is filled in from a work email (not from free mail such as Gmail), and the
page is read in the background while you type, so **Continue** works at any time.
What it finds comes back as one sentence you can correct, such as "Looks like Acme
is a website builder. Right?", with **Yes** and **Not quite**. A sentence we are
sure of shows as already confirmed. **Not quite**, **No website yet**, or a site
that says too little opens one optional line instead: "In a few words, what does
Acme do?" What kind of product it is sets the widget options in your install code,
the example customer domain in the brand preview, the prompt for your AI and the
guide the code step links to. Your product name sits under it, filled in from the
website or your email address; your customers see it on the widget.

The first screen asks nothing about AI agents. With **My product's users**, **My AI
writes the code** is the agent path: pick the agent you use (Claude Code, Claude
Desktop, Codex, Cursor, VS Code, ChatGPT or another), add CustomDomain™ to it with a
command or a few fields, and copy the prompt. With **My clients** or a domain of
your own, the last screen offers **Let an AI agent connect domains** ("It only does
what you approve."), which opens the console's **MCP server** page with the setup
for your agent. Most agents sign in with your account, so there is no key to paste,
and an agent counts as connected only once it signed in and you approved what it may
do. See [MCP: drive it from an agent](/docs/getting-started/mcp#set-up-an-agent-from-the-welcome)
and [Agents](/docs/agents/overview).

Pointing a domain at a website needs the Growth plan: CustomDomain™ does not host
websites, it serves your domain through its edge to a site hosted elsewhere
(see [Where domains point](/docs/connect-flow/routing)). Once your own domain is
connected, the last screen says so beside **Point it at my website**. Connecting
the domain does not need it.

Everything else that can be worked out is filled in for you to confirm: your logo,
name and brand color are read from your website, and for your own domain the welcome
offers the one your work email is at. Change anything that is off.

The domain step finds where the domain is registered and hosted as soon as you
type it, says whether we can set it up automatically or will show you the
records, and opens the same connect widget your customers use. If it is not
registered yet, it lists names close to it that you can buy, with the first year
price, and links to the console's domain search to buy one. If you have no domain
yet, **I don't have a domain yet** offers that search, or carrying on to connect
one when you have it. When something is left, it says what and who can do it:
anyone with access to your DNS host can finish it.

Your place and answers are saved on every step, so closing the tab and coming
back resumes where you left off. Each step has its own address
(`/welcome?step=brand`), so a refresh stays on it and the browser's Back goes
where the **Back** button does, and a domain you already connected shows as
connected. The last screen shows what you have and the one next action, with a
few more to start with. While a domain waits for its DNS records it also offers to
ping you when it is live, with **Notify me** and **Not now**: your browser asks only
when you click, and it turns push on for a domain going live and nothing else (see
[Notifications](/docs/guides/notifications)). Open the welcome again any time to change who the domains
are for: from the first row of the checklist on **Overview**, or from **Change my
answers** under the go-live list if you picked **My product's users**.

A short tour of the console follows. Replay it any time from the setup
checklist on **Overview**. The checklist follows what you picked: for your own
domain, connecting it (and setting up email, or pointing it at your site, if you
chose those); for a few clients, connecting the first domain; always ending with
inviting your team. For clients or your own domain, a **Your site is live** row also
appears while a live domain has nothing to serve: it points at our edge, but no app
address is set, so its visitors see an error page. The row stays open until every live
domain serves something. If you chose **Let an AI agent connect domains**, an optional
**Connect your agent** row is done when an agent has signed in and been approved, and
says so by name; an agent that uses a key leaves nothing to see, so the row says that
too. If you picked **My product's users**, the checklist is the go-live list below, and
it ends with the same optional row when the welcome found your product is an AI app or
agent builder.

### Your go-live list (My product's users) [#your-go-live-list-my-customers]

If you run a product and want your users to bring their own domains, **Overview**
shows a go-live list. A row is done when something has happened that proves it,
not when you visited a page, and each row says what was checked:

| Row | Done when |
| --- | --- |
| **Start your free trial** (owners and admins) | Billing is set up and a trial or a plan is running. A card starts the 14 day trial and nothing is charged until it ends. Creating API keys needs it |
| **Run your first connection** (then **Try the widget with your own domain**) | A domain connected through the widget is live, including one you connect from [**Try it live**](#try-it-live). A real domain counts as one of your domains, which is why this row says so. **Start** opens four ways to begin: see the widget as your customers do, connect a domain you own, try the whole flow in test mode on made up `.test` names (they use nothing), or copy a prompt for your AI agent with your application ID. The same choice opens once by itself after the welcome; **Skip** closes it for good, and the row stays open until a real domain is live |
| **Mint widget tokens on your server** | Your backend asked for a widget token with an API key (the route from the **Install** tab, written for your backend). A token minted from the console's **Try it live** does not count: it is the console's own, minted for you and not for a customer |
| **Choose where customers' domains point** | Your own infrastructure is set as the target, or a default origin is set for the edge (which needs the Growth plan) |
| **Get told when a domain goes live** | A webhook delivery was answered with a success status |
| **Make the widget yours** (optional) | A look was saved, for the workspace or for an application |
| **Connect your first customer** | A real customer connected a domain through your widget, which is a connection that carries the customer id your server passed when it minted the token. A domain you connect from **Try it live** carries the console's own id for you (`console:<member>`), so it never counts as a customer, however many you try |

Test mode is a separate application you switch to from the application switcher, marked
**Test mode** wherever it appears. It needs no card, it is not counted or billed, and
its domains show on **Domains** and **Activity** only while you are switched to it. See
[Test mode](/docs/guides/test-mode) for what each ending does and what it leaves out.

A row can be done and still warn: a default origin that failed its check, or a
webhook whose newest delivery failed, each with a link to fix it. The list stays
on **Overview** while any row warns, even when every required row is done, and it
reads again when you come back to the tab, because most of these steps are
finished somewhere else. A customer who connects makes the earlier rows done as
well, since they cannot have connected without the widget opening and your server
minting a token. A token minted straight at the API with your client secret is
not seen by the console; your first customer proves it instead.

The list follows what the workspace's owner or an admin said: a teammate you invite
sees the same go-live list (without the trial row, which is for owners and admins).
In a workspace for clients or for your own domain, a teammate sees the usual
checklist until they answer the welcome too. Your customers' domains are on
**Domains**, on the **From your product (widget)** tab; the list itself opens on
**All**.

Every page opens with one line on what it is for and a **Docs** link to the part of
these docs that explains it. A **?** beside a word that decides what you do next

and a link. The words the console uses are listed in the [Glossary](/docs/concepts/glossary).

### Navigation

**Settings → General → Navigation** picks which menu everyone in the workspace sees.
**Classic** is the menu this page describes. **New (preview)** puts search first, shows
fewer menu items, and on a phone adds a bar along the bottom of the screen; every page
is still there, one tap away. An owner or admin chooses it for the whole workspace: it
changes at once for them and on the next page load for everyone else. While we hold a
workspace on the classic menu, the card says so and the choice waits.

### API keys and widget tokens

Open **API & SDK**. Every workspace has a **default key** at the top of the page,
made with the workspace and needing no card: owners can view and rotate it at any
time, and everyone else sees its prefix (see [API keys](/docs/authentication/overview)). For another key, press
**Create key**, give it a name and pick an **Environment**: Production
(`cd_live_…`), Development or Staging (both `cd_test_…`). The full key is shown
once ("Copy your new key now"); only a hash is kept. Admins and owners can create
and revoke keys; for anyone else **Create key** and **Revoke** are off, and the page
says why. Creating one needs a card on file, which starts the 14 day free trial
(nothing is charged until it ends). Your first ten domains are free without a
card, but another key is not, so a workspace without a card that presses **Create
key** sees "Start your free trial to create API keys" with an **Add a card**
link, and the key is created once the card is in.

The application the console made for you has a client secret, but the console
keeps it sealed and never shows it. To mint widget tokens from your server, send
one of these keys to the console's `POST https://app.customdomain.ai/api/widget-token`;
the **Install** tab on **Widget** writes the code for you. Do not rotate that
application's secret: the console's copy would go stale and the preview, **Try it
live** and that endpoint would stop working. See
[Widget tokens](/docs/authentication/widget-tokens).

Optional surfaces (the activity log, DNS monitoring, buying domains, the MCP
server and agent access) are off until you need them: switch them on under
**Settings → Features**. Owners and admins can switch them on; a member or viewer
who opens a page that is off is told who can, with a link to that feature's docs,
and is not offered a button. A workspace that already used one keeps it on, and
agent access switches on by itself when you approve an agent. Each switch shows or
hides a page and nothing else: **DNS monitoring** shows the **Monitor** page, and
every live domain is checked, with alerts on drift, whether it is on or off. **Buy
domains** shows **Buy a domain**, where you search for a name and buy it yourself.

### The console follows who the domains are for

The answer to the welcome's first question is kept for the whole workspace, and the
console lays itself out by it. Nothing is hidden or removed: every page is still one
click away. The answer changes the order of the menu, what the main button does, and
what **Overview** and **Domains** lead with.

| | My product's users | My clients | A domain of my own |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Menu** | Overview, Widget, Domains, Analytics, then API & SDK, Routing and Connected accounts | Overview, Domains, Connected accounts, Analytics, then Routing. Widget and API & SDK are under **Integrate**: click its name to open it | Overview, Domains, Analytics, then Routing and Connected accounts. Widget and API & SDK are under **Integrate** |
| **Main button** | **Open the widget** | **Connect a domain**, straight to your own domain: it does not ask who is connecting it | The same |
| **Overview** | The go-live list, then the domains that need attention (when any do), your customers' latest connections and the widget's week | The domains that need attention first, and which DNS providers they are with | With one domain, that domain: its status and the next step. With two or more, the counts and the domains that need attention (when any do) |
| **Empty Domains page** | "Your customers' domains appear here", with the widget as the way on | "Add your domains", with a button to paste a list | "Connect your domain" |

The counts on **Overview** include every domain of every application, read from the
same list **Domains** shows, and each count is the number its filter lists there.
**Domains** opens on **All** whoever the domains are for, so a count on **Overview**
opens the domains it counted, and a domain you look for is there whichever way it
was connected. The source tabs narrow the list from there. The first-run tour
follows the same layout. A workspace that has not said who its domains are for
gets the console as it has always been.

A domain **needs attention** when its setup failed or stopped making progress, when
its records were changed at the provider, or when it is still being set up 48 hours
after it was added. Setup has no deadline, so a domain still waiting keeps being
checked; it is listed so that somebody looks. A live domain needs attention too when
its certificate is failing or about to expire, when a change to its records was left
for you to fix, when its provider stopped accepting our access, or when it is **live,
but has nothing to serve**: it points at our edge, but no app address is set, so its
visitors see an error page. The console uses this one rule for the **Needs attention**
count and card on **Overview** (the card is there in every mode when a domain needs
attention, and each row says why, such as "Waiting for DNS, 6 days" or "HTTPS
certificate: The certificate expired."), the **Needs attention** filter and the
**Needs attention first** order on **Domains**, and the counts per client. A live domain
that needs attention is under both **Live** and **Needs attention**.

Change it any time under **Settings → What this workspace is for**, where the three
answers are **My product's users**, **My clients** and **A domain of my own**. Owners and admins
can change it; it changes the console for everyone in the workspace, and everyone else
sees it read only. A teammate you invite sees the same console you do, whatever they
answer in the welcome.

The **bell** at the top right holds your latest notifications: a domain going
live, a domain that needs attention, a failed payment, and more. Turn on push
under **Settings → Notifications** to get them on your phone or computer too. See
[Notifications](/docs/guides/notifications).

### Applications

The **Application** switcher at the top of the sidebar is always there, even
with a single application. It lists your applications and offers **New
application** and **Manage applications**. Use more than one to keep surfaces
apart: Production and Staging, or one per product. Each application has its
own widget credentials, API keys and domains; switching is remembered in your
browser. Pages that list or count show every application: **Overview**,
**Domains** (narrow it with its application filter), **Analytics** (all
applications to start with) and the keys on **API & SDK**, each key named with
its application. Pages that set one up act on the active application: **Widget**,
**Domain routing**, **Connect a domain** (a new domain lands in it) and adding a
webhook endpoint. An API key works across the whole workspace (a test mode
application's keys work only there), and a domain it connects lands in the key's
application unless the call names another.

Viewers see applications and their domains but cannot create one or use its
widget, and the console says so. Buying a domain, bulk connect and the widget's
token need the `member` role or above.

**New application** asks for a name (with one-tap suggestions such as
*Staging*) and an environment (Production, Staging or Development). Once it
exists you can switch to it or go straight to its widget.

Each application has its own page: open it from **Applications** or from the
switcher. Its summary counts the application's domains by status, and its
**Domains** (the ones that need attention first, each opening its details),
**API keys**, **Routing** and **Settings** (the widget, **Rename**, **Switch to
it**, **DNS hosting** and **Delete application**) are tabs. Deleting asks you to type the name, revokes the application's
API keys, and is refused for your workspace's default application and for one
that still has domains (disconnect them first). An application created through
the API rather than in the console works the same, except that its widget
can't be previewed here: its client secret was issued to whoever created it
and was never stored in the console, so mint its tokens with `POST /v1/tokens`
and that secret.

#### DNS hosting [#domain-maintenance]

An application's **Settings** tab has a **DNS hosting** section (it was called
**Domain maintenance**): your
platform keeps your users' domains working for them (records stay correct,
certificates renew, problems get fixed) once each user agrees in the widget. One
setting where the user bought the domain changes, its *nameservers*, which say
who runs the domain's DNS. The guide is
[the DNS hosting guide (nameserver migration)](/docs/guides/nameserver-migration).
A test mode application has none. The section has:

- **Host your users' DNS**, a switch, off by default. Nothing changes for
  your users until you **Save**; once saved, each user is asked when they connect
  a domain.
- **Before and after**: a user's domain as two parts. Where it is registered (paid
  for) stays; what keeps it working (its DNS) moves to whoever runs it. A **?**
  beside DNS, registrar and nameservers gives one plain line and a link to the
  guide.
- **Who runs it**: **CustomDomain™** (recommended, the default; nothing to set up)
  or **Your own account** (pick one of your connected Cloudflare, Amazon Route 53,
  Google Cloud DNS or Azure DNS accounts; only this application uses it). Under
  **Advanced**, **Use your own nameservers instead** takes 2 to 13 nameservers, one
  per line.
- **Show it as the first choice**: users see it first, and adding records stays
  one tap away. Off, it is a quiet second choice.
- **Hosted domains**, under the section once it is on: how many are
  hosted, being set up and need attention, the typical setup time, why some
  stopped, and one row per domain (who runs it, its status in plain words, the
  records copied, when it was last checked), each opening the domain.

The API calls the switches `mode` (`off`, `offer`, `prefer`) and **Who runs it**
`target`; the guide has [how they map](/docs/guides/nameserver-migration#turn-it-on).

A hosted domain shows a **DNS hosting** section on its **DNS** tab: its
status, three steps (**Copying settings**, **Waiting for the registrar**,
**Live**), the next thing to do, and, when its settings could only be read from
outside, what to check and **The records look right, continue**
([the copy](/docs/guides/nameserver-migration#the-copy)). It names the registrar,
where the nameservers are changed, only when it is known, and shows where its
records are, its nameservers under **Technical details**, and the ways back:
**Move back**, and **Move back to GoDaddy's DNS** (the registrar's own DNS) beside
it, or in its place once the old nameservers no longer answer
([what moving back does](/docs/guides/nameserver-migration#moving-back)).

## 3. Connect a domain

**Connect a domain** (the sidebar button, **Overview** or **Domains**) first
asks who is connecting it:

- **Connect a domain yourself**: your own site, or one you're setting up for
  a customer. You add the DNS records. This is the console path, and the
  rest of this page.
- **Let your customers connect theirs**: takes you to the **Widget** page to
  embed it in your product.

Links that already say which (a domain's **Resume** link, or **Connect a
domain yourself** on Overview) skip the question. The connect page then says
plainly that you are on the console path. Enter the hostname:

- `www.acme.com` / `app.acme.com`: a subdomain. Any DNS provider can hold the
  `CNAME` we need.
- `acme.com`: the root. It works everywhere, but the record depends on the
  provider; see [Root domains](/docs/dns/root-domains).

The pre-flight refuses two things up front: a domain that doesn't resolve at
all (unregistered, or never delegated at the registrar) and a hosting
platform's own subdomain (`www.<name>.vercel.app`). Both used to sit on
"waiting for DNS" for days.

## 4. Choose how to add the records

The options shown are the truth for *your* provider, straight from the server.
They are the [ways to connect](/docs/concepts/glossary#ways-to-connect) that fit
the domain. The recommended one is open; the others are closed one-line rows under
**Other ways to connect**, in this order:

1. **Sign in with the provider**: Cloudflare, DigitalOcean, DNSimple, Linode,
   Netlify, Vercel (by installing the CustomDomain™ integration) and
   WordPress.com, plus **Approve access at Porkbun**. Approve once in a popup
   and we add the records. Fastest. **Keep this sign-in** starts ticked: we
   keep it, encrypted, so we can put the records back and remove them with the
   domain; untick it to use the sign-in once. Sign in with Azure DNS
   (a Microsoft account) and Sign in with Dynadot or Unstoppable Domains
   (through the provider's MCP server) are used once. OVHcloud's consent page
   and the other MCP server sign-ins are built but not switched on yet; see
   [DNS providers](/docs/dns/providers#built-but-not-switched-on-yet).
2. **One-click setup**: only where the provider serves our template
   (NameSilo, Glauca Digital, Domain Chief today). After you approve the change
   there, the provider sends you back here. GoDaddy, IONOS, Aruba,
   Squarespace, Cloudflare and Vercel are *not* offered this, on purpose: they
   have not onboarded our templates.
3. **Secure sign-in**: at Namecheap, GoDaddy, IONOS,
   Name.com, Squarespace, Hover, Spaceship and Hostinger, where neither of the
   two above runs. **Open secure sign-in** opens the provider's own sign-in in a
   secure window; you approve the exact changes and we add the records. See
   [Secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in).
4. **API key**: paste a scoped provider credential, one labeled field per part,
   with a link to where it is created, the steps and the one requirement that
   matters; anything else, and the provider's notes, sit under a closed
   **Details**. **Keep it as a connected account** starts ticked: the key is kept
   encrypted, as an account you can remove under **Connected accounts**, so we
   can fix or remove the records later (untick it to use the key once). If we
   cannot keep it, the records are still written, the key is used once, and the
   page says so. Any
   GoDaddy account that holds a domain can create a Personal Access Token;
   Namecheap's API needs an eligible account and our server's IP on its
   allowlist. Route 53 offers an access role first, with no key to paste.
5. **Add the records** by hand: the exact rows to copy, with a link to your
   provider's DNS panel. Then **Check now**.
6. **DNS hosting**: you change the domain's nameservers once, where it was
   bought, and CustomDomain™ writes every record after that. It is not a row on
   this page: it is the **DNS hosting** card at the foot of the domain's **DNS**
   tab, and the application's **DNS hosting** setting decides whether your
   customers are offered it in the widget ([above](#domain-maintenance)). The
   domain check reports the same verdict as `rails.nameservers`. See
   [Managed DNS](/docs/dns/managed-dns).

Not the one with DNS access? Under the records, **Send these records to a
teammate** emails them to whoever is. Replies go to you.

- An **owner or admin** sending to someone who isn't in the workspace also
  invites them as a member; the email carries the records and the invite.
- Someone **already in the workspace** gets the records and a link back to
  this domain's setup in the console.
- A **member** can send the records but not invite: the email links a
  single-use guided setup that needs no account.
- **Viewers** can't send email from the console.

Someone outside your team, such as a client who has the DNS? Use **Copy a link
for someone outside your team** instead. It makes a setup link for this one
domain that works with no account and invites nobody; see
[A link for the client](#a-link-for-the-client).

## 5. Verify

The connection sits in `pending` until the records resolve, then goes
`live`. Records written for you (a sign-in, one-click setup or an API key)
usually verify within seconds: a new connection is checked every five seconds
at first, and when public DNS still has an old answer, we ask the domain's
own nameservers instead. You can leave and come back:
**Domains** shows every connection with its reason and a **Resume** link.
After three minutes of waiting the page offers a fallback. A stalled
connection is *paused*, not failed: it keeps being checked every six hours.

Every domain in your workspace opens on its detail page, whichever
application it belongs to; the page names the application. A domain that
isn't live leads with **What happened**: what the last attempt did (it timed
out, the provider refused a key, it is still waiting for the records, or it is
connected somewhere else), the next steps in order, and the action that fits:
**Finish setup**, **Retry** (which restarts a failed attempt), or removing an
attempt nobody will finish. Below it, a short history says when the domain
was added and through what, and when its DNS was last checked.

While a domain is waiting, its detail page offers **Get a ping when it is live**
(owners and admins), so you can close the tab and be told the moment it connects;
see [Notifications](/docs/guides/notifications). It also opens **What public DNS shows
right now**: each record's Name to type, whether public DNS has it, what
answers instead ("still points to Squarespace"), and the mistakes that block
a connection even when a record looks right, such as the full hostname typed
into the Name field or Cloudflare's proxy turned on. It is the same check the
widget runs for your customers (`GET /v1/connections/{id}/diagnose`), and the
page to send a customer whose domain is stuck.

## 6. Serve something

Live means the edge terminates TLS and proxies the domain to your
application's **origin** (**Edge serving**). If you haven't set one, the domain shows a "not
configured" page: set it under **Routing**, the page titled **Where domains
point**, as the **Application address**, or point the domains straight at your own servers
there instead. See [Where domains point](/docs/connect-flow/routing). Root-domain redirects
(`www_redirect`) and per-path rules live on the connection's detail page.

## 7. See its traffic

Once the CustomDomain™ edge serves a domain, its detail page has a
**Traffic** tab: requests by the kind of answer visitors got (success,
redirects, client errors, server errors), bandwidth sent to visitors, how
fast your origin answered (p95 and median time to first byte), approximate
visitors, and when the last request arrived, over 24 hours, 7, 30 or 90 days.
**Analytics** has a **Traffic** tab for the whole workspace (or one
application), with the busiest domains.

Only traffic that passes through the edge is counted. A domain connected for
DNS only points at your own servers, so its visitors never reach us; its page
says so instead of showing zeros, and your hosting has those numbers.

Counts arrive about a minute after each visit. No visitor's IP address is
stored: visitors are estimated from a sketch of keyed hashes, and someone who
visits on two days counts once on each. Hourly detail is kept 14 days and
daily totals 400 days. The API has the same figures:
`GET /v1/connections/{id}/traffic` and `GET /v1/traffic`.

## Who opens the widget, and secure sign-in

Two more panels on **Analytics** appear once there is something to count. Both
follow the date range and the application picker, like the rest of the page.

- **Devices and places**, on the **Widget** tab: the widget sessions by device
  (desktop, phone, tablet), top countries and browsers, each a ranked list, and
  how often a session on each kind of device connected its domain. Counts only:
  no IP address, city or single session is ever shown. See
  [what a widget session records](/docs/widget-sdk/reference#what-a-widget-session-records).
- **Secure sign-in**, on the **Providers** tab, when your customers used
  [secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in): sessions started, signed in and
  done, the average time to sign in and to done, a table per DNS provider
  (started, signed in, changes shown, applied, done), and why the rest stopped
  ("Human check on screen", "Code or approval never came", "Customer cancelled"
  and so on), counted without and with a proxy in the customer's country. A reason
  a proxy could change is marked **A proxy can change this**.

The API has both: `audience` and `secure_sign_in` on `GET /v1/analytics/connect`.

### The API key a secure sign-in made [#provider-api-key]

When a secure sign-in made a
[lasting API key](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in#lasting-api-keys) at a domain's DNS
provider, the domain's page has a **Provider API key** card:

- One line on where the key stands, and its status: **Working**, **Ends soon**,
  **Needs the end user to sign in again** or **Ended**.
- When it ends, its name at the provider, when it was checked, and when it was
  renewed and a reconnect link last went out.
- **What it can do**, when the key reaches more than the domain's DNS, in a
  sentence: "This key can also transfer the domain away from Spaceship, and make
  charges on the account (renewals and purchases)."
- **Send reconnect link** emails your customer a link to sign in again when you
  gave us their address, and copies the link otherwise. **Copy reconnect link**
  copies one to pass on. A link works once, for 14 days
  ([reconnecting](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in#reconnect)).
- After a renewal, a note that the old key still exists at the provider, with a
  link to the provider's keys page to revoke it there.

**Connected accounts** lists such a key with the badge **Made at sign in**. Its
sheet shows the domain, when the key ends, its name, scopes, when it was checked
and the account's name or number at the provider where the page showed one, never the key. It
belongs to one application and serves only that domain, and it is renewed by your
customer's sign-in, never by pasting a key, so its only action is **Remove**.
Removing it deletes CustomDomain™'s copy; the key itself stays at the provider
until it is revoked there. A domain's timeline says when the key was made,
renewed, nearing its end or refused.

## Many domains, and whose they are

If you manage domains for clients, or have a lot of your own, you don't have to
connect them one at a time. After **My clients**, the welcome's next screen is a
box: paste a column from a spreadsheet, a line of addresses or
a list of links, in any mix. The same box is on **Domains** under **Add
several**, up to 200 at a time.

- **What it reads.** An address gives its host, a repeat is added once and
  counted, and anything that is not a domain is listed and left out. Nothing you
  typed is thrown away: what could not be added stays in the box.
- **Whose it is.** A name typed above the box is shown next to every domain
  below it. A name after a comma or a tab on a line (`beta.org, Beta Bakery`) is
  used for that domain instead. A domain already in your workspace is left as it
  was. The name is set when the domain is added and cannot be changed afterwards,
  so to rename a client or give a domain to another, remove the domain and add it
  again.
- **The first ten domains are free.** A new workspace connects its first ten
  domains without a card, and the rest once the free trial has started. The box
  says so before you paste, with **Start free trial** beside it (nothing is
  charged for 14 days). It adds what it can now and keeps the rest in the box,
  and after the trial starts you land back on the same page with the rest still
  there. A teammate who is not an owner or an admin is told to ask one of them.
- **Adding is not connecting.** A list puts the domains in your workspace, each
  waiting for its DNS records. Finish each the way you would finish one, from its
  row on **Domains**, or send the step to the person who has the DNS (below).

On **Domains**, a workspace for clients says **client** where the others say
**customer**:

- **Client** narrows the list to one client's domains, or to those with no
  client.
- **Order** shows newest first (the default), oldest first, domain, client, or
  what needs attention first.
- **Export CSV** saves exactly the rows shown, in the order shown: domain,
  status, client, DNS host, when it was added and from where, the application,
  and what is left to do. It opens in a spreadsheet. A client name that starts
  with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` is saved as text, so a spreadsheet never runs it.

A domain's own page says "For Acme Dental" in a workspace for clients, where it
would say who connected it elsewhere.

### A link for the client

**Send these records to a teammate** also invites its recipient to the workspace
when an owner or an admin sends it, which is wrong for a client who has the DNS.
**Copy a link for the client** (in the ⋯ menu of a row on **Domains**, and beside
the email option on the connect step; it reads **Copy a setup link** in other
workspaces) makes a link for that one domain instead:

- Whoever opens it finishes that domain with no account, and nobody is invited.
  The page carries your workspace's name and nothing about its billing.
- It works once and lasts 30 days. It cannot be listed or cancelled afterwards,
  so make it when you are ready to send it. The console shows it on the page as
  well as copying it.
- It is for a domain that is not live yet, and it needs the member role or
  above. A workspace can make 30 an hour.

## Design the widget

**Widget → Design** is where the widget gets your look. The preview is the
widget itself, the one your customers get, running on example data: nothing
here is sent anywhere, and no domain or connection is needed.

Step through the journey with the row above the preview (enter the domain,
detect the provider, sign in, apply, connected), open the other screens under
**More screens** (existing records, manual setup, email records, an error and
more), and pick the steps of a checklist. Each screen says which status it
reports to your code. Look at the widget in light or dark, on a computer or a
phone, and in any language it speaks.

Under **Ways to connect** is the screen a person gets for each way a domain can
be connected, so you can see them all without a domain that needs each one:

| Screen | What the person sees |
| --- | --- |
| **Secure sign-in** | A provider with no sign-in of its own (the example is IONOS): **Open secure sign-in**, which opens a window we run where they sign in. |
| **One-click setup** | A provider that applies the change itself after one approval (the example is NameSilo): **Continue at** the provider. |
| **DNS hosting: offered first** | Your DNS hosting setting is **Offered first**, so the question comes before the records: the consent below. |
| **DNS hosting: consent** | What they agree to before we run their domain's DNS: the nameservers now, and **Yes, set it up**. |
| **DNS hosting: moving** | The steps while it moves (copy settings, security check, make the change, live), here waiting for a sign-in where they bought the domain. |
| **Reconnect** | One sign-in that renews the access we keep for a domain. |

Their buttons only say that this is a preview: no window opens and nothing is
sent. A link to the Design tab can name one of these screens with `stage=`
(`secure-sign-in`, `one-click`, `dns-hosting-first`, `dns-hosting`,
`dns-hosting-move` or `reconnect`), so another page can open the preview on it.

| Group | What you set |
| --- | --- |
| **Brand** | The name in the first heading ("Connect your domain to …"), the logo, a separate logo for dark mode (or a light plate behind a logo drawn for light pages, which the designer offers in one click when a logo would be hard to see on the dark surface), the logo's size (16 to 48 pixels, 32 by default), and whether "Powered by CustomDomain™" shows. |
| **Colors** | The brand color for buttons, links and focus rings. Text on it is chosen to be readable, and it is lifted in dark mode when it would vanish. An optional background and text color, used in both light and dark. |
| **Shape and type** | One corner radius (buttons, fields, cards and the pop-up follow it in proportion), a system font, the pop-up's width on a computer, and its shadow. |
| **Behavior** | Light, dark or match the device; the confetti; the close button; the progress steps. |
| **Screens and options** | "Add the records instead" on the sign-in screen, "Let an AI assistant do it" and "Email these instructions". It also reads out the application's DNS hosting setting (**DNS hosting: Off**, **Offered** or **Offered first**), with **Change in Settings** to its [Domain maintenance](#domain-maintenance) section: that setting, not the widget, decides whether users are offered DNS hosting. |

**Each application has its own look.** The designer edits the application
chosen in the sidebar. **Save** gives that application a look of its own.
**Save as the workspace default** (under the ⋯ menu) sets the look every
application without its own uses, and **Use the workspace default for this
application** drops the application's own look. Saving needs an owner or admin.
The install code and **Try it live** use the saved look; the preview shows what
you have not saved yet. The install code carries only what differs from the
widget's own look, so an untouched color or corner radius is not written into it.

For the logo, **Upload** (or drop a file on it). PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF (its
first frame), AVIF and SVG are accepted. Your browser prepares every upload the
same way, so logos look consistent: fully transparent edges are trimmed, the
image is fit inside 512 by 160 pixels (256 by 80 on screen at 2x), and it is
saved as a PNG under 200 KB. An SVG is converted to a PNG; the SVG itself is
never stored. Each logo is shown on the surface it will sit on, and you'll see a
warning when it would be hard to see there, or when it has a solid background
that would show as a box. The upload is hosted at once and appears in the
preview; it belongs to the application when you press **Save**.

Uploaded logos are served from `https://app.customdomain.ai/brand/logo/…`
at a URL named for the file's content, so it never changes and is cached
for a year. **Replace** uploads a new one; **Remove** clears it from the design.
Earlier uploads stay available at their URLs (your 20 most recent, and any an
application still uses), because a widget you already shipped keeps the logo
URL it was pasted with until you update its code. You can still paste the URL of
a logo you host yourself (HTTPS) instead.

## Try it live

**Widget → Try it live** opens the real widget, the one your customers get, over
the console page, with the look you saved and a short-lived token the console
mints for you. Everything it connects is real: the domain shows under
**Domains** and counts as one of your domains.

Type a domain you own in **Domain to try** and press **Open the widget**. The
token is then bound to that domain, and the widget starts on it, so the whole
flow runs as it does for a customer who arrived with a domain: the provider is
found, and where the DNS host offers it, the
[secure sign-in](/docs/guides/secure-sign-in) is offered (**Open secure
sign-in**), which only runs for a token bound to the domain and to the person
using it. Nothing changes at your DNS host until you approve it. Leave the field
empty and the widget opens on its first screen, as before.

Every token minted here is the console's own: it carries `console:<your member
id>` as its `end_user_ref`, a prefix the console reserves for its tests and no
customer id starts with. That is what keeps a test apart from a customer:

- The go-live list's **Run your first connection** row is done when the domain
  is live, and **Connect your first customer** never is, however many domains
  you try. The owner's nudges count customers the same way.
- Wherever the console names who a domain is for (**Domains** and its client
  filter, the recent domains on the Overview, search, and the top of the domain's
  own page), the domain is labelled **Try it live (your member id)** where a
  customer's domain shows the customer's id.
- An API key the secure sign-in makes at the DNS host belongs to that test's end
  user (**Connected accounts** says so), never to the workspace, so a test never
  widens a key your customers' domains are served with. See
  [The API key a secure sign-in made](#provider-api-key).
- A webhook for the domain carries `user_id: "console:<member id>"`, which your
  receiver can skip.

A test minted here does not prove **Mint widget tokens on your server**: that
row needs your backend's own route. In test mode the same card connects made up
`.test` names and counts for nothing; see [Test mode](/docs/guides/test-mode).

## What the console is *not*

It is one application in your workspace. Everything it does is an API call
you could make yourself (the [API](/docs/getting-started/api) page maps each
step), and its **Widget** and **API & SDK** pages are where the other paths
begin.
