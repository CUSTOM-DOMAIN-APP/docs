description: The no-code path, from sign-up to a domain serving your app over HTTPS, and what every screen is actually doing.
---

Use the console when the domain is **yours** and you want it to point at
something you run. If your *customers* should connect *their* domains inside
your product, that is the [widget](/docs/getting-started/widget), not this page.

Two different people connect domains, and the console shows which did:

| Who | Where | What they see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your customer | Inside your product, in the widget | The widget, in your branding. The domain appears under **Domains** as *From your product (widget)*. |
| You or a teammate | Here, in the console | The console connect flow this page describes. The domain appears as *Added in the console*. |

Your server, AI agents and MCP clients can create domains through the
[API](/docs/getting-started/api) too; those are listed as *Via the API or
agents*.

## 1. Sign up and verify

Sign up at [app.customdomain.ai](https://app.customdomain.ai/signup). A
verification email is sent and its link (valid for **one hour**) must be
clicked before you can reach the console: unverified accounts can't sign in
or create API keys. You'll see a *Check your email* screen with **Resend the
link**, and if your link expired, signing in re-sends a fresh one
automatically. (Self-hosters: this strict mode is the
`REQUIRE_EMAIL_VERIFICATION` policy. The production deployment defaults it to
enforced; unset it only if you deliberately want immediate activation.) Google
and GitHub follow the same rule: if the provider has not confirmed your email
address, you land on *Check your email* and the link we send finishes the sign
in.

Tick the box under the heading to agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy
Policy. Email, Google and GitHub all wait for it. If you already had an account
before the box existed, or you came in through an invite or a Google or GitHub
sign in from the sign in page, the console asks once, on a page of its own, and
then takes you where you were going.

The signup form also carries bot protection: a bot-check widget, signup rate
limits, and disposable-email-address blocking. If the bot check fails, wait a
moment and try again.

Signed up with an email and a password? Once the welcome is done, **Overview**
offers once to add Google or GitHub as another way to sign in: **Add Google**,
**Add GitHub** or **Not now**. It only connects an account that uses the same
email address as yours, and either answer is remembered on every device. You can
do the same any time under **Settings, Security**.

## 2. Tell us who the domains are for

The first time you sign in, the welcome asks one question: who are the domains
for? It is one click, and it sets up the steps that suit you. There is no skip:
the question is short, and every step after it has **Not now**, which moves on and
leaves that item on the checklist on **Overview** for later.

| You pick | The steps after the question |
| --- | --- |
| **My customers** (you run a product and want your users to bring their own domains) | About you, your brand with a live preview on the real widget, how you will add it to your product (optional), then the widget code |
| **My clients, or many of my own** | A box to paste your domains into, with a client's name beside each (or one domain, if that is all you have). See [Many domains, and whose they are](#many-domains-and-whose-they-are) |
| **Just me** (a domain you own) | Your domain, with an option to set up email on it in the same step |
| **Not sure yet** | A short list of things to try (connect a domain of your own, preview the widget with your brand, let an AI agent connect domains), then the path of the one you pick |

Under the cards, **Also let an AI agent connect and manage domains for me** adds
one more step, **Give your agent access**, to any of them. Pick the agent you use
(Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex, Cursor, VS Code, ChatGPT or another), add
CustomDomain™ to it with a command or a few fields, and paste in the prompt the step
gives you, which says what to do first for what you told the welcome. Most agents sign
in with your account, so there is no key to paste, and the step says **connected**
only once the agent signed in and you approved what it may do. It is optional: **Not
now** moves on. See [MCP: drive it from an agent](/docs/getting-started/mcp#set-up-an-agent-from-the-welcome)
and [Agents](/docs/agents/overview).

Pointing a domain at a website needs the Growth plan: CustomDomain™ does not host
websites, it serves your domain through its edge to a site hosted elsewhere
(see [Domain routing](/docs/connect-flow/routing)). The **Just me** card says so
before you choose it. Connecting the domain does not need it.

Everything that can be worked out is filled in for you to confirm. Your company
name and website come from your sign-up email (not from free mail such as Gmail,
where it asks for the website instead), your logo, name and brand color are read
from your website, and for your own domain it offers the one your work email is
at. Change anything that is off.

The domain step finds where the domain is registered and hosted as soon as you
type it, says whether we can set it up automatically or will show you the
records, and opens the same connect widget your customers use. If it is not
registered yet, it lists names close to it that you can buy, with the first year
price, and links to the console's domain search to buy one. If you have no domain
yet, **I don't have a domain yet** offers that search, or carrying on to connect
one when you have it. When something is left, it says what and who can do it:
anyone with access to your DNS host can finish it.

Your place and answers are saved on every step, so closing the tab and coming
back resumes where you left off, and a domain you already connected shows as
connected. The last screen shows what you have and the one next action, with a
few more to start with. While a domain waits for its DNS records it also offers to
ping you when it is live, with **Notify me** and **Not now**: your browser asks only
when you click, and it turns push on for a domain going live and nothing else (see
[Notifications](/docs/guides/notifications)). Open the welcome again any time to change who the domains
are for: from the first row of the checklist on **Overview**, or from **Change my
answers** under the go-live list if you picked **My customers**.

A short tour of the console follows. Replay it any time from the setup
checklist on **Overview**. The checklist follows what you picked: for your own
domain, connecting it (and setting up email, or pointing it at your site, if you
chose those); for a few clients, connecting the first domain; always ending with
inviting your team. If you asked for an agent, an optional **Connect your agent** row
is done when an agent has signed in and been approved, and says so by name; an agent
that uses a key leaves nothing to see, so the row says that too. If you picked **My customers**, the checklist is the go-live
list below.

### Your go-live list (My customers)

If you run a product and want your users to bring their own domains, **Overview**
shows a go-live list. A row is done when something has happened that proves it,
not when you visited a page, and each row says what was checked:

| Row | Done when |
| --- | --- |
| **Start your free trial** (owners and admins) | Billing is set up and a trial or a plan is running. A card starts the 14 day trial and nothing is charged until it ends. Creating API keys needs it |
| **Try the widget with your own domain** | A domain connected through the widget is live. It is a real domain and counts as one of your domains, which is why this row says so. With no domain of your own to try it on, **Try test mode** opens the same widget on made up `.test` names; they use nothing, and the row stays open until a real domain is live |
| **Mint widget tokens on your server** | Your backend asked for a widget token with an API key (the route from the **Install** tab, written for your backend). A token minted from the console's **Try it live** does not count |
| **Choose where customers' domains point** | Your own infrastructure is set as the target, or a default origin is set for the edge (which needs the Growth plan) |
| **Get told when a domain goes live** | A webhook delivery was answered with a success status |
| **Make the widget yours** (optional) | A look was saved, for the workspace or for an application |
| **Connect your first customer** | A real customer connected a domain through your widget, which is a connection that carries the customer id your server passed when it minted the token |

Test mode is a separate application you switch to from the application switcher, marked
**Test mode** wherever it appears. It needs no card, it is not counted or billed, and
its domains show on **Domains** and **Activity** only while you are switched to it. See
[Test mode](/docs/guides/test-mode) for what each ending does and what it leaves out.

A row can be done and still warn: a default origin that failed its check, or a
webhook whose newest delivery failed, each with a link to fix it. The list stays
on **Overview** while any row warns, even when every required row is done, and it
reads again when you come back to the tab, because most of these steps are
finished somewhere else. A customer who connects makes the earlier rows done as
well, since they cannot have connected without the widget opening and your server
minting a token. A token minted straight at the API with your client secret is
not seen by the console; your first customer proves it instead.

The list follows what the workspace's owner or an admin said: a teammate you invite
sees the same go-live list (without the trial row, which is for owners and admins).
In a workspace for clients or for your own domain, a teammate sees the usual
checklist until they answer the welcome too. Your customers' domains are on
**Domains**, on the **From your product (widget)** tab, which is the tab it opens
on.

### API keys and widget tokens

Open **API & SDK**. Every workspace has a **default key** at the top of the page,
made with the workspace and needing no card: owners and admins can view it at any
time (see [API keys](/docs/authentication/overview)). For another key, press
**Create key**, give it a name and pick an **Environment**: Production
(`cd_live_…`), Development or Staging (both `cd_test_…`). The full key is shown
once ("Copy your new key now"); only a hash is kept. Admins and owners can create
keys. Creating one needs a card on file, which starts the 14 day free trial
(nothing is charged until it ends). Your first ten domains are free without a
card, but another key is not, so a workspace without a card that presses **Create
key** sees "Start your free trial to create API keys" with an **Add a card**
link, and the key is created once the card is in.

The application the console made for you has a client secret, but the console
keeps it sealed and never shows it. To mint widget tokens from your server, send
one of these keys to the console's `POST https://app.customdomain.ai/api/widget-token`;
the **Install** tab on **Widget** writes the code for you. Do not rotate that
application's secret: the console's copy would go stale and the preview, **Try it
live** and that endpoint would stop working. See
[Widget tokens](/docs/authentication/widget-tokens).

Optional surfaces (the activity log, DNS monitoring, domain sales, the MCP
server and agent access) are off until you need them: switch them on under
**Settings → Features**. A workspace that already used one keeps it on, and
agent access switches on by itself when you approve an agent.

### The console follows who the domains are for

The answer to the welcome's first question is kept for the whole workspace, and the
console lays itself out by it. Nothing is hidden or removed: every page is still one
click away. The answer changes the order of the menu, what the main button does, and
what **Overview** and **Domains** lead with.

| | My customers | My clients, or many of my own | Just me |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Menu** | Overview, Widget, Domains, Analytics, then API & SDK, Domain routing and Connected accounts | Overview, Domains, Connected accounts, Analytics, then Domain routing. Widget and API & SDK are under **Integrate**: click its name to open it | Overview, Domains, Analytics, then Domain routing and Connected accounts. Widget and API & SDK are under **Integrate** |
| **Main button** | **Open the widget** | **Connect a domain**, straight to your own domain: it does not ask who is connecting it | The same |
| **Overview** | The go-live list, then your customers' latest connections and the widget's week | The domains that need attention first, and which DNS providers they are with | With one domain, that domain: its status and the next step. With two or more, the counts |
| **Domains** | Opens on **From your product (widget)**, or on **All** until a customer has connected one | Opens on **All** | Opens on **All** |
| **Empty Domains page** | "Your customers' domains appear here", with the widget as the way on | "Connect your first domain" | "Connect your domain" |

The counts on **Overview** include every domain, so when they link to **Domains** in a
workspace for your customers they open the **All** tab, and what you click is what you
see. The first-run tour follows the same layout. A workspace that has not said who its
domains are for, or picked **Not sure yet**, gets the console as it has always been.

Change it any time under **Settings → What this workspace is for**. Owners and admins
can change it; it changes the console for everyone in the workspace, and everyone else
sees it read only. A teammate you invite sees the same console you do, whatever they
answer in the welcome.

The **bell** at the top right holds your latest notifications: a domain going
live, a domain that needs attention, a failed payment, and more. Turn on push
under **Settings → Notifications** to get them on your phone or computer too. See
[Notifications](/docs/guides/notifications).

### Applications

The **Application** switcher at the top of the sidebar is always there, even
with a single application. It lists your applications and offers **New
application** and **Manage applications**. Use more than one to keep surfaces
apart: Production and Staging, or one per product. Each application has its
own widget credentials, API keys and domains, and the console shows the
active one; switching is remembered in your browser.

**New application** asks for a name (with one-tap suggestions such as
*Staging*) and an environment (Production, Staging or Development). Once it
exists you can switch to it or go straight to its widget.

Each application has its own page: open it from **Applications** or from the
switcher. Its summary counts the application's domains by status, and its
**Domains** (the ones that need attention first, each opening its details),
**API keys**, **Routing** and **Settings** (the widget, **Rename**, **Switch to
it** and **Delete application**) are tabs. Deleting asks you to type the name, revokes the application's
API keys, and is refused for your workspace's default application and for one
that still has domains (disconnect them first). An application created through
the API rather than in the console works the same, except that its widget
can't be previewed here: its client secret was issued to whoever created it
and was never stored in the console, so mint its tokens with `POST /v1/tokens`
and that secret.

## 3. Connect a domain

**Connect a domain** (the sidebar button, **Overview** or **Domains**) first
asks who is connecting it:

- **Connect a domain yourself**: your own site, or one you're setting up for
  a customer. You add the DNS records. This is the console path, and the
  rest of this page.
- **Let your customers connect theirs**: takes you to the **Widget** page to
  embed it in your product.

Links that already say which (a domain's **Resume** link, or **Connect a
domain yourself** on Overview) skip the question. The connect page then says
plainly that you are on the console path. Enter the hostname:

- `www.acme.com` / `app.acme.com`: a subdomain. Any DNS provider can hold the
  `CNAME` we need.
- `acme.com`: the root. It works everywhere, but the record depends on the
  provider; see [Root domains](/docs/dns/root-domains).

The pre-flight refuses two things up front: a domain that doesn't resolve at
all (unregistered, or never delegated at the registrar) and a hosting
platform's own subdomain (`www.<name>.vercel.app`). Both used to sit on
"waiting for DNS" for days.

## 4. Choose how to add the records

The options shown are the truth for *your* provider, straight from the server.
They appear in this order; the recommended one is marked.

1. **Sign in with the provider**: Cloudflare, DigitalOcean, DNSimple, Linode,
   Netlify, Vercel (by installing the CustomDomain™ integration) and
   WordPress.com, plus **Approve access at Porkbun**. Approve once in a popup
   and we add the records. Fastest. **Keep this sign-in** starts ticked: we
   keep it, encrypted, so we can put the records back and remove them with the
   domain; untick it to use the sign-in once. Sign in with Google Cloud (only
   for the app's test users until Google verifies it), Sign in with Azure DNS
   (a Microsoft account) and Sign in with Dynadot or Unstoppable Domains
   (through the provider's MCP server) are used once. OVHcloud's consent page
   and the other MCP server sign-ins are built but not switched on yet; see
   [DNS providers](/docs/dns/providers#built-but-not-switched-on-yet).
2. **One-click setup**: only where the provider serves our template
   (NameSilo, Glauca Digital, Domain Chief today). After you approve the change
   there, the provider sends you back here. GoDaddy, IONOS, Aruba,
   Squarespace, Cloudflare and Vercel are *not* offered this, on purpose: they
   have not onboarded our templates.
3. **API key**: paste a scoped provider credential, one labeled field per part,
   with a link to where it is created, the steps and the one requirement that
   matters; anything else, and the provider's notes, sit under a closed
   **Details**. We keep it encrypted when
   **Remember this key** stays ticked (untick it to use the key once). Any
   GoDaddy account that holds a domain can create a Personal Access Token;
   Namecheap's API needs an eligible account and our server's IP on its
   allowlist. Route 53 offers an access role first, with no key to paste.
4. **Add the records by hand**: the exact rows to copy, with a link to your
   provider's DNS panel. Then **Check now**.

Not the one with DNS access? Under the records, **Send these records to a
teammate** emails them to whoever is. Replies go to you.

- An **owner or admin** sending to someone who isn't in the workspace also
  invites them as a member; the email carries the records and the invite.
- Someone **already in the workspace** gets the records and a link back to
  this domain's setup in the console.
- A **member** can send the records but not invite: the email links a
  single-use guided setup that needs no account.
- **Viewers** can't send email from the console.

Someone outside your team, such as a client who has the DNS? Use **Copy a link
for someone outside your team** instead. It makes a setup link for this one
domain that works with no account and invites nobody; see
[A link for the client](#a-link-for-the-client).

## 5. Verify

The connection sits in `pending` until the records resolve, then goes
`live`. Records written for you (a sign-in, one-click setup or an API key)
usually verify within seconds: a new connection is checked every five seconds
at first, and when public DNS still has an old answer, we ask the domain's
own nameservers instead. You can leave and come back:
**Domains** shows every connection with its reason and a **Resume** link.
After three minutes of waiting the page offers a fallback. A stalled
connection is *paused*, not failed: it keeps being checked every six hours.

Every domain in your workspace opens on its detail page, whichever
application it belongs to; the page names the application. A domain that
isn't live leads with **What happened**: what the last attempt did (it timed
out, the provider refused a key, it is still waiting for the records, or it is
connected somewhere else), the next steps in order, and the action that fits:
**Finish setup**, **Retry** (which restarts a failed attempt), or removing an
attempt nobody will finish. Below it, a short history says when the domain
was added and through what, and when its DNS was last checked.

While a domain is waiting, its detail page offers **Get a ping when it is live**
(owners and admins), so you can close the tab and be told the moment it connects;
see [Notifications](/docs/guides/notifications). It also opens **What public DNS shows
right now**: each record's Name to type, whether public DNS has it, what
answers instead ("still points to Squarespace"), and the mistakes that block
a connection even when a record looks right, such as the full hostname typed
into the Name field or Cloudflare's proxy turned on. It is the same check the
widget runs for your customers (`GET /v1/connections/{id}/diagnose`), and the
page to send a customer whose domain is stuck.

## 6. Serve something

Live means the edge terminates TLS and proxies the domain to your
application's **origin**. If you haven't set one, the domain shows a "not
configured" page: set it under **Domain routing**. Root-domain redirects
(`www_redirect`) and per-path rules live on the connection's detail page.

## 7. See its traffic

Once the CustomDomain™ edge serves a domain, its detail page has a
**Traffic** tab: requests by the kind of answer visitors got (success,
redirects, client errors, server errors), bandwidth sent to visitors, how
fast your origin answered (p95 and median time to first byte), approximate
visitors, and when the last request arrived, over 24 hours, 7, 30 or 90 days.
**Analytics** has a **Traffic** tab for the whole workspace (or one
application), with the busiest domains.

Only traffic that passes through the edge is counted. A domain connected for
DNS only points at your own servers, so its visitors never reach us; its page
says so instead of showing zeros, and your hosting has those numbers.

Counts arrive about a minute after each visit. No visitor's IP address is
stored: visitors are estimated from a sketch of keyed hashes, and someone who
visits on two days counts once on each. Hourly detail is kept 14 days and
daily totals 400 days. The API has the same figures:
`GET /v1/connections/{id}/traffic` and `GET /v1/traffic`.

## Many domains, and whose they are

If you manage domains for clients, or have a lot of your own, you don't have to
connect them one at a time. The welcome's first screen for **My clients, or many
of my own** is a box: paste a column from a spreadsheet, a line of addresses or
a list of links, in any mix. The same box is on **Domains** under **Add
several**, up to 200 at a time.

- **What it reads.** An address gives its host, a repeat is added once and
  counted, and anything that is not a domain is listed and left out. Nothing you
  typed is thrown away: what could not be added stays in the box.
- **Whose it is.** A name typed above the box is shown next to every domain
  below it. A name after a comma or a tab on a line (`beta.org, Beta Bakery`) is
  used for that domain instead. A domain already in your workspace is left as it
  was. The name is set when the domain is added and cannot be changed afterwards,
  so to rename a client or give a domain to another, remove the domain and add it
  again.
- **The first ten domains are free.** A new workspace connects its first ten
  domains without a card, and the rest once the free trial has started. The box
  says so before you paste, with **Start free trial** beside it (nothing is
  charged for 14 days). It adds what it can now and keeps the rest in the box,
  and after the trial starts you land back on the same page with the rest still
  there. A teammate who is not an owner or an admin is told to ask one of them.
- **Adding is not connecting.** A list puts the domains in your workspace, each
  waiting for its DNS records. Finish each the way you would finish one, from its
  row on **Domains**, or send the step to the person who has the DNS (below).

On **Domains**, a workspace for clients says **client** where the others say
**customer**:

- **Client** narrows the list to one client's domains, or to those with no
  client.
- **Order** shows newest first (the default), oldest first, domain, client, or
  what needs attention first.
- **Export CSV** saves exactly the rows shown, in the order shown: domain,
  status, client, DNS host, when it was added and from where, the application,
  and what is left to do. It opens in a spreadsheet. A client name that starts
  with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` is saved as text, so a spreadsheet never runs it.

A domain's own page says "For Acme Dental" in a workspace for clients, where it
would say who connected it elsewhere.

### A link for the client

**Send these records to a teammate** also invites its recipient to the workspace
when an owner or an admin sends it, which is wrong for a client who has the DNS.
**Copy a link for the client** (in the ⋯ menu of a row on **Domains**, and beside
the email option on the connect step; it reads **Copy a setup link** in other
workspaces) makes a link for that one domain instead:

- Whoever opens it finishes that domain with no account, and nobody is invited.
  The page carries your workspace's name and nothing about its billing.
- It works once and lasts 30 days. It cannot be listed or cancelled afterwards,
  so make it when you are ready to send it. The console shows it on the page as
  well as copying it.
- It is for a domain that is not live yet, and it needs the member role or
  above. A workspace can make 30 an hour.

## Where each domain came from

Every row on **Domains** carries a small badge for where it was created, and
the tabs above the list filter by it (counts are for the selected
application):

| Tab | Badges | Created by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **From your product (widget)** | Widget | A customer, in the widget inside your product. |
| **Added in the console** | Console | You or a teammate, here. |
| **Via the API or agents** | API, Agent, MCP | Your server with an API key, an AI agent you authorized, or the MCP server. |

recorded (September 2026) have no badge and are listed under **All** only.
The API returns the same fact as `created_via` on every connection
(`widget`, `console`, `api`, `agent`, `mcp`, or empty when not recorded). The
**Overview** shows the split too, under the counts.

## Design the widget

**Widget → Design** is where the widget gets your look. The preview is the
widget itself, the one your customers get, running on example data: nothing
here is sent anywhere, and no domain or connection is needed.

Step through the journey with the row above the preview (enter the domain,
detect the provider, sign in, apply, connected), open the other screens under
**More screens** (existing records, manual setup, email records, an error and
more), and pick the steps of a checklist. Each screen says which status it
reports to your code. Look at the widget in light or dark, on a computer or a
phone, and in any language it speaks.

| Group | What you set |
| --- | --- |
| **Brand** | The name in the first heading ("Connect your domain to …"), the logo, a separate logo for dark mode (or a light plate behind a logo drawn for light pages, which the designer offers in one click when a logo would be hard to see on the dark surface), the logo's size, and whether "Powered by CustomDomain™" shows. |
| **Colors** | The brand color for buttons, links and focus rings. Text on it is chosen to be readable, and it is lifted in dark mode when it would vanish. An optional background and text color, used in both light and dark. |
| **Shape and type** | One corner radius (buttons, fields, cards and the pop-up follow it in proportion), a system font, the pop-up's width on a computer, and its shadow. |
| **Behavior** | Light, dark or match the device; the confetti; the close button; the progress steps. |
| **Screens and options** | "Set up manually instead" on the sign-in screen, "Let an AI assistant do it", "Email these instructions", and the offer to host DNS with CustomDomain™. |

**Each application has its own look.** The designer edits the application
chosen in the sidebar. **Save** gives that application a look of its own.
**Save as the workspace default** (under the ⋯ menu) sets the look every
application without its own uses, and **Use the workspace default for this
application** drops the application's own look. Saving needs an owner or admin.
The install code and **Try it live** use the saved look; the preview shows what
you have not saved yet.

For the logo, **Upload** (or drop a file on it). PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF (its
first frame), AVIF and SVG are accepted. Your browser prepares every upload the
same way, so logos look consistent: fully transparent edges are trimmed, the
image is fit inside 512 by 160 pixels (256 by 80 on screen at 2x), and it is
saved as a PNG under 200 KB. An SVG is converted to a PNG; the SVG itself is
never stored. Each logo is shown on the surface it will sit on, and you'll see a
warning when it would be hard to see there, or when it has a solid background
that would show as a box. The upload is hosted at once and appears in the
preview; it belongs to the application when you press **Save**.

Uploaded logos are served from `https://app.customdomain.ai/brand/logo/…`
at a URL named for the file's content, so it never changes and is cached
for a year. **Replace** uploads a new one; **Remove** clears it from the design.
Earlier uploads stay available at their URLs (your 20 most recent, and any an
application still uses), because a widget you already shipped keeps the logo
URL it was pasted with until you update its code. You can still paste the URL of
a logo you host yourself (HTTPS) instead.

## What the console is *not*

It is one application in your workspace. Everything it does is an API call
you could make yourself (the [API](/docs/getting-started/api) page maps each
step), and its **Widget** and **API & SDK** pages are where the other paths
begin.
