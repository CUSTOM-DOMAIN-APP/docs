    contents:
      - content: >-
          Removes the domain, and with `records` says what happens to the DNS
          records we added. `remove` deletes them and puts back what they
          replaced, when we know it; `keep` leaves them. What `remove` can do
          depends on how the domain was connected, and `GET
          /connections/{id}/removal` says so before you call this: with access
          we kept (our hosted zone, a kept sign-in, a continuous provider
          account, a kept Domain Connect authorization) it is automatic; with a
          one time sign-in or key it needs one more authorization, and this
          answers `409 reauthorization_required` with the plan (sign in again
          with `oauth:start` and `purpose: "remove"`, send a key to `POST
          /connections/{id}:disconnect`, or call this with `records=keep` and
          follow the manual steps); for Domain Connect one-click and manual
          setups the answer lists the exact manual steps. Records whose value
          changed since we wrote them, values that were already there before we
          wrote them, and records another connected domain still uses are never
          touched. With a key or a sign-in, the zone must show at least one of
          our records before any record is reported removed or gone; otherwise
          the answer lists the manual steps (the key may belong to another
          account).


          Whatever the choice, the domain stops being served the moment the
          removal starts (edges forget it within a minute), its certificate
          stops renewing and is deleted, its monitoring stops, and
          `connection.disconnected` carries a `removal` object with what
          happened to the records. `GET /connections/{id}/removal` answers the
          same outcome once the connection is gone. A second removal of the same
          domain while one runs answers `409 removal_in_progress`.


          Without `records` the call keeps its original contract: records are
          taken out only where it always took them out (a kept Domain Connect
          authorization, a kept sign-in, our hosted zone) and kept otherwise,
          including with a connected provider account or the Route 53 access
          role; the answer is `204` with no body. With `records` the answer is
          `200` with the outcome.


          An end user's widget token (scope `connect`) must be bound to the
          domain and, when the connection names an end user, minted with that
          `end_user_ref`; otherwise `403`.
